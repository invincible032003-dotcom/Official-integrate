@topic Education Indicators: Access & Participation
Q: The Apparent (Gross) Intake Rate is used as a substitute for the Net Intake Rate when:
A) data on new entrants by single years of age are not available
B) enrolment data are not available at all
C) population projections are not available
D) the school-entrance age is not defined
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 43
EXP: **AIR = (Grade 1 enrolment of all ages ÷ population of the official entrance age) × 100**. It is used instead of the **NIR** when **single-year age data on new entrants** are missing. The NIR counts only entrants **of the official entrance age**.
SC: Apparent/Gross means all ages in the numerator. Net means the right-age entrants only.

Q: The Net Intake Rate differs from the Apparent Intake Rate in that its numerator includes:
A) only Grade 1 entrants of the official school-entrance age
B) all Grade 1 entrants irrespective of age
C) repeaters in Grade 1
D) pre-school enrolment
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 44
EXP: **NIR = (Grade 1 entrants of official entrance age ÷ population of that age) × 100**. It measures access more precisely than the AIR, which can go above 100% because of over-age and under-age entrants.
SC: "Net" removes the wrong-age children. The numerator and denominator refer to the same age.

Q: Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) at the primary level can exceed 100 per cent because:
A) its numerator includes over-aged and under-aged pupils while the denominator is the official age-group population
B) its denominator includes adults
C) it double-counts girls
D) it excludes private schools
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 44
EXP: **GER = total enrolment (all ages) ÷ population of the official age group × 100**. Over-age and under-age enrolment in the numerator can push it **above 100%**. **NER** counts only official-age children, so NER ≤ 100% and GER ≥ NER.
SC: GER − NER gives the extent of over- and under-age enrolment.

Q: Used together, GER and NER help measure:
A) the extent of over-aged and under-aged enrolment
B) teacher-pupil ratio
C) the dropout rate
D) transition to secondary level
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 44
EXP: The source notes that GER can be used **together with NER to measure the extent of over-aged and under-aged enrolment**. The gap between them is enrolment outside the official age band.
SC: The GER−NER gap measures age mismatch.

Q: Gross Enrolment Ratio in Early Childhood Development programmes indicates:
A) the capacity of the education system to prepare young children for elementary education
B) the proportion of Grade 6 graduates
C) adult literacy
D) teacher availability
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 43
EXP: **ECD GER** measures young children's participation in pre-school or ECD programmes. It shows how well the system **prepares children for elementary education**.
SC: ECD means pre-school readiness.

@topic Education Indicators: Internal Efficiency
Q: The Cohort Survival Rate, computed by the reconstructed cohort method, is used primarily to assess:
A) internal efficiency and "wastage" in education
B) population growth of school-age children
C) the literacy rate
D) gender parity
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 44
EXP: The **Cohort Survival Rate** is the share of a cohort that reaches a given grade. It measures **internal efficiency** and **wastage** from dropout and repetition. It is built by the **reconstructed cohort method** from promotion, repetition and dropout rates, and it is sensitive to migration.
SC: Survival tracks how many of 1,000 entrants reach the final grade, which is a measure of wastage.

Q: In the reconstructed cohort method, the flow rates needed for each grade are:
A) promotion, repetition and dropout (school leaver) rates
B) enrolment, population and literacy rates
C) birth, death and migration rates
D) pass, fail and absentee rates
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 45
EXP: The method follows a notional cohort (for example 1,000 pupils) using **promotion** and **repetition** rates for each grade. **Dropout** is the remainder (1 − promotion − repetition). Summing gives pupil-years and total promotees.
SC: Every pupil in a grade is promoted, repeats, or drops out, and these three shares add to 100%.

Q: The School Leaver Rate is given by:
A) 1 − Promotion Rate − Repetition Rate
B) Promotion Rate − Repetition Rate
C) 1 − Survival Rate
D) Dropouts ÷ Graduates
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 47
EXP: **School Leaver Rate = 1 − promotion rate − repetition rate**. It is the EFA measure of dropout. It counts pupils who leave during the grade **and** those who finish but do not enrol the next year. It is more complete than the **Simple Dropout Rate** but unreliable where migration is heavy.
SC: Whatever is not promoted and not repeating has left.

Q: How does the Simple Dropout Rate differ from the School Leaver Rate?
A) It does not capture pupils who finish a grade but fail to enrol in the next grade the following year
B) It includes repeaters
C) It is computed only for secondary level
D) It is always larger than the school leaver rate
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 47
EXP: The **Simple Dropout Rate** counts only pupils who **do not finish** a grade. It misses those who finish but do **not enrol** the next year. So the School Leaver Rate is theoretically **more comprehensive**.
SC: Simple dropout counts mid-year leavers only. School leaver counts mid-year leavers plus those who do not return.

Q: The Coefficient of Efficiency is computed as:
A) (Total promotees/graduates including repeaters × number of years of the cycle) ÷ pupil-years
B) Pupil-years ÷ graduates
C) Enrolment ÷ population
D) Repeaters ÷ enrolment
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 45
EXP: **Coefficient of Efficiency = (graduates × ideal years of the cycle) ÷ actual pupil-years spent**, for example ×6 for elementary and ×4 for secondary. A value of 1 (100%) means no wastage. Its reciprocal relates to **years input per graduate**.
SC: CE = ideal years ÷ actual years. Wastage pushes it below 1.

Q: "Years input per graduate" is calculated as:
A) Pupil-years ÷ total promotees/graduates (including repeaters)
B) Graduates ÷ pupil-years
C) Enrolment ÷ graduates
D) Grade 1 entrants ÷ population
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 46
EXP: **Years input per graduate = pupil-years ÷ graduates**. It is the average number of years spent to produce one graduate. In an efficient system it equals the cycle length (6 for elementary).
SC: Input years per output graduate, so pupil-years go on top.

Q: If the reconstructed cohort gives total pupil-years of 5,000 for a 6-year elementary cycle and 700 graduates, the years input per graduate is approximately:
A) 7.14
B) 6.00
C) 0.84
D) 8.57
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 46
EXP: Years input per graduate = **5,000 ÷ 700 ≈ 7.14 years**, compared with the ideal 6 years. The coefficient of efficiency = (700 × 6) ÷ 5,000 = **0.84**, which is a distractor here.
SC: YIPG = pupil-years ÷ graduates. CE = ideal ÷ YIPG = 6 ÷ 7.14 ≈ 0.84.

Q: The Repetition Rate for a grade is:
A) Repeaters in the grade this year ÷ enrolment in that grade the previous year × 100
B) Repeaters ÷ graduates × 100
C) Promotees ÷ repeaters × 100
D) Dropouts ÷ population × 100
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 46
EXP: **Repetition Rate (grade x, year n) = repeaters in grade x in year n ÷ enrolment in grade x in year n−1 × 100**. It is an EFA indicator of how many pupils repeat a grade.
SC: The base is always last year's enrolment in the same grade.

Q: The Transition Rate from elementary to secondary level assesses:
A) the extent to which pupils move to the next higher level of education
B) teacher transfers between schools
C) change in the pupil-teacher ratio
D) movement of pupils between private and public schools
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 47
EXP: **Transition Rate = new entrants to the first grade of the higher level ÷ graduates of the lower level in the previous year × 100**. It is sensitive to migration, so it is not computed at school level.
SC: Transition means crossing a level, for example from primary to secondary.

Q: The Completion Rate measures:
A) the percentage of grade/year 1 entrants who graduate from the level
B) the percentage of teachers completing training
C) the literacy rate of adults
D) the proportion of schools completing construction
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 48
EXP: **Completion Rate = graduates of the final grade ÷ grade 1 entrants of the matching cohort × 100**. It shows what share of entrants actually finish the cycle.
SC: Completion = finishers ÷ starters.

Q: The Failure Rate for a grade is based on:
A) enrolment minus promotees
B) repeaters minus dropouts
C) graduates minus entrants
D) population minus enrolment
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 48
EXP: The source defines **Failures = Enrolment − Promotees**, so the Failure Rate = failures ÷ enrolment × 100.
SC: Anyone enrolled but not promoted has failed that grade.

Q: The Retention Rate determines:
A) the proportion of pupils in one school year who continue in school in the succeeding year
B) the proportion of teachers retained
C) the percentage of graduates getting jobs
D) the share of girls in enrolment
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 49
EXP: **Retention Rate = enrolment in grades 2–6 this year ÷ enrolment in grades 1–5 last year × 100** (elementary). It shows how many stay on in school. It is affected by migration, so it is not advised at school level.
SC: Retention is this year's continuing pupils compared with last year's base.

Q: Which of the following education indicators is described as vulnerable to migration and hence NOT advisable at the school level?
1. Cohort Survival Rate
2. School Leaver Rate
3. Retention Rate
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Hard
P: 49
EXP: All three are flagged as **vulnerable to migration**. Pupils moving in or out of an area distort cohort-based ratios at small levels (school, municipality). The Transition Rate carries the same caution.
SC: Any indicator that follows the same children across years is affected by migration.

Q: In PLFS, the reference period for the usual status (ps+ss) approach and for current weekly status (CWS) are respectively:

@topic PLFS 2019-20: Key Results
Q: As per PLFS 2019-20, the Labour Force Participation Rate (usual status ps+ss) for persons of all ages in India was:
A) 40.1%
B) 53.5%
C) 38.2%
D) 50.9%
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 52
EXP: LFPR (all ages, ps+ss) = **40.1%**. For **age 15+** it was **53.5%**. The WPR was 38.2% (all ages) and 50.9% (15+).
SC: 40.1 LFPR and 38.2 WPR (all ages). The gap is about 1.9 points, i.e. the unemployed.

Q: In PLFS 2019-20, the Unemployment Rate (usual status ps+ss) for persons of all ages was:
A) 4.8%
B) 6.1%
C) 10.1%
D) 15.0%
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 60
EXP: UR (ps+ss, all ages) = **4.8%**. For **educated persons aged 15+** (secondary and above) it was **10.1%**, and for **youth aged 15–29** it was **15.0%**. The 6.1% figure is from PLFS 2017-18.
SC: 4.8 overall, 10.1 educated, 15.0 youth. The rate rises as the group narrows.

Q: In PLFS, the earnings estimates for urban areas are based on:

Q: Worker Population Ratio (WPR) is defined as:
A) number of employed persons per 100 persons in the population
B) number of employed persons per 100 persons in the labour force
C) number of unemployed persons per 100 persons in the labour force
D) number of persons in the labour force per 100 persons in the population
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 53
EXP: **WPR = employed ÷ total population × 100**. **LFPR** = labour force ÷ population × 100. **UR** = unemployed ÷ labour force × 100. So WPR = LFPR × (1 − UR/100).
SC: WPR and LFPR both have the population as the base. UR has the labour force as the base.

Q: Using PLFS 2019-20 figures (all ages, ps+ss), if LFPR = 40.1% and WPR = 38.2%, the implied unemployment rate is approximately:
A) 4.7%
B) 1.9%
C) 9.5%
D) 2.4%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 60
EXP: UR = (LFPR − WPR) ÷ LFPR × 100 = (40.1 − 38.2) ÷ 40.1 × 100 ≈ **4.7%**. This matches the published **4.8%** after rounding. The value 1.9% is the unemployed share of the whole population, not of the labour force.
SC: UR = 1 − WPR/LFPR. Divide by the labour force, not the population.
