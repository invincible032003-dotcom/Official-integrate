@topic PLFS: Objectives & Reports
Q: The National Statistical Office launched the Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) in:
A) April 2017
B) July 2017
C) January 2018
D) April 2019
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 64
EXP: The PLFS was **launched in April 2017** so that labour force data would be available at shorter intervals. The first annual report covered **July 2017–June 2018**.
SC: Launched April 2017. The first annual cycle started July 2017.

Q: Which of the following is/are primary objective(s) of PLFS?
1. To estimate key employment indicators quarterly for urban areas in Current Weekly Status
2. To estimate employment indicators annually in both usual status (ps+ss) and CWS for rural and urban areas
3. To estimate quarterly indicators for rural areas in usual status
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 only
B) 1 and 2 only
C) 2 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: B
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 64
EXP: The two objectives are (1) **quarterly CWS estimates for urban areas only** and (2) **annual estimates in usual status and CWS for rural and urban areas**. Statement 3 is wrong because there were no quarterly rural or usual-status estimates.
SC: Quarterly means urban and CWS. Annual means everything.

Q: The first Annual Report of PLFS (July 2017–June 2018) was released in:
A) May 2019
B) June 2020
C) January 2019
D) July 2018
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 64
EXP: The first Annual Report was released in **May 2019**, the second (2018-19) in **June 2020**, and the third (2019-20) later.
SC: Report 1 in May 2019, report 2 in June 2020, roughly a year apart.

Q: During the COVID-19 pandemic, PLFS fieldwork was suspended from:
A) 18 March 2020, and resumed on 1 June 2020
B) 25 March 2020, and resumed on 1 July 2020
C) 1 April 2020, and resumed on 15 June 2020
D) 18 March 2020, and resumed on 1 August 2020
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 64
EXP: Fieldwork was **suspended on 18.03.2020** and **resumed on 1 June 2020**. Information was still collected for the **original reference periods**, so there was no break in the quarterly flow. Revisit schedules were done **by telephone**.
SC: Suspended 18 March, resumed 1 June. Revisits were done by phone.

Q: After fieldwork resumed in June 2020, the revisit schedules in PLFS were canvassed:
A) telephonically, for the actual reference periods of samples
B) through online self-enumeration
C) by postal questionnaires
D) through village panchayats
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 64
EXP: To reduce physical contact, **revisit schedules were canvassed by telephone** using the **actual reference periods** in the original paper revisit schedules.
SC: Revisits were done by phone, still for the original reference periods.

@topic PLFS: Sample Design
Q: In urban areas, PLFS uses a rotational panel design in which each selected household is visited:
A) four times: one first visit and three revisits
B) twice: one first visit and one revisit
C) once only
D) twelve times: every month
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 65
EXP: In the urban **rotational panel**, each household gets a **first visit** and then **three quarterly revisits**, four visits in all. This supports quarterly CWS estimates. **Rural** households have **no revisit**.
SC: Urban: 1 + 3 = 4 visits. Rural: 1 visit.

Q: In rural areas, what share of the annual FSU allocation was covered in each quarter of PLFS?
A) 25%
B) 50%
C) 33%
D) 100%
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 65
EXP: Rural samples were spread **equally across four quarters**, so **25% of FSUs** were covered each quarter.
SC: Four quarters means a quarter of the sample each time.

Q: The First Stage Units (FSUs) of PLFS in rural and urban areas respectively are:
A) Census 2011 villages (Panchayat wards for Kerala) and UFS blocks
B) UFS blocks and Census 2011 villages
C) Households and villages
D) Districts and towns
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 65
EXP: **Rural FSU = 2011 Census village** (a **Panchayat ward in Kerala**). **Urban FSU = UFS block**. The ultimate stage units are **households**. Large FSUs have an intermediate stage (hamlet group or sub-block).
SC: Villages come from the Census frame and urban blocks come from the UFS frame.

Q: In PLFS, the special stratum formed in the rural sector of which State consisted of all villages that were difficult to access?
A) Nagaland
B) Arunachal Pradesh
C) Mizoram
D) Jammu & Kashmir
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 65
EXP: In the **rural sector of Nagaland**, a **special stratum** was made of all difficult-to-access villages. This is standard NSS practice.
SC: Nagaland is the State with the special "difficult villages" stratum.

Q: In urban areas of PLFS, strata within each NSS region were formed on the basis of:
A) size class of towns as per Census 2011
B) number of households in each ward
C) literacy rate of towns
D) district boundaries
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 65
EXP: **Urban strata** were formed by the **size class of towns** (Census 2011) within each NSS region, with no sub-stratification. In **rural areas**, each NSS region was one stratum, split into **r/8 sub-strata** of roughly equal population.
SC: Urban strata use town size. Rural sub-strata use equal-population groups (r/8).

Q: At the all-India level, how many FSUs were allotted annually for PLFS 2019-20?
A) 12,800 (7,024 villages and 5,776 UFS blocks)
B) 12,569 (6,913 villages and 5,656 blocks)
C) 10,000 (5,000 each)
D) 14,000 (8,000 villages and 6,000 blocks)
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 65
EXP: **12,800 FSUs were allotted** (7,024 villages and 5,776 UFS blocks). **12,569 were actually surveyed** (6,913 villages and 5,656 blocks).
SC: Allotted 12,800, surveyed 12,569. Don't mix up allotted and surveyed.

Q: In PLFS, the State-level sample was allocated between rural and urban sectors in proportion to population with:
A) double weightage to the urban sector
B) double weightage to the rural sector
C) equal allocation irrespective of population
D) triple weightage to metro cities
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 65
EXP: Allocation was **proportional to Census 2011 population**, but the **urban sector got double weight**. Urban needs quarterly estimates and has more varied labour markets.
SC: Urban gets double weight because of its quarterly panel.

Q: The sample allocation at the stratum level in PLFS was adjusted to multiples of:
A) 8, with a minimum of 8
B) 4, with a minimum of 4
C) 2, with a minimum of 2
D) 10, with a minimum of 10
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 65
EXP: Allocations were in **multiples of 8 with a minimum of 8**. In urban areas this gives 4 panels × 2 sub-samples. In rural areas it gives 4 quarters × 2 sub-samples.
SC: 8 = 4 (panels or quarters) × 2 (sub-samples).

Q: Urban FSUs in PLFS were selected by:
A) PPSWR, size being the number of households in the UFS block
B) SRSWOR
C) PPSWR, size being the area of the block
D) systematic sampling with equal probability
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 65
EXP: **Urban**: PPSWR with **size = number of households** in the UFS block. **Rural**: PPSWR with **size = village population**. Both used two independent sub-samples.
SC: Urban size = households. Rural size = population.

Q: How many households were selected from each sample FSU for the detailed PLFS schedule, and by what method within second-stage strata?
A) 8 households, by SRSWOR
B) 16 households, by PPSWR
C) 10 households, by systematic sampling
D) 12 households, by SRSWR
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 66
EXP: **8 households per FSU** were selected by **SRSWOR** from the second-stage strata: **3 SSS in rural** and **4 SSS in urban** areas.
SC: 8 households, chosen without replacement (SRSWOR).

Q: In PLFS, the number of second-stage strata (SSS) formed in rural and urban FSUs respectively was:
A) 3 and 4
B) 4 and 3
C) 2 and 2
D) 3 and 3
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 66
EXP: Listed households were stratified into **3 SSS in villages** and **4 SSS in UFS blocks**. The SSS are formed on the basis of the educational attainment of household members, which improves precision for labour indicators.
SC: Rural 3, urban 4. Urban has one more.

Q: The PLFS schedule canvassed for the first visit and revisit is numbered:
A) Schedule 10.4
B) Schedule 10.0
C) Schedule 1.0
D) Schedule 25.0
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 67
EXP: PLFS used **Schedule 10.4** for both the first visit and revisits. Schedule 10 is traditionally the NSS employment-unemployment schedule. Schedule 1.0 is consumer expenditure.
SC: NSS schedule 10.x is employment, and PLFS uses 10.4.

Q: In PLFS reports, the reference period of the estimates for a particular survey period is taken as:
A) the mid-point of the corresponding survey period
B) the first day of the survey period
C) the last day of the survey period
D) the date of release of the report
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 67
EXP: The **reference point of an estimate is the mid-point** of the survey period, whether a quarter or the full year.
SC: A time-averaged estimate refers to the middle of the period.

Q: In PLFS estimates, the transgender category was:
A) included in males
B) included in females
C) shown separately
D) excluded
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 67
EXP: The report states that, in presenting estimates, **transgender persons were included in males**. The sample was too small for separate estimates.
SC: This is a reporting convention to remember: transgender persons were counted with males.

@topic PLFS: Household Concepts
Q: For defining a household in PLFS, a temporary visitor is excluded if the total period of stay in the household is expected to be less than:
A) 6 months
B) 3 months
C) 1 year
D) 1 month
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 70
EXP: "Normally lived together" excludes **visitors and guests staying less than 6 months** and includes **temporary stay-aways absent for less than 6 months**.
SC: The 6-month rule applies both ways: short visitors are out and short absentees are in.

Q: Consider the following cases in PLFS household definition:
1. A child residing in a hostel for studies is excluded from the parents' household.
2. A resident domestic servant is included in the employer's household.
3. A tenant is treated as part of the landlord's household.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: A
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 70
EXP: Statements 1 and 2 are correct. A **hostel child** is excluded from the parents' household. A **resident domestic servant or paying guest** is included in the host's household. A **tenant is not** included.
SC: A paying guest is included but a tenant is not. This is a classic NSS distinction.

Q: When the criteria of "living together" and "sharing food from a common kitchen" conflict, PLFS gives more importance to:
A) living together
B) common kitchen
C) blood relationship
D) pooling of income
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 70
EXP: **"Living together" takes priority** over the common kitchen. The exception is a person who eats with the family but sleeps elsewhere for lack of space, who is still counted in the family's household.
SC: Living together beats the common kitchen.

Q: In PLFS, each inmate of a hotel, mess or hostel is treated as a single-member household unless:
A) a group of them pool their income for spending
B) they are from the same State
C) they stay longer than 6 months
D) they are students
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 70
EXP: Hostel or hotel inmates are **single-member households**. If a group **pools income for spending**, they form **one household**. A family living in a hotel is one household.
SC: Pooled income makes them one household.

Q: In rural areas, PLFS classifies households into how many household types based on the major source of income?
A) Six
B) Four
C) Five
D) Three
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 70
EXP: **Rural: 6 types**: self-employed in agriculture, self-employed in non-agriculture, regular wage/salary, casual labour in agriculture, casual labour in non-agriculture, and others. **Urban: 4 types**: self-employed, regular wage/salary, casual labour, and others.
SC: Rural splits agriculture and non-agriculture, giving 6. Urban does not, giving 4.

Q: For determining household type in PLFS, the household's income from economic activities is considered for the reference period of:
A) 365 days preceding the survey
B) 30 days preceding the survey
C) 7 days preceding the survey
D) the last agricultural season
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 70
EXP: Household type is based on the household's **net** income from economic activities over the **365 days** before the survey. Income of servants and paying guests is excluded.
SC: Household type uses a 365-day window and net income.

Q: In PLFS, the household's usual monthly consumer expenditure is computed as A + B + C, where B is:
A) purchase value of household durables bought during the last one year, divided by 12
B) value of home-grown consumption
C) value of wages in kind
D) expenditure on education and health only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 71
EXP: **A** = usual monthly household expenditure. **B** = durables bought in the last year ÷ 12. **C** = imputed monthly value of consumption from wages in kind, home-grown stock and free collection. **UMCE = A + B + C**.
SC: B stands for big-ticket durables, spread over 12 months.

@topic PLFS: Education & Training Concepts
Q: For PLFS, a person is considered literate if he/she:
A) can both read and write a simple message with understanding in at least one language
B) has completed primary school
C) can sign his/her name
D) has attended any school for at least one year
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 71
EXP: Literacy means the ability to **read and write a simple message with understanding** in **any one language**. Signing one's name or schooling alone is not enough.
SC: Read + write + understanding. All three are needed.

Q: While counting "number of years in formal education" in PLFS, which of the following is correct?
A) Repetition years are also counted
B) Pre-primary (kindergarten) years are counted
C) Time spent between discontinuation and restart is counted
D) In-service job trainings are counted
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 72
EXP: **Repetition years count**. Pre-primary years are **excluded** (counting starts at class I). Gaps are **not** counted. In-service or job-specific trainings are **excluded** unless they are part of formal programmes.
SC: Count from class I, include repeats, exclude gaps and job trainings.

Q: For a course like Chartered Accountancy, where the duration is not predetermined, PLFS counts the number of years in education for the completed course as:
A) 2 years
B) 3 years
C) 5 years
D) 0 years
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 72
EXP: For completed courses of **unknown duration** (CA, CS, doctorate), PLFS **adds 2 years** to the years already counted. A CA after graduation = years to graduation + 2.
SC: Unknown duration counts as 2 years.

Q: PLFS records the highest level of technical education successfully completed in how many categories?
A) 16
B) 12
C) 10
D) 8
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 73
EXP: **16 categories**: no technical education, plus 5 subjects (agriculture, engineering/technology, medicine, crafts, other) × 3 levels (degree; diploma below graduate; diploma graduate and above) = 1 + 15.
SC: 1 + (5 subjects × 3 levels) = 16.

Q: Training that is structured but more flexible than formal learning, provided in community settings or workplaces without the accreditation of formal learning, is termed:
A) non-formal training
B) formal training
C) informal training
D) hereditary training
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 73
EXP: **Formal**: institutional and certified. **Non-formal**: structured but flexible, with no accreditation. **Informal**: unstructured, happens in daily life, and does not lead to certification.
SC: Formal is certified, non-formal is structured, informal is unstructured.

Q: A person who learnt photography entirely through his own effort, without training under any person or organisation, is considered in PLFS to have acquired training through:
A) self-learning
B) learning on the job
C) hereditary source
D) formal training
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 74
EXP: The report uses this exact example: learning photography through one's own effort is **self-learning**, a non-formal source.
SC: Own effort with no teacher is self-learning.

Q: A person who learns tailoring from a master tailor (not an ancestral trade) is classified as having received vocational training from:
A) other sources
B) hereditary sources
C) formal training
D) self-learning
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 74
EXP: Skills learnt from another person, or from household members in a trade **different from the ancestral one**, fall under "**other**" sources. Hereditary means the ancestors' own trade.
SC: Hereditary requires the ancestral trade. A new trade learnt from someone else is "other".

Q: If an employer provides formal training in a trade to a person while in employment, PLFS treats it as:
A) formal vocational/technical training
B) learning on the job
C) informal training
D) self-learning
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 74
EXP: **Learning on the job** covers informal exposure at work. If the employer gives **formal** training, it counts as **formal** vocational training.
SC: The type of training decides the category, not who gives it.

@topic PLFS: Economic Activity & Activity Status
Q: Which of the following activities was NOT considered an economic activity for PLFS, although the UN SNA treats it as production?
A) Processing of primary products for own consumption
B) Production of primary goods for own consumption
C) Own-account construction of a house
D) Free collection of firewood
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 74
EXP: PLFS counts market activities, **primary goods production for own consumption** (NIC divisions 01–09, including free collection) and **own-account production of fixed assets**. It does **not** count **processing of primary products for own consumption**, even though SNA does.
SC: Primary production for own use counts. Processing it at home does not.

Q: Which of the following, though they may result in earnings, were NOT treated as economic activities in PLFS?
A) Prostitution and begging
B) Street vending
C) Paid domestic work
D) Tutoring for fees
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 75
EXP: The report says **prostitution and begging** are **not economic activities** for the survey. Such persons get code **97** (others) and are not in the labour force.
SC: Begging and prostitution get code 97, not in labour force.

Q: For assigning a unique activity status, the major time criterion is used for ___ and the priority criterion for ___.
A) usual status; current weekly and daily status
B) current weekly status; usual status
C) current daily status; usual status
D) usual status; subsidiary status only
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 75
EXP: **Usual status** (365 days) uses the **major time criterion**. **CWS and CDS** use the **priority criterion**: working takes priority over seeking work, which takes priority over being outside the labour force.
SC: Long period means major time. Short period means priority (work first).

Q: In PLFS activity codes, code 21 denotes:
A) helper in household enterprise
B) own-account worker
C) employer
D) regular wage/salaried employee
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 76
EXP: **11** = own-account worker, **12** = employer, **21** = unpaid helper in a household enterprise, **31** = regular wage/salaried, **41/42/51** = casual labour.
SC: The first digit gives the class: 1x and 2x self-employed, 3x regular, 4x-5x casual.

Q: Which of the following activity status codes is applicable ONLY under the current status approach and not under usual status?
A) 42 – casual labour in MGNREG public works
B) 31 – regular wage/salaried
C) 81 – sought/available for work
D) 91 – attended educational institution
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 77
EXP: Codes **42, 61, 62, 71, 72, 82 and 98** apply **only to current status**. In usual status, code **41** covers casual labour in **all** public works.
SC: The current-only codes are 42, 61, 62, 71, 72, 82 and 98, i.e. temporary absences and MGNREGA.

Q: Persons with activity status codes 11 to 82 constitute:
A) the labour force
B) the workforce
C) persons not in the labour force
D) the unemployed
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 77
EXP: **Workers** have codes **11–72**, the **unemployed** 81–82, and so the **labour force** is **11–82**. Codes 91–99 mean not in the labour force.
SC: Workforce 11-72, add 81-82 for the labour force, 9x means outside.

Q: In PLFS, activity status code 93 refers to persons who:
A) attended to domestic duties and were also engaged in free collection of goods, sewing, tailoring, etc. for household use
B) attended to domestic duties only
C) were rentiers or pensioners
D) were children aged 0–4 years
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 76
EXP: **92** = domestic duties only. **93** = domestic duties **plus** free collection (vegetables, firewood, cattle feed) or sewing and weaving for household use. **94** = rentiers and pensioners. **99** = children aged 0–4.
SC: 92 is domestic only. 93 adds collection and home production.

Q: Which of the following is NOT treated as "public works" for classifying casual labour in PLFS?
A) Employment under Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojana (PMRY)
B) Works under MGNREGA
C) Works under Sampoorna Grameen Rozgar Yojana
D) Works under National Food for Work Programme
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 79
EXP: **Public works** are government-sponsored **wage-employment and relief** schemes such as **MGNREG, SGRY and NFFWP**. **Self-employment** schemes such as **SGSY, REGP, PMRY and VAMBAY** are **not** public works.
SC: Wage-employment schemes are public works. Self-employment schemes are not.

Q: Under MGNREGA, 2005, the guaranteed wage employment per household per financial year is at least:
A) 100 days
B) 150 days
C) 50 days
D) 365 days
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 79
EXP: MGNREGA guarantees **at least 100 days** of wage employment per **household** each **financial year** to adult members (18+) who volunteer for unskilled manual work.
SC: 100 days per household, not per person.

Q: In PLFS, a person's subsidiary economic activity status is recorded if he/she pursued some economic activity for:
A) 30 days or more during the 365 days preceding the survey
B) 15 days or more
C) 60 days or more
D) at least one hour on any day
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 80
EXP: Apart from the **usual principal status** (by major time), a person with economic activity for **30 days or more** in the reference year has a **subsidiary status (ss)**. The ps+ss measure combines both.
SC: Subsidiary means 30+ days within the 365.

Q: The remuneration of self-employed persons, as described in PLFS concepts, consists of:
A) a non-separable combination of reward for labour and profit of the enterprise
B) only wages
C) only profits
D) fixed salary plus bonus
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 78
EXP: For the self-employed, **labour reward and enterprise profit cannot be separated**. They also have **autonomy** and **economic independence** in running their operation.
SC: Self-employed income = labour return + profit, and the two cannot be separated.
