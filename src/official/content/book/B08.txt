@topic Current Weekly & Current Daily Status
Q: Under Current Weekly Status (CWS), a person is considered working if he/she:
A) worked for at least one hour on at least one day during the 7 days preceding the survey
B) worked for at least 4 hours on each day of the week
C) worked for at least 30 days in the last year
D) worked for the major part of the week
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 81
EXP: CWS uses the **priority criterion**. Working for **at least 1 hour on at least 1 day** of the reference week (or having work but not doing it) makes a person **employed**, even if they were unemployed or outside the labour force the rest of the week.
SC: One hour on one day is enough for CWS. Work takes priority.

Q: The priority order in the priority criterion used for current status is:
A) working > seeking/available for work > neither working nor available
B) seeking work > working > not in labour force
C) not in labour force > working > seeking work
D) working > not in labour force > seeking work
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 81
EXP: **Working** has the highest priority, then **seeking or available for work**, then **neither** (not in the labour force). Within the chosen broad status, the detailed status is fixed by **major time**.
SC: Employed > Unemployed > Out of labour force, in that order.

Q: In Current Daily Status (CDS), at most how many economic activities are recorded for a person on a single day?
A) Two
B) One
C) Three
D) Unlimited
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 81
EXP: CDS records **at most two work activities per day**, distinguished by **status × 2-digit NIC-2008**. A day's work is split into half-day units, which is why CDS measures person-days.
SC: At most two activities a day, i.e. two half-days.

Q: For current daily status, economic activities on a day are differentiated at the level of:
A) status × 2-digit NIC-2008
B) status × 5-digit NIC-2008
C) 3-digit NCO-2004 only
D) sector only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 81
EXP: The report states that economic activities are distinguished at **status × 2-digit NIC-2008**. Industry of the main work is coded at **5-digit NIC** and occupation at **3-digit NCO-2004**.
SC: CDS separates activities at 2-digit NIC. Coding of industry is at 5-digit.

Q: In PLFS, the industry of work and occupation are recorded respectively using:
A) 5-digit NIC-2008 and 3-digit NCO-2004
B) 2-digit NIC-2008 and 4-digit NCO-2015
C) 4-digit NIC-2004 and 2-digit NCO-1968
D) 3-digit NIC-2008 and 1-digit NCO-2004
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 82
EXP: The PLFS 2019-20 report used **NIC-2008 at 5 digits** for industry and **NCO-2004 at 3 digits** for occupation.
SC: NIC 5-digit and NCO 3-digit.

Q: Under NIC-2008 Division 97 (households as employers of domestic personnel), PLFS used additional codes. Code 97006 denotes:
A) Tutor
B) Driver
C) Cook
D) Gardener
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 82
EXP: The codes are 97001 housemaid/servant, 97002 cook, 97003 gardener, 97004 gatekeeper/watchman, 97005 governess/baby-sitter, **97006 tutor**, 97007 driver, 97009 others.
SC: The order runs house → kitchen → garden → gate → child → tutor → driver (1 to 7).

@topic Hours Actually Worked
Q: The concept of "hours actually worked" in PLFS follows the definition given by:
A) the 18th ICLS
B) the 13th ICLS
C) the 15th ICLS
D) the 19th ICLS
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 82
EXP: Hours actually worked follows the **18th ICLS** (2008) definition, within the NSSO production boundary. The 13th ICLS covers the economically active population, the 15th the informal sector, and the 19th work statistics.
SC: 18th ICLS = working time, 15th = informal sector, 17th = informal employment.

Q: Which of the following is INCLUDED in hours actually worked as per PLFS?
A) Time spent waiting for customers or business at the place of work
B) Time spent on travel from home to work
C) Paid meal breaks without productive activity
D) Paid annual leave
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 82
EXP: **Waiting for customers or patients and on-call duty** counts as time related to production, and is included. Commuting, longer meal breaks (even if paid) and any leave or holidays are **excluded**.
SC: Waiting at work counts. Commuting and meal breaks do not.

Q: Consider the following time components:
1. Short tea/coffee breaks at the workplace
2. "In-between time" due to machinery breakdown while remaining available for work
3. Time spent in training to upgrade skills for the job
4. Time spent in educational activity not intended for the economic unit, though paid by the employer
Which of the above are included in hours actually worked?
A) 1, 2 and 3 only
B) 1 and 2 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: A
T: Statement
D: Hard
P: 83
EXP: Short rest breaks, **unavoidable in-between time** and **job-related training** are included. **Education not intended for the economic unit** is excluded even if the employer pays for it.
SC: If it serves the job it is included. Personal education is excluded even when paid.

Q: For self-employed persons with atypical work (farmers, shop owners), the basic criterion to determine hours actually worked is whether they were:
A) available to perform the work (ready to receive orders or attend clients)
B) physically producing output every hour
C) registered with a labour office
D) paid for each hour
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 83
EXP: For such self-employed persons, the **availability criterion** is used: whether they were **ready to receive orders, attend to clients or perform** the activity. Periods of **absence** (illness, leave, strikes, bad weather) are excluded.
SC: For the self-employed, being available (for example the shop being open) counts as working.

@topic Earnings Concepts
Q: Earnings of regular salaried/wage employees in PLFS refer to:
A) gross remuneration before deductions for taxes and employees' social security contributions
B) take-home pay after all deductions
C) earnings including employers' contribution to provident fund
D) earnings including severance pay
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 84
EXP: Earnings are **gross**, i.e. before deductions for tax, the employee's PF, insurance and so on. They **exclude employer contributions** to social security and pension schemes and **severance or termination pay**. Wages in kind are valued at **current retail price**, and **bonus** is apportioned to the reference period.
SC: Gross of the employee's deductions, net of the employer's contributions, and no severance pay.

Q: Gross earnings of self-employed persons in PLFS are obtained as:
A) Gross output − total expenses, with output valued at basic prices
B) Sales revenue only
C) Gross output at market prices including product taxes
D) Net profit after income tax
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 85
EXP: **Gross earnings = gross output (at basic price, including own consumption) − total expenses** (current inputs, hired labour, rent and interest, production taxes net of production subsidies).
SC: Self-employed income = output − costs, with output valued at basic price.

Q: In PLFS, the gross earnings of a helper in a household enterprise are taken as:
A) Zero
B) Equal share of enterprise profit
C) Minimum wage
D) Half of the owner's earning
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 85
EXP: **Helpers (code 21)** are unpaid, so their **earnings are taken as zero**. When owners come from the same household, earnings are split **equally** among them. For partnerships across households, earnings follow the partnership agreement.
SC: Helpers are unpaid, so their earning is zero.

Q: The "basic price" used for valuing the output of self-employed enterprises in PLFS is:
A) amount receivable from purchaser minus product taxes plus product subsidies
B) purchaser's price including transport margins
C) factor cost minus production taxes
D) market price plus product taxes
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 84
EXP: **Basic price = amount receivable per unit − taxes on products + subsidies on products**. This matches the national accounts concept behind GVA at basic prices.
SC: Basic price = producer's price net of product taxes plus product subsidies.

@topic Enterprise Types & Conditions of Employment
Q: In PLFS, household enterprises comprise:
A) all proprietary and partnership enterprises
B) only proprietary enterprises
C) proprietary, partnership and private limited companies
D) cooperatives and trusts
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 85
EXP: A household enterprise is run by one or more household members, or jointly by households as partners. **All proprietary and partnership enterprises are household enterprises**, wherever they are located. In PLFS, these make up the **informal sector**.
SC: Proprietary + partnership = household enterprise = informal sector in PLFS.

Q: As per the definitions in PLFS, the minimum number of members required to form a public limited company is:
A) Seven
B) Two
C) Fifty
D) Two hundred
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 86
EXP: A **public limited company** needs at least **seven** members and can invite the public to subscribe. A **private limited company** is **limited to 200 members**, restricts share transfer and cannot invite the public.
SC: Public needs at least 7. Private allows at most 200.

Q: If a government agency is a member or shareholder of a registered co-operative society, PLFS treats the society as:
A) still a co-operative society, not a public sector enterprise
B) a public sector enterprise
C) a government department
D) an autonomous body
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 86
EXP: The report says government membership in a co-operative **does not turn it into a public sector enterprise** for the survey.
SC: A co-operative stays a co-operative even with government as a member.

Q: Which of the following is given as an example of an Autonomous Body in PLFS definitions?
A) Indian Statistical Institute
B) Food Corporation of India
C) Indian Railways
D) Life Insurance Corporation
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 86
EXP: **Autonomous bodies** (AIIMS, IITs, **ISI**, housing boards, pollution control boards) are set up by Act or registered as societies, receive grants and do **non-market production**. The FCI and LIC are **PSEs**. **Railways** is a **departmental enterprise** under Government.
SC: Institutes and boards are autonomous bodies. Corporations are PSEs. Railways and Posts are Government departments.

Q: Indian Railways and the Department of Posts are classified in PLFS enterprise types as:
A) Government/local body (departmental enterprises)
B) Public sector enterprises
C) Autonomous bodies
D) Public limited companies
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 85
EXP: The **Government/local body** category includes Central and State departments, local bodies and **departmental enterprises such as Indian Railways and the Department of Posts**.
SC: Departmental enterprises count as Government, not PSEs.

Q: For job contract duration in PLFS, a written contract that can be terminated only for administrative reasons (misconduct, incompetence) with no fixed end date is treated as having a duration of:
A) more than 3 years
B) 1 year or less
C) more than 1 year to 3 years
D) no written contract
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 87
EXP: A contract ending more than 3 years away, or with **no fixed end** that can be terminated **only for administrative or economic reasons**, is treated as **more than 3 years**. If there is no written contract, it is recorded as "no written job contract" regardless of how long the job has lasted.
SC: A permanent-type contract falls in the ">3 years" category.

Q: In PLFS, an employee operating a PPF account in individual capacity with no employer contribution is considered to be:
A) not availing social security benefit through the employer
B) covered under PF/pension social security
C) covered under gratuity
D) eligible for paid leave
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 88
EXP: A social security benefit counts only if the **employer contributes, arranges or pays**. An individually run PPF **without employer contribution does not count**. Schemes with contributions from **both** employer and employee do count.
SC: The benefit must come through the employer to count.

Q: The social security benefits considered in PLFS are combinations of which three components?
A) PF/pension, gratuity, and health care & maternity benefits
B) PF, unemployment insurance and housing allowance
C) Pension, bonus and paid leave
D) ESI, EPF and LIC
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 88
EXP: The three components are **PF/pension, gratuity, and health care & maternity benefits**, giving 7 combinations. An employee with none is recorded as not eligible for any social security.
SC: P-G-H: PF/Pension, Gratuity, Health/maternity. 2³ − 1 = 7 combinations.

@topic ICLS: Informal Sector & Informal Employment
Q: The international definition of the informal sector (enterprise-based) was adopted by the:
A) 15th ICLS, January 1993
B) 17th ICLS, 2003
C) 13th ICLS, 1982
D) 19th ICLS, 2013
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 97
EXP: The **15th ICLS (January 1993)** adopted the resolution on **employment in the informal sector** (production units). The **17th ICLS (2003)** added guidelines on **informal employment** (jobs).
SC: 15th = sector (units), 17th = employment (jobs).

Q: The concept of informal sector refers to ___ as observation units, while informal employment refers to ___ as observation units.
A) production units; jobs
B) jobs; production units
C) households; persons
D) persons; establishments
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 102
EXP: The 17th ICLS guidelines say the **informal sector** is **enterprise-based** (production units) while **informal employment** is **job-based**. A formal enterprise can have informal jobs.
SC: Sector = units, employment = jobs.

Q: Which Expert Group recommended that the measurement of employment in the informal sector be complemented with a definition and measurement of informal employment?
A) Delhi Group
B) Voorburg Group
C) Ottawa Group
D) Siena Group
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 101
EXP: The **Delhi Group** (Expert Group on Informal Sector Statistics, chaired by India) made the recommendation at its **fifth meeting**. Voorburg covers service statistics, Ottawa price indices, and Siena social statistics.
SC: Delhi Group = informal sector. Remember the city groups by subject.

Q: As per the 15th ICLS, informal sector units are characterised by:
1. production primarily to generate employment and incomes for the persons concerned
2. little or no division between labour and capital
3. being separate legal entities with complete sets of accounts
Which of the above is/are correct?
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: A
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 97
EXP: Statements 1 and 2 describe informal units. Statement 3 is the opposite: informal units are household enterprises that are **not separate legal entities** and have **no complete accounts**. The owners carry unlimited personal liability.
SC: Informal means no separate legal identity, no full accounts and unlimited liability.

Q: As per the 15th ICLS, the informal sector comprises which two components within the household sector?
A) Informal own-account enterprises and enterprises of informal employers
B) Formal enterprises and quasi-corporations
C) Public sector units and cooperatives
D) Non-profit institutions and trusts
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 97
EXP: Within the household sector, the informal sector = (i) **informal own-account enterprises** (no continuous employees) + (ii) **enterprises of informal employers** (with continuous employees, below a size limit or unregistered).
SC: Own-account + informal employers.

Q: Under the 15th ICLS, household enterprises engaged exclusively in non-market production (goods for own final consumption) are:
A) excluded from the informal sector for employment statistics
B) always included in the informal sector
C) treated as formal enterprises
D) classified as quasi-corporations
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 99
EXP: Paragraph 14 says household units producing **only for own final use** are **excluded from the informal sector**. Paid domestic workers are handled separately.
SC: The informal sector must have some market production.

Q: Under the 15th ICLS, self-employed professionals such as doctors, lawyers and accountants should be:
A) included in the informal sector if they meet its criteria
B) always excluded from the informal sector
C) always included in the formal sector
D) treated as domestic workers
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 100
EXP: Paragraph 17 says professional or technical units run by the self-employed are **included in the informal sector if they fulfil paragraphs 8 or 9**. Being a professional alone does not make the unit formal.
SC: The unit's characteristics decide, not the occupation.

Q: As per the 17th ICLS guidelines, contributing family workers are counted in informal employment:
A) irrespective of whether they work in formal or informal sector enterprises
B) only if they work in informal sector enterprises
C) only if they work in agriculture
D) never
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 102
EXP: **Contributing family workers** have **informal jobs by definition**, whether in formal enterprises (cell 1) or informal ones (cell 5).
SC: Family helpers are always informal.

Q: As per the 17th ICLS conceptual matrix, employees holding formal jobs in informal sector enterprises (cell 7) are:
A) excluded from informal employment
B) included in informal employment
C) counted as informal employment outside the informal sector
D) counted as contributing family workers
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 103
EXP: **Cell 7** (formal jobs in informal-sector enterprises) is **excluded** from informal employment, although it is still **employment in the informal sector**. Informal employment = cells **1–6 and 8–10**. Employment in the informal sector = cells **3–8**.
SC: Cell 7 is in the informal sector but not informal employment.

Q: As per the 17th ICLS matrix, "informal employment outside the informal sector" comprises cells:
A) 1, 2, 9 and 10
B) 3 to 8
C) 1 to 6 and 8 to 10
D) 7 only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 104
EXP: Informal employment outside the informal sector = **cells 1, 2, 9, 10**. These are family workers in formal enterprises, informal employees of formal enterprises, own-use producers, and paid domestic workers.
SC: Informal employment (1-6, 8-10) minus informal-sector cells (3-6, 8) leaves 1, 2, 9 and 10.

Q: Employees are considered to hold informal jobs (17th ICLS) if their employment relationship is, in law or practice, NOT subject to:
A) national labour legislation, income taxation, social protection or employment benefits
B) the Companies Act only
C) the Right to Information Act
D) the Collection of Statistics Act
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 103
EXP: Informal employee jobs lack coverage by **labour law, income tax, social protection or benefits** such as notice, severance pay and paid leave. PLFS measures this through **no written contract, no paid leave and no social security**.
SC: No protection means an informal job, which PLFS shows through its conditions-of-employment indicators.

Q: In PLFS, information on informal sector enterprises and conditions of employment was collected for industry groups/divisions:
A) 014, 016, 017, 02–99 of NIC-2008 (AGEGC and non-agriculture)
B) 011 to 015 only
C) All divisions 01–99 including crop growing
D) Divisions 10–33 (manufacturing) only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 90
EXP: Coverage was **AGEGC** (Agricultural sector Excluding Growing of Crops: groups 014 animal production, 016 support activities, 017 hunting, and divisions 02 forestry and 03 fishing) plus **non-agriculture (05–99)**. Groups 011, 012, 013 and 015 were **excluded**.
SC: AGEGC = Agriculture Excluding Growing of Crops, i.e. 014, 016, 017, 02, 03.

@topic Social/Religious Groups, Indicators & Classifications
Q: In PLFS, the social group recorded for all members of a household is:
A) that of the head of the household, as reported by the informant
B) each member's own social group
C) as per the State list of castes
D) as per the father's caste certificate
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 90
EXP: The **head's social group** applies to **all members**. It is based **entirely on the informant's response**, not on any official list. Four groups are used: ST, SC, OBC and others. Religion is recorded the same way (7 major religions).
SC: The head's group applies to everyone, as self-reported.

Q: "Proportion Unemployed (PU)" in PLFS is defined as:
A) unemployed persons as a percentage of the total population
B) unemployed persons as a percentage of the labour force
C) employed persons as a percentage of the population
D) labour force as a percentage of the population
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 91
EXP: **PU = unemployed ÷ total population × 100**. **UR = unemployed ÷ labour force × 100**. So LFPR = WPR + PU.
SC: PU is over the population, UR is over the labour force. LFPR = WPR + PU.

Q: If in a region WPR = 45% and PU = 3%, the unemployment rate is:
A) 6.25%
B) 3.0%
C) 6.67%
D) 48.0%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 91
EXP: LFPR = WPR + PU = 48%. **UR = PU ÷ LFPR × 100 = 3 ÷ 48 × 100 = 6.25%**. 6.67% comes from dividing by the WPR, which is wrong.
SC: UR = PU ÷ (WPR + PU).

Q: Why cannot the household usual monthly consumer expenditure collected in PLFS be used to estimate household consumption?
A) It is collected through a single-shot question, only for forming UMPCE decile classes
B) It is collected only for urban areas
C) It excludes food items
D) It refers to a 7-day recall period
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 91
EXP: PLFS collects UMCE through a **single-shot question**, only to make **UMPCE decile classes**. Consumption is estimated through the detailed **HCES (Schedule 1.0)**.
SC: A single question is enough for ranking but not for measuring consumption.

A) F
B) C
C) G
D) H
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 93
EXP: A Agriculture; B Mining; C Manufacturing; D Electricity, gas; E Water supply and waste; **F Construction** (divisions 41–43); G Trade; H Transport and storage; I Accommodation and food.

Q: Under NIC-2008, Division 84 "Public administration and defence; compulsory social security" falls under Section:
A) O
B) P
C) N
D) Q
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 94
EXP: **Section O = Public administration and defence** (division 84). P = Education (85). Q = Health and social work (86–88). N = Administrative and support services (77–82).
SC: O-P-Q = Official administration, Pedagogy (education), Quality of health.

Q: NIC-2008 Section T covers:
A) activities of households as employers and undifferentiated production for own use
B) extraterritorial organisations
C) tourism and travel
D) telecommunications
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 95
EXP: **Section T** = activities of **households as employers of domestic personnel (97)** and undifferentiated goods and services production for own use (98). **Section U** = extraterritorial organisations (99). Telecom is in J (61).
SC: The last two sections are T (households, 97-98) and U (extraterritorial bodies, 99).

Q: In NCO-2004, Division 6 refers to:
A) Skilled Agricultural and Fishery Workers
B) Craft and Related Trades Workers
C) Elementary Occupations
D) Clerks
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 96
EXP: NCO-2004 divisions: 1 Legislators/Senior Officials/Managers, 2 Professionals, 3 Technicians and Associate Professionals, 4 Clerks, 5 Service and Sales, **6 Skilled Agricultural and Fishery**, 7 Craft, 8 Plant and Machine Operators, 9 Elementary, X not classified.
SC: The divisions fall in skill order from 1 down to 9. Farming is 6, just before craft (7).

Q: In NCO-2004, sub-division "X0" relates to:
A) new workers seeking employment
B) armed forces
C) workers reporting unidentifiable occupations
D) workers not reporting any occupation
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 96
EXP: **X0 = new workers seeking employment**, X1 = occupations unidentifiable or inadequately described, X9 = no occupation reported.
SC: X0 means zero experience, i.e. new job-seekers.
