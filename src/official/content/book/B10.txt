@topic ASI: Legal Basis, Schedule & Periods
Q: From ASI 2010-11 onwards, the Annual Survey of Industries is conducted under the statutory provisions of:
A) the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008 (as amended in 2017) and the Rules of 2011
B) the Collection of Statistics Act, 1953 and Rules of 1959
C) the Factories Act, 1948
D) the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 151
EXP: Until 2009-10 the ASI ran under the **CoS Act 1953** and its **1959 Rules**. J&K used the J&K CoS Act 1961 and its 1964 Rules. **From ASI 2010-11** it runs under the **CoS Act 2008** (amended 2017) and the **CoS Rules 2011**.
SC: Old ASI: Act 1953 / Rules 1959. New ASI (2010-11 onwards): Act 2008 / Rules 2011.

Q: Part-II of the ASI schedule, which collects data on man-days worked, absenteeism and labour turnover, is processed by:
A) the Labour Bureau
B) the IS Wing, DQAD, Kolkata
C) the NSO (FOD)
D) the Office of the Economic Adviser
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 151
EXP: **Part-I** (assets, employment, inputs, outputs, products) is processed by the **IS Wing, DQAD, Kolkata**. **Part-II** (labour statistics such as working days, man-days, absenteeism, labour turnover and man-hours) is processed by the **Labour Bureau**.
SC: Part I is economic data handled by the IS Wing. Part II is labour data handled by the Labour Bureau.

Q: The reference period for ASI 2020-21 is:
A) 1 April 2020 to 31 March 2021
B) 1 January 2020 to 31 December 2020
C) 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2021
D) November 2021 to July 2022
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 151
EXP: The ASI reference year is the **financial year (April–March)** for both parts. The **survey period** usually runs from **November to July** of the year after the reference year.
SC: Reference period = financial year. Field work = November to July afterwards.

Q: The actual survey (field) period for ASI generally runs:
A) from November to July in the subsequent year following the reference period
B) during the reference year itself
C) only in the month of April
D) from January to December of the reference year
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 151
EXP: Books of account close in March, so the survey is carried out from **November to July** after the reference year ends.
SC: Collect after the accounts are closed, i.e. November to July.

@topic ASI: Coverage & Units
Q: Which of the following is covered under ASI?
A) Factories registered under Sections 2(m)(i) and 2(m)(ii) of the Factories Act, 1948
B) Defence establishments
C) Railway workshops
D) Oil storage and distribution depots
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 151
EXP: The ASI covers factories under **Sections 2(m)(i) and 2(m)(ii)**, **bidi and cigar establishments** (1966 Act), and **electricity undertakings not registered with the CEA**. **Defence establishments, oil storage depots and departmental units** (railway and RTC workshops, Government mints) are **excluded**.
SC: Registered factories are in. Defence and departmental workshops are out.

Q: Bidi and cigar establishments are covered under ASI if registered under:
A) the Bidi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966
B) the Tobacco Board Act, 1975
C) the Shops and Establishments Act
D) the Companies Act, 1956
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 151
EXP: The ASI covers **bidi and cigar** establishments registered under the **Bidi and Cigar Workers (Conditions of Employment) Act, 1966**. For bidi and cigar, the establishment is the unit of enumeration.
SC: Bidi units are covered through their own 1966 Act.

Q: Electricity undertakings engaged in generation, transmission and distribution are covered under ASI only if they are:
A) not registered with the Central Electricity Authority
B) registered with the Central Electricity Authority
C) owned by State Electricity Boards
D) using renewable energy only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 151
EXP: Electricity undertakings **not registered with the CEA** are covered by the ASI. Those registered with the CEA are covered through CEA statistics, which avoids double counting.
SC: CEA-registered units are counted by the CEA, so the ASI takes the rest.

Q: As decided by the Standing Committee on Industrial Statistics, ASI coverage was extended to units with how many or more employees that are not registered under Sections 2m(i)/2m(ii) but are registered under any of seven specified Acts/Boards in the Business Register of Establishments?
A) 100
B) 50
C) 20
D) 10
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 152
EXP: Units with **100 or more employees**, registered under any of **seven** Acts or authorities (Companies Act, Factories Act, Shops & Establishment Act, Societies Act, Cooperative Societies Act, KVIB, DIC) in the **BRE** and verified by FOD, are also considered for selection.
SC: The extension covers large units (100+) found through the BRE.

Q: For data collection in ASI, which approach is followed?
A) Establishment approach
B) Enterprise approach
C) Household approach
D) Company approach
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 152
EXP: The ASI follows the **establishment approach**. An establishment is an enterprise or part of one at a **single location** with a **single (or principal) productive activity**. An enterprise may have several locations and activities.
SC: ASI works by factory site (establishment), not by company (enterprise).

Q: The primary unit of enumeration in ASI for electricity, gas and water supply undertakings is:
A) an undertaking or a licensee
B) a factory
C) a workshop
D) an establishment
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 152
EXP: The unit is a **factory** for manufacturing, a **workshop** for repair services, an **undertaking or licensee** for electricity, gas and water, and an **establishment** for bidi and cigar.
SC: Manufacturing = factory, repair = workshop, utilities = undertaking, bidi = establishment.

Q: A "Joint Return" in ASI is permitted when two or more establishments of the same owner belong to the same:
A) State, sector and 3-digit industry group, falling under the census scheme
B) district and any industry
C) company across different States
D) product at 7-digit NPCMS level only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 152
EXP: A **Joint Return (JR)** is allowed for establishments of one owner in the **same State, same sector** (bidi, factory or electricity) and **same 3-digit NIC**, under the census scheme. It is common for bidi and electricity units.
SC: For a JR the State, the sector and the 3-digit NIC must all match.

@topic ASI: Legal Roles under CoS Act 2008
Q: As per the Collection of Statistics Rules, 2011, the Nodal Officer, whose consultation is mandatory for conducting any survey, must be an officer not below the rank of:
A) Joint Secretary to the Government of India
B) Director
C) Additional Secretary
D) Deputy Secretary
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 152
EXP: The **Nodal Officer** is an officer of the nodal statistical department **not below Joint Secretary** rank. Consulting the Nodal Officer is **mandatory** before any survey under the CoS Act.
SC: The Nodal Officer is of Joint Secretary rank or above, and must be consulted before a survey.

Q: Under the CoS Act, 2008, which official is appointed for a specified period and territory to conduct the ASI and is empowered to issue notices to factories?
A) Statistics Officer
B) Nodal Officer
C) Chief Inspector of Factories
D) Registrar of Companies
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 152
EXP: A **Statistics Officer** (Section 4 of the Act, Rule 7) is appointed to conduct the survey. The officer can **issue notices** and **authorise staff** to collect information. Defaulters can be prosecuted with the Statistics Officer's sanction.
SC: The Statistics Officer runs the survey. The Nodal Officer is consulted beforehand.

Q: Under the CoS Act, 2008, a person employed in executing the Act who violates confidentiality may be prosecuted by or with the consent of the appropriate Government under Section:
A) 25
B) 15
C) 9
D) 33
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 153
EXP: The manual cites **Section 25** for prosecuting officials who fail in duty, make false declarations or breach confidentiality. Defaulting units are prosecuted with the Statistics Officer's sanction.
SC: Section 25 covers prosecution of officials.

Q: Duly filled-in ASI returns for the Central Sample are transmitted for processing to:
A) the IS Wing, DQAD, through the ASI Web Portal
B) the State DES by post
C) the Labour Bureau only
D) the CIF office
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 153
EXP: FOD verifies the returns and sends the Central Sample returns **online through the ASI Web Portal** to the **IS Wing, DQAD, Kolkata** for processing and publication.
SC: Returns go online to the IS Wing in Kolkata.

Q: The ASI frame is revised every year before selection of units by:
A) NSO (FOD) in consultation with the Chief Inspector of Factories of the State
B) the Labour Bureau alone
C) the Registrar of Companies
D) the RBI
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 153
EXP: **FOD** updates the frame **annually** with the **CIF**. De-registered factories are removed and newly registered ones added. Feedback from States is also taken when a State runs the State sample.
SC: The frame is maintained by FOD with the CIF, every year.

Q: Under the ASI Census Scheme, all industrial units in which of the following States/UTs are surveyed completely?
A) Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura, Ladakh and Andaman & Nicobar Islands
B) Delhi, Chandigarh and Puducherry
C) Rajasthan, Bihar and Kerala
D) Maharashtra, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 154
EXP: All units in the **nine less industrially developed States/UTs** are in the census: the NE States except Assam, plus Sikkim, **Ladakh and A&N Islands**. **Delhi, Chandigarh and Puducherry** use a **50+ employee** threshold. **Rajasthan, Bihar, Kerala** (with J&K, HP and Chhattisgarh) use **75+**.
SC: Small industrial bases (NE, Ladakh, A&N) have all units in the census.

Q: For the census scheme of ASI, the employment threshold for Delhi, Chandigarh and Puducherry is:
A) 50 or more employees
B) 75 or more employees
C) 100 or more employees
D) 20 or more employees
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 154
EXP: Thresholds: **50+** for Chandigarh, Delhi and Puducherry. **75+** for J&K, HP, Rajasthan, Bihar, Chhattisgarh and Kerala. **100+** for all other States.
SC: 50 for city-UTs, 75 for six mid States, 100 for the rest.

Q: Which industrial units are included in the ASI census scheme irrespective of State or size?
A) All units with frame NIC 0893 (salt extraction)
B) All textile mills
C) All units with fewer than 10 workers
D) All public sector units
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 154
EXP: **All units with frame NIC 0893 (salt extraction)** are in the census. **All Joint Return factories** and all units in strata with **4 or fewer units** are also census units.
SC: Salt extraction (0893) is always in the census.

Q: In the ASI sample scheme, strata are formed on the basis of:
A) State × District × Sector × 3-digit NIC-2008
B) State × 2-digit NIC only
C) District × employment size only
D) Sector × 5-digit NIC
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 154
EXP: Strata = **State × District × Sector** (bidi, manufacturing, electricity) **× 3-digit NIC-2008**. Strata with **4 or fewer units** go to the census.
SC: The stratum is defined by State, District, Sector and 3-digit NIC.

Q: Samples in the ASI sample scheme are drawn using:
A) Circular systematic sampling after arranging units in descending order of employees
B) Simple random sampling without replacement
C) PPS with replacement, size = output
D) Cluster sampling by district
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 154
EXP: Units in each stratum are arranged in **descending order of total employees**, then drawn by **circular systematic sampling**. An **even number (minimum 4)** is selected and spread over **4 sub-samples**.
SC: Sort by employees, then circular systematic, minimum 4 units in 4 sub-samples.

Q: In the ASI design, which sub-samples are assigned to NSO (FOD) and which to the State/UT?
A) Sub-samples 1 & 3 to NSO (FOD); 2 & 4 to the State/UT
B) Sub-samples 1 & 2 to NSO (FOD); 3 & 4 to the State/UT
C) All four to NSO (FOD)
D) Sub-samples 2 & 4 to NSO (FOD); 1 & 3 to the State/UT
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 154
EXP: **Sub-samples 1 & 3** go to **NSO (FOD)** and **2 & 4** to the **State/UT**. **Central Sample = census units + sub-samples 1 & 3**. **State Sample = census units + sub-samples 2 & 4**. **Pooled** estimates use all of them.
SC: Odd sub-samples are Central, even sub-samples are State.

Q: For deriving State-level estimates based on the State Sample, a State/UT must use:
A) Census units (collected by NSO-FOD) along with its own two sub-samples
B) only its own two sub-samples
C) only census units
D) all four sub-samples excluding census units
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 155
EXP: **State Sample = all census units** (collected by FOD and processed by the IS Wing) **plus the State's two sub-samples**. States must combine both to get their estimates.
SC: Census units are shared by both samples.

D: Medium

EXP: **NIC-1970** was based on ISIC Rev. 2 (1968). **NIC-1998** on ISIC Rev. 3. **NIC-2004** on ISIC Rev. 3.1. **NIC-2008** on **ISIC Rev. 4**.
SC: NIC 70/98/04/08 map to ISIC Rev 2/3/3.1/4.

1. NIC-1998
2. Classification of Indian Industries
3. NIC-1987
4. NIC-2004

B) 3, 2, 1, 4
C) 2, 1, 3, 4
D) 3, 1, 2, 4
ANS: A
T: Chronology
D: Medium

SC: NPCMS = CPC (products). NIC = ISIC (industries).

@topic ASI: Factory & Capital Concepts
Q: As per Section 2(m) of the Factories Act, 1948, premises using power in a manufacturing process qualify as a factory if they employ:
A) 10 or more workers
B) 20 or more workers
C) 50 or more workers
D) 5 or more workers
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 156
EXP: **2m(i)**: **10 or more workers with power**. **2m(ii)**: **20 or more without power**. For **Rajasthan and Maharashtra**, amendments raised these to **20 with power and 40 without**, applied from ASI 2015-16.
SC: 10 with power, 20 without. Rajasthan and Maharashtra doubled both.

Q: For ASI purposes, "power" means any motive power used to drive the plant and machinery, except:
A) animal and human power
B) electrical power
C) steam power
D) solar power
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 156
EXP: Under the Factories Act, **power** means any **motive power except animal and human power**.
SC: Muscle power (human or animal) is not "power".

Q: Fixed capital in ASI excludes intangible assets, except:
A) computer software
B) goodwill
C) patents
D) trademarks
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 157
EXP: **Fixed capital** is the **depreciated value** of fixed assets (life above 1 year) on the closing day. It includes head office assets allocable to the factory and **hire-purchase assets at full value (excluding interest)**, and **excludes intangibles except computer software**.
SC: Software is the only intangible counted in fixed capital.

Q: In ASI, "invested capital" is defined as:
A) Fixed capital + Physical working capital
B) Fixed capital + Working capital
C) Working capital − Outstanding loans
D) Gross value of plant and machinery + stocks
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 158
EXP: **Invested capital = fixed capital + physical working capital**. **Productive capital = fixed capital + working capital**, where working capital adds cash and net receivables to physical working capital.
SC: Invested uses physical WC (inventories). Productive uses total WC (plus cash and receivables).

Q: Which of the following is EXCLUDED from physical working capital in ASI?
A) Stock of materials supplied by others to the factory for processing
B) Stock of fuels and lubricants owned by the factory
C) Finished goods ready for sale at the end of the year
D) Semi-finished goods owned by the factory
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 157
EXP: Physical working capital covers inventories **owned or controlled** by the factory. **Materials supplied by others for processing** are **excluded** because the factory does not own them. Finished goods processed by others from the factory's materials are included.
SC: Ownership decides. Goods owned by others are excluded.

Q: For ASI purposes, "outstanding loans" represent:
A) long-term loans with duration of more than one year
B) all short-term borrowings
C) unused overdraft facility
D) trade credit from suppliers
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 158
EXP: In the ASI, outstanding loans are **only long-term liabilities (over one year)**, interest-bearing or not, as on the closing day.
SC: ASI loans = long-term only.

@topic ASI: Labour & Output Concepts
Q: In ASI, "Total Persons Engaged" includes:
1. Working proprietors and their family members actively engaged without pay
2. Watch and ward staff and sales department staff
3. Unpaid members of co-operative societies working for the factory
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 158
EXP: All three are included. Total persons engaged = all persons connected directly or indirectly with manufacturing, including administrative, clerical, supervisory, sales, watch and ward staff, **working proprietors and unpaid family members**, and **unpaid co-operative members**.
SC: "Total persons engaged" covers everyone, paid or unpaid.

Q: A worker engaged through a third-party agency that charges the factory for the service is termed in ASI as:
A) contract worker
B) casual worker
C) apprentice
D) outworker
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 158
EXP: **Contract workers** are not employed directly by the factory. They are engaged through an **agency or contractor**, which charges the factory.
SC: A third-party contractor means a contract worker.

Q: In computing labour turnover in ASI, which of the following is ignored?
A) Inter-departmental transfers within the same establishment
B) Transfers to other establishments under the same management
C) Retirement
D) Retrenchment due to modernisation
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 158
EXP: **Accession** and **separation** include transfers **to or from other establishments**, death, retirement and retrenchment. **Transfers within the same establishment are ignored**.
SC: Only movement across the factory gate counts.

Q: Which of the following is EXCLUDED from "wages" in ASI?
A) Employer's contribution to provident fund and other social security
B) Dearness allowance
C) Overtime payment
D) Production bonus paid regularly
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 159
EXP: **Wages** include basic pay, DA, HRA and other allowances, overtime, pay for leave and holidays, and **regular** bonuses. They **exclude employer contributions to PF and social security**, maternity and creche expenses, group benefits and reimbursed business expenses. Wages are **gross** of employee deductions.
SC: Employer contributions are supplements, not wages.

Q: In ASI, Compensation of Employees is defined as:
A) Emoluments + Supplements to emoluments
B) Wages + Bonus
C) Wages − Provident fund deductions
D) Emoluments − Taxes
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 160
EXP: **Emoluments** = wages to all employees + imputed benefits in kind + infrequent bonuses. **Supplements** = employer's PF, pension and gratuity contributions, ESI and other social security, and group benefits. **CE = emoluments + supplements**.
SC: CE = E + S (emoluments + supplements).

Q: Cotton waste used in a factory, which assists the manufacturing process but loses its identity without entering the product, is an example of:
A) consumable stores
B) basic materials
C) intermediate products
D) by-products
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 160
EXP: **Consumable stores** help the process and lose their identity without entering the product, for example **cotton waste**. **Basic materials** such as metal for machines or leather for shoes change form but stay in the product.
SC: Consumed in the process = stores. Embodied in the product = materials.

Q: In ASI, "Gross Output" includes which of the following?
1. Ex-factory value of products and by-products
2. Net value of semi-finished goods
3. Rent received for buildings and plant & machinery
4. An amount equal to expenses on R&D
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 1, 2 and 3 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Hard
P: 161
EXP: Gross output = products and by-products (**ex-factory value**, excluding taxes and including subsidies) + **net value of semi-finished goods** + own construction + receipts for industrial and non-industrial services + **rent received** + net sale value of goods resold in the same condition + electricity generated and sold + **an amount equal to R&D expenses**. R&D is also added to **total input**, so it does not change GVA.
SC: R&D is added to both output and input, so it has no effect on GVA.

Q: Products in ASI are valued at ex-factory value, which means:
A) exclusive of taxes and duties on sale and inclusive of subsidies
B) inclusive of all taxes and duties
C) exclusive of subsidies
D) at retail price
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 161
EXP: **Ex-factory value** excludes taxes and duties on sale and includes subsidies. This corresponds to the **basic price** concept.
SC: Ex-factory = net of taxes, plus subsidies = basic price.

Q: In ASI, Net Value Added (NVA) is obtained by:
A) deducting total input and depreciation from gross output
B) deducting depreciation from total input
C) deducting rent and interest from GVA
D) deducting compensation of employees from gross output
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 162
EXP: **GVA = gross output − total input**. **NVA = GVA − depreciation**. **Net income = NVA − rent paid − interest paid**. **Profit = net income − compensation of employees**.
SC: Output → GVA (minus input) → NVA (minus depreciation) → Net income (minus rent and interest) → Profit (minus CE).

Q: In ASI, "Profit" is obtained by deducting which item from Net Income?
A) Compensation of employees
B) Depreciation
C) Rent paid
D) Total input
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 162
EXP: **Profit = net income − compensation of employees**. Rent and interest are deducted earlier (NVA → net income), and depreciation is deducted before that (GVA → NVA).
SC: The last step subtracts labour cost (CE) to get profit.

Q: Given ASI data for a factory: Gross output = ₹500 lakh, Total input = ₹350 lakh, Depreciation = ₹20 lakh, Rent paid = ₹5 lakh, Interest paid = ₹15 lakh, Compensation of employees = ₹60 lakh. What is the profit?
A) ₹50 lakh
B) ₹70 lakh
C) ₹110 lakh
D) ₹130 lakh
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 162
EXP: GVA = 500 − 350 = 150. NVA = 150 − 20 = 130. Net income = 130 − 5 − 15 = 110. **Profit = 110 − 60 = ₹50 lakh**.
SC: Subtract step by step: input, depreciation, rent and interest, then CE.
