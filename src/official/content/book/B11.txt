@topic ASI Schedule: Structure
Q: Part-I of the ASI schedule is structured into how many blocks?
A) 14
B) 12
C) 10
D) 16
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 163
EXP: Part-I has **14 blocks (A to N)**. Block A is identification, B is factory particulars, C to L are economic particulars, M is field operations and N is officers' remarks. Separate **Part-A and Part-B** record the scrutiny report and the Scrutinising Officer's observations.
SC: A to N is 14 letters, which gives 14 blocks.

Q: Which blocks of the ASI schedule relate to the entire establishment rather than only the manufacturing process?
A) Block K (ICT usage) and Block L (energy conservation measures)
B) Block H and Block I
C) Block C and Block D
D) Block M and Block N
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 165
EXP: All other blocks relate to the manufacturing process. **Blocks K (ICT usage) and L (energy conservation)** relate to the **whole establishment**.
SC: K and L (ICT and energy) are establishment-wide.

Q: Match List-I (ASI Block) with List-II (Content):
| List-I | List-II |
|---|---|
| A. Block H | 1. Imported input items consumed |
| B. Block I | 2. Products and by-products manufactured |
| C. Block J | 3. Indigenous input items consumed |
| D. Block E | 4. Employment and labour cost |
A) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
B) A-1, B-3, C-2, D-4
C) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
D) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2
ANS: A
T: Match
D: Medium
P: 164
EXP: Block **C** fixed assets, **D** working capital and loans, **E** employment and labour cost, **F** other expenses, **G** other output/receipts, **H** indigenous inputs, **I** imported inputs, **J** products and by-products, **K** ICT, **L** energy conservation.
SC: In the alphabet, H (home-made inputs) comes before I (imported inputs), then J (products).

Q: Most of the information in the ASI schedule is collected from:
A) audited Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Account of the factory
B) GST returns only
C) interviews with workers
D) Chief Inspector of Factories' registers
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 163
EXP: Data come mainly from the **audited financial statements** (Balance Sheet and P&L). **Annexure VI** gives a ready reference that maps balance sheet items to schedule items.
SC: ASI data are taken from the books of account.

@topic Block A: Identification & Status
Q: In ASI, the Despatch Serial Number (DSL) is:
A) unique across the country for a particular survey year, but the same factory may get different DSLs in different years
B) permanent for a factory across all years
C) assigned by the CIF
D) the same as the CIN
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 165
EXP: The **DSL** reconciles schedules despatched by FOD with those received by the IS Wing. It is **unique for a survey year** and can change between years. The **PSL (Permanent Serial Number)** stays the same over time and is unique within **State × Sector × 4-digit NIC**.
SC: DSL changes each year (despatch). PSL is permanent.

Q: In Block A of ASI, the "scheme code" for census units and sample units is respectively:
A) 1 and 2 (and always 2 for the State sample)
B) 2 and 1
C) 0 and 1
D) C and S
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 166
EXP: **Census units = 1**, **sample units = 2**. In the **State sample** the code is always 2.
SC: Census comes first (1), sample second (2).

Q: The "industry code as per return" in Block A of ASI is decided on the basis of:
A) maximum ex-factory value of the major product among products and by-products (5-digit NIC-2008)
B) the product with maximum quantity produced
C) the industry with maximum employment
D) the code on the Factory licence
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 166
EXP: The code is based on the product with the **highest ex-factory value**, recorded at **5-digit NIC-2008**. For job-work units with no Block J entries, it comes from the maximum output in **Block G**.
SC: Choose the product with the highest value, not the highest quantity.

Q: A unit engaged in repair of vehicles also sells vehicles, with trading output far exceeding repair output. For ASI, the industry code to be reported is:
A) the code relating to repair and maintenance activity
B) the trading code, since its output is larger
C) any code chosen by the owner
D) no code, the unit is excluded
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 166
EXP: Trading is **outside ASI's purview**, so the **repair and maintenance** code is reported. If the unit has **stopped repair altogether** and only trades, it is given status code **"Deleted (04)"**.
SC: ASI ignores trading. If only trading remains, the unit is deleted (code 04).

Q: In Block A of ASI, the sector code for a unit located in an urban area is:
A) 2
B) 1
C) 3
D) U
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 167
EXP: **Rural = 1, Urban = 2**, based on the latest administrative order. No other code is allowed, and the item cannot be left blank.
SC: Rural 1 and urban 2, as in all NSS surveys.

Q: Which of the following is a necessary condition for compiling a Joint Return (JR) in ASI?
1. Same State code
2. Same management
3. Only combined accounts are available, with resources not separately identifiable
4. Same 3-digit NIC industry group
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 1, 2 and 3 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 167
EXP: All four conditions are required. JR formation happens **only at the frame updation stage** (before sample selection). Sample scheme units can **never** form a JR, so for the sample scheme the number of units is always **1**.
SC: A JR needs same State, same management, combined accounts and same 3-digit NIC.

Q: After sample selection in ASI, if a Sample Scheme unit is found to satisfy JR conditions with Census Scheme units having a combined return, the information for the Sample unit must be:
A) segregated and a separate return filled for it; the JR code is assigned during the next frame update
B) merged into the existing joint return for the current year
C) dropped from the survey permanently
D) treated as a new census unit immediately
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 168
EXP: Census and sample units **cannot be joined after selection**. The sample unit's data are **apportioned** and a **separate return** is filed. At the **next frame update** it gets the same JR code, so it becomes a census unit from the following year.
SC: No JR after selection. Segregate now and join at the next frame update.

Q: In ASI, status of unit code 4 (Deleted) is assigned when:
A) the unit has had status code 3 continuously for three years or more, or does not exist, or is de-registered or out of coverage, and deletion is proposed by FOD
B) the unit is temporarily closed for one month
C) the unit refuses to submit the return
D) the unit's accounting year is not closed
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 169
EXP: **Code 4 (Deleted)** covers three or more years of code 3, non-existence, de-registration for operational reasons, or being out of coverage, when FOD proposes deletion. A new unit not yet in production, or with accounts not closed, gets **code 7**.
SC: Three years dormant (code 3) leads to deletion (code 4).

Q: If the status of a unit in ASI is '1' (open), which blocks must be filled in?
A) Blocks A, B, C, D and E
B) Block A only
C) Blocks A, B and C
D) Blocks A, B, C and E
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 169
EXP: Status **1** needs Blocks **A–E**. Status **2** needs **A, B, C, E**. Status **3** needs **A, B, C**. Any other status needs **Block A** only.
SC: The more active the unit, the more blocks are filled: 1 → A-E, 2 → A-C + E, 3 → A-C, others → A.

@topic Block B: Particulars of the Factory
Q: In ASI Block B, a Government Company-Public is one where the Government's paid-up share capital is not less than 51% and the number of shareholders is:
A) at least 7, with no upper limit
B) at least 1, with a maximum of 200
C) exactly 2
D) at least 50
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 170
EXP: A **Government** company has at least **51%** government share capital. A **Public** company has at least **7 shareholders and no upper limit**. A **Private** company has at least **1 and at most 200** shareholders. **Non-Government** companies have less than 51% government share, with the same public/private rules.
SC: 51% decides Government vs Non-Government. 7+ vs up to 200 decides Public vs Private.

Q: A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP), recorded as a separate organisation type in ASI, is characterised by:
A) being a separate legal entity with partners' liability limited to their agreed contribution
B) unlimited personal liability of all partners
C) mandatory Government shareholding
D) inability to hold property in its own name
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 170
EXP: An **LLP** is a **separate legal entity** with perpetual existence. It can contract and hold property. Each **partner's liability is limited to the agreed contribution**, and partners are protected from another partner's misconduct.
SC: LLP = limited liability of a company + flexibility of a partnership.

Q: The Corporate Identity Number (CIN) of a company, recorded in ASI Block B, is a:
A) 21-character alphanumeric code issued by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs
B) 15-digit GSTIN
C) 10-character PAN
D) 12-digit Aadhaar-linked number
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 171
EXP: The **CIN** has **21 characters**: listing status (L/U) + 5-digit industry code (NIC-2004) + 2-letter State code + 4-digit year of incorporation + 3-letter ownership code + 6-digit ROC registration number.
SC: 1 + 5 + 2 + 4 + 3 + 6 = 21.

Q: In a CIN, the first character 'U' indicates that the company is:
A) not listed
B) listed
C) a Union Government company
D) an unlimited liability company
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 171
EXP: The first character shows **listing status**: **L = listed**, **U = unlisted**. The Union Government ownership code is **GOI**, and unlimited liability companies use **ULL/ULT**.
SC: L for Listed, U for Unlisted.

Q: In the CIN ownership codes, "PTC" denotes:
A) Private limited Indian non-government company
B) Public limited Indian non-government company
C) Private limited foreign company incorporated in India
D) Public Trust Company
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 171
EXP: **PLC** = public limited Indian non-government, **PTC** = private limited Indian non-government, **FLC/FTC** = public/private foreign companies incorporated in India, **GOI** = Union Government company, **SGC** = State Government company, **NPL** = Section 25 (not-for-profit) company.
SC: The middle letter gives L for pubLic or T for privaTe. F at the start means foreign.

Q: In ASI Block B, the CIN is to be filled in only for units whose type of organisation code is:
A) 4, 5, 6 or 7 (companies)
B) 1 or 2
C) 8 only
D) 9 only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 172
EXP: Codes **4–7** are the company types (Government/Non-Government × Public/Private), and only companies have a CIN. Proprietorships (1), partnerships (2), LLPs (3), co-operatives (8) and others (9) do not.
SC: Only companies have a CIN, and companies are codes 4 to 7.

Q: ASI Block B asks whether the unit has ISO certification of which series?
A) ISO 14000
B) ISO 9000
C) ISO 27001
D) ISO 45001
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 172
EXP: Block B item 4 records **ISO 14000 series** (environmental management) certification only. ISO 9000 or other certificates do not count as "yes" here. Annexure X has a note on ISO 14000.
SC: ASI asks about the environmental ISO, which is the 14000 series.

Q: For ASI, "formal training" offered by a unit to its employees means structured vocational or technical training of at least:
A) 200 hours
B) 100 hours
C) 50 hours
D) 500 hours
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 173
EXP: Block B item 11 treats structured vocational or technical training of **at least 200 hours** for permanent or contractual employees as formal training at the workplace.
SC: Formal training threshold: 200 hours.

Q: In Block B, an R&D unit integrated with the factory and registered with which bodies gets code 1?
A) DST or DBT
B) CSIR or ICMR
C) DRDO
D) UGC
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 173
EXP: **Code 1**: R&D unit registered with **DST/DBT**. **Code 2**: registered with other agencies. **Code 3**: no R&D unit.
SC: DST/DBT registration gets code 1.

Q: For amalgamated accounts covering 18 months, ASI requires full values for all blocks EXCEPT:
A) mandays worked, mandays paid and absenteeism, which relate to the reference period only
B) fixed assets
C) products and by-products
D) input items
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 172
EXP: With amalgamated accounts (for example 18 months), "months of operation" is reported as **18** and financial blocks use **full values**. **Mandays worked, mandays paid and absenteeism** relate to the **reference period only**.
SC: Money figures cover all 18 months. Labour days cover the reference year only.

@topic Block C: Fixed Assets
Q: Under ASI, the value of a fixed asset that has completed its theoretical working life should be recorded as:
A) Re. 1
B) Zero
C) Its original cost
D) Its scrap value
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 173
EXP: A fully depreciated asset still in use is shown at **Re. 1** so that it stays on record. Depreciation is always computed on **original cost**, not on re-valued cost.
SC: A fully depreciated asset still in use is kept at a token Re. 1.

Q: Which of the following is EXCLUDED from fixed assets in ASI Block C?
A) Goodwill and preliminary expenses
B) Computer software
C) Assets taken on hire purchase
D) Major repairs that add to the working life of assets
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 174
EXP: **Intangibles such as goodwill and preliminary expenses**, advances for fixed assets not yet received, and copyright or mining lease rights in the balance sheet are **excluded**. Software, hire-purchase assets (full value, excluding interest) and major repairs are **included**.
SC: Software is the only intangible allowed.

Q: For a fixed asset under a financial lease, ASI records its full value in Block C of:
A) the lessee (economic owner)
B) the lessor (legal owner)
C) both lessor and lessee
D) neither
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 174
EXP: In a **financial lease**, the **lessee** is the economic owner, so the asset goes in the **lessee's Block C**. In an **operational lease**, ownership stays with the lessor and the lessee does not record it.
SC: Financial lease: the lessee records it. Operating lease: the lessor keeps it.

Q: ASI follows which approach for estimating total fixed assets in the factory sector?
A) Ownership approach (including owner-like possession such as long-term lease)
B) User approach only
C) Cash-flow approach
D) Replacement-cost approach
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 174
EXP: ASI uses the **ownership approach**. All assets owned and in the balance sheet are included, even if rented out, and so are **owner-like possessions** such as long-term leases. Rent received goes in **Block G**.
SC: If you own it (or effectively own it), record it, even if it is rented out.

Q: In ASI, long-term (perpetual) leasehold land is treated as:
A) owned land, with the lease amount recorded in Block C
B) rented land, with rent recorded in Block F
C) excluded entirely
D) a current asset in Block D
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 175
EXP: **Perpetual leasehold land** is treated like owned land (**Block C**). **Short-term** leasehold land is excluded from Block C, and only rent paid is recorded in **Block F**. Encroached land is recorded at market value. Land used for cultivation is excluded.
SC: Long lease counts as owned (Block C). Short lease counts as rent (Block F).

Q: A refrigerator used in a pharmaceutical factory to preserve medicines after manufacture is classified in ASI Block C as:
A) plant and machinery
B) other fixed assets
C) building
D) not a fixed asset
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 176
EXP: A refrigerator is normally not plant and machinery. But where it is **essential to the product** (medicines must be refrigerated), it counts as **plant and machinery**. Similarly, AC units fitted to machinery are P&M, while window ACs are "other fixed assets".
SC: If it is essential to production, it counts as P&M.

Q: A crane that does not form a part of plant and machinery is classified in ASI as:
A) transport equipment
B) plant and machinery
C) other fixed assets
D) building
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 176
EXP: A crane that is **part of the machinery** is **P&M**. **Otherwise it is transport equipment**. An animal-driven cart used for transport is also transport equipment, together with the animal.
SC: A crane in the machinery is P&M. A standalone crane is transport.

Q: Computers used to run plant and machinery or used as plant and machinery (e.g., in the printing industry) are recorded in ASI under:
A) plant and machinery
B) computer equipment including software
C) other fixed assets
D) consumable stores
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 177
EXP: Ordinary computers, UPS, printers and software go under **computer equipment (item 5)**. **Computers that run or act as machinery** go under **P&M**.
SC: Classify by function: a computer acting as a machine is P&M.

Q: Under the straight-line method of depreciation used in ASI, the rate of depreciation R is:
A) [(C − S)/n] / C × 100
B) (1 − (S/C)^(1/n)) × 100
C) (C − S) × n
D) S/C × 100
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 179
EXP: **SLM**: R = [(C − S)/n] ÷ C × 100, where C is original cost, S is scrap value and n is life in years. **WDV**: R = (1 − (S/C)^(1/n)) × 100, applied to the reducing balance. If no depreciation is provided, estimate it by SLM.
SC: SLM gives an equal slice each year. WDV applies a constant percentage to the balance, which uses the nth root.

Q: A machine costs ₹10 lakh, has a scrap value of ₹1 lakh and a working life of 9 years. The annual depreciation under the straight-line method is:
A) ₹1 lakh
B) ₹1.11 lakh
C) ₹0.9 lakh
D) ₹10 lakh
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
P: 179
EXP: SLM annual depreciation = (C − S)/n = (10 − 1)/9 = **₹1 lakh**. The rate on cost is 1/10 × 100 = 10%.
SC: Divide (cost − scrap) by life.

Q: If a company calculates depreciation under both the Companies Act, 2013 and the Income Tax Act, 1961, which is used for ASI?
A) Companies Act, 2013
B) Income Tax Act, 1961
C) Average of the two
D) Whichever is higher
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 180
EXP: ASI uses depreciation as per the **Companies Act, 2013**, i.e. the book depreciation. The Income Tax figure is only for tax returns.
SC: Book depreciation (Companies Act) is used, not tax depreciation.

Q: In ASI Block C, the positive net balance of capital work-in-progress (closing minus opening) is reflected in Block G as:
A) value of own construction
B) rent received
C) sale value of goods sold in same condition
D) electricity sold
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 178
EXP: A net increase in **CWIP** (Col 7 − Col 3 of item 9), if positive, is shown in **Block G, item 4, as own construction**, which is part of gross output.
SC: CWIP growth counts as own construction output.

@topic Block D: Working Capital & Loans
Q: Which of the following is included under "raw materials & components and packing materials" in ASI Block D?
A) Materials supplied by the factory to others for processing
B) Materials supplied by others to the factory for processing
C) Semi-finished fixed assets
D) Unused overdraft
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 180
EXP: Stocks the factory **owns**, including materials sent **to others** for processing, material in transit, trading goods and raw material scrap, are included. **Materials received from others for processing** are excluded.
SC: Ownership decides working capital, the same as for physical WC.

Q: In ASI, finished goods held in stock are valued at:
A) the ex-factory price (excluding taxes) at which goods were sold immediately prior to the reference date, else book value
B) retail market price including taxes
C) cost of raw materials only
D) the price expected next year
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 181
EXP: Finished goods are valued at the **selling price excluding excise and sales tax** just before the reference date. **Book value** is used if that price is not available.
SC: Value them at the recent ex-factory selling price.

Q: Which of the following is EXCLUDED from "other current assets" in ASI Block D?
A) Amount receivable for sale of fixed assets
B) Prepaid expenses
C) Advance income tax
D) Security deposits refundable within one year
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 182
EXP: Excluded items: unused overdraft, investments in private companies, trade investment, **receivables for sale of fixed assets**, long-term capital advances and long-term loans with interest. Prepaid expenses, advance tax, short-term deposits and bills receivable are included.
SC: Anything on capital account is excluded from current assets.

Q: If a negative entry appears in current assets in the balance sheet, ASI records it as:
A) a positive value under "other current liability"
B) a negative current asset
C) zero
D) a fixed asset
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 182
EXP: Current assets and current liabilities can **never be negative** in ASI. A negative current asset is moved to **other current liabilities** as a positive figure, and a negative liability is moved to other current assets.
SC: Flip the sign and move the item to the other side.

Q: A fixed deposit of any tenure that can be liquidated instantly (with or without penal charges) is recorded in ASI Block D under:
A) cash in hand and at bank
B) outstanding loans
C) other fixed assets
D) sundry debtors
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 181
EXP: Fixed deposits that can be **liquidated instantly** are treated as **cash in hand and at bank**, whatever their tenure. So are current and savings accounts, drafts in hand and uncashed cheques issued. Deposits with telephone or electricity departments are not current assets.
SC: If it can be withdrawn instantly, it counts as cash.

Q: Which of the following forms part of "outstanding loans" (Block D, item 17) in ASI?
A) Convertible and non-convertible debentures
B) Bank overdraft for six months
C) Cash credit
D) Sundry creditors
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 182
EXP: Outstanding loans are **long-term**. They include **all debentures** (convertible ones remain long-term liabilities until redemption), bank loans against fixed assets, loans from subsidiaries, fixed deposits from the public, and loans from directors (**excluding accrued interest**). **Overdrafts and cash credit** up to one year are short-term (item 13).
SC: Debentures are long-term. Overdraft and cash credit are short-term.

@topic Block E: Employment Coverage
Q: For ASI Block E, which persons are EXCLUDED?
A) Directors of incorporated enterprises paid solely for attending Board meetings
B) Working proprietors
C) Unpaid family workers
D) Active business partners
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 183
EXP: Block E covers everyone working in and for the establishment, including working proprietors, active partners and unpaid family workers. **Directors paid only for attending Board meetings are excluded**. Block E data also go to the **Labour Bureau**.
SC: A sitting-fee director is not an employee.

Q: In ASI Block E, which of the following is NOT counted as a "worker" even though it may be termed a worker under the Factories Act?
A) Persons in supervisory or managerial positions
B) Casual labour directly employed in manufacturing
C) Labour producing fixed assets for the factory's own use
D) Labour generating electricity for the factory
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 183
EXP: **Workers** include casual and regular staff directly employed in manufacturing and ancillary work, including repair, own-account fixed assets and power generation. **Supervisors, managers and confidential staff** and **unpaid** persons (working proprietors, family members) are **not workers**, although they are counted in total persons engaged.
SC: A worker is paid and non-supervisory.
