@topic Block E: Employment & Labour Cost
Q: In a highly automated factory where only "supervisors" work on the factory floor and are directly engaged in production, ASI treats them as:
A) workers, irrespective of their designation
B) supervisory staff
C) other employees
D) unpaid family members
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 184
EXP: If a person is **directly engaged in production**, they are treated as a **worker regardless of designation**. Clerks in planning, estimating and drawing offices, and gatekeepers guarding manufacturing areas, are also workers.
SC: What the person does decides the category, not the job title.

Q: Persons (not workers under the Factories Act) who are given material by the factory to work in their own homes are treated in ASI as follows:
A) not shown in Block E; payment to them is shown in Block F, item 1
B) shown as workers in Block E
C) shown as contract workers in Block E
D) excluded entirely from the schedule
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 184
EXP: **Home workers** are not reported in Block E. Their remuneration goes to **Block F, item 1** (work done by others on materials supplied by the factory).
SC: Home workers are treated as outsourced work (Block F-1), not as employees.

Q: Working proprietors, partners and family members are reported in Block E items 1–7 only when they are:
A) paid; otherwise they are reported against item 8
B) aged above 18
C) registered under the Factories Act
D) resident in the factory
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 184
EXP: Paid proprietors, partners and co-op members go in items 1–7. **Unpaid** ones go in **item 8 (unpaid family members/proprietor/co-op members)**. Dormant or inactive partners are excluded.
SC: Paid ones go in items 1-7. Unpaid active ones go in item 8.

Q: Which of the following is recorded against "bonus" (Block E, item 10) in ASI?
A) Profit-sharing, festival and year-end bonuses paid at less frequent intervals
B) Production bonus paid every pay period
C) Good attendance bonus paid monthly
D) Incentive bonus paid with each salary
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 185
EXP: **Item 10 (bonus)** covers **infrequent** bonuses such as profit sharing, festival, year-end and ex-gratia. **Regular** bonuses paid each pay period (production, attendance, incentive) are part of **wages/salary (column 8)**.
SC: Regular bonuses go in wages. Occasional bonuses go in the bonus item.

Q: Expenditure incurred by a factory on "labour welfare fund" is recorded in ASI Block E under:
A) contribution to provident fund and other funds (item 11)
B) workmen and staff welfare expenses (item 12)
C) wages/salary
D) Block F operating expenses
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 185
EXP: **Item 11** covers PF, pension, gratuity, ESI, work-injury compensation, retrenchment and lay-off benefits, **VRS payments** and **labour welfare fund**. **Item 12** covers welfare benefits in kind such as canteen, medical, creche, LTC and clothing other than uniforms.
SC: Payments into funds go in item 11. Benefits in kind go in item 12.

Q: House Rent Allowance (HRA) paid in cash to employees is treated in ASI as part of:
A) wages and salaries
B) workmen and staff welfare expenses
C) supplements to emoluments
D) operating expenses
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 185
EXP: The manual notes that **HRA is part of wages and salaries**. Welfare expenses (item 12) include cheap rations, meals, medical, LTC, children's education allowance and hired buses.
SC: Cash allowances (HRA, DA) count as wages.

Q: In ASI, "non-manufacturing days" include:
A) days on which only repair/maintenance and construction work was undertaken, as well as non-working days
B) Sundays and public holidays when the factory is closed
C) days of lock-out
D) days on which the factory produced output
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 185
EXP: **Manufacturing days** are days with actual production. **Non-manufacturing days** are days with only repair, maintenance or construction, plus **non-working days**, when the factory or office is open but no work is done because of a lack of materials or power.
SC: Open but not producing means a non-manufacturing day.

Q: For a factory with status of unit code 2 (existing with assets and staff but not producing), mandays in ASI Block E are reported under:
A) non-manufacturing days only
B) manufacturing days only
C) both, equally split
D) not reported
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 185
EXP: A status-2 unit has no production, so its **mandays go only under non-manufacturing days**.
SC: No production means no manufacturing days.

Q: In ASI Block E, the "average number of persons worked" is computed as:
A) total mandays worked ÷ number of working days
B) total mandays paid ÷ 365
C) number on rolls on the last day
D) mandays worked ÷ 12
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 185
EXP: **Average persons worked = total mandays worked (column 5) ÷ number of working days (item 13(iii))**.
SC: Person-days divided by days gives persons.

Q: For workers employed through contractors, the wages recorded in ASI Block E are:
A) the payment made to these workers; the difference between the payment to the contractor and the workers' payment goes to Block F, item 3
B) the entire payment made to the contractor
C) zero
D) the contractor's profit
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 186
EXP: Block E records **what the contract workers actually receive**. The **contractor's margin** (payment to contractor minus workers' dues) goes to **Block F, item 3 (operating expenses)**.
SC: The workers' share goes in Block E and the contractor's cut goes in Block F-3.

Q: Income tax of employees paid by the factory over and above their salary is treated in ASI as:
A) part of salaries/wages
B) operating expenses
C) excluded
D) welfare expenses
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 186
EXP: The manual says **income tax of employees paid by the owners over and above salary is included in salaries/wages**. Retaining allowances and cash allowances (car maintenance, garden upkeep) are included too.
SC: Tax paid on the employee's behalf is a perk, so it counts as salary.

@topic Block F: Other Expenses
Q: Kerosene oil used for cleaning machinery is recorded in ASI under:
A) repair and maintenance expenditure (Block F, item 2)
B) consumable stores (Block H)
C) fuel consumed
D) operating expenses
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 187
EXP: Kerosene used for cleaning machinery **helps keep the machinery in working condition**, so it goes under **repair and maintenance (F-2)**. Consumable stores help production directly.
SC: If it maintains the fixed assets, it is repair and maintenance. If it aids production, it is stores.

Q: Which of the following is included under "operating expenses" (Block F, item 3) in ASI?
A) Uniforms provided mandatorily to employees for working hours
B) Rent paid for buildings
C) Interest paid on loans
D) Insurance charges
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 188
EXP: Operating expenses (SG&A) include inward freight, rates and taxes (not income tax), communication, audit fees, bank charges, advertising, legal services, consultancy, payments to labour contractors, exchange fluctuation loss, key-man insurance and **mandatory uniforms**. Rent, interest and insurance have **separate items** in Block F.
SC: Anything without its own item (rent, interest, insurance, R&D) falls into operating expenses.

Q: Legal charges recorded under operating expenses in ASI EXCLUDE:
A) fees paid to Income Tax/Sales Tax practitioners
B) stamp papers
C) legal services rendered to the unit
D) filing fees
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 187
EXP: Fees to **Income Tax/Sales Tax practitioners** are **post-manufacturing expenses** and are excluded from legal charges in operating expenses. Stamp papers and other legal charges are included.
SC: Tax consultants' fees are post-manufacturing, so they are excluded.

Q: In ASI, labour cost incurred for own construction of fixed assets is recorded in:
A) Block E (not in Block F, item 4)
B) Block F, item 4
C) Block G, item 4
D) Block C
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 188
EXP: **Block F, item 4** records only **raw materials and components** used for own construction. **Labour cost** for own construction goes in **Block E**. Any F-4 entry must have a matching **G-4 (value of own construction)** entry.
SC: Materials go in F-4, labour goes in E, and output goes in G-4.

Q: Interest paid to partners and proprietors on their capital is:
A) not included in interest paid (Block F, item 10)
B) included in interest paid
C) included in operating expenses
D) included in rent paid
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 189
EXP: Block F-10 includes **all interest paid on loans** of the factory, of any duration. **Interest on partners' or proprietors' capital is excluded**, because it is a return to owners and not a cost.
SC: Paying interest to yourself is not a cost.

Q: A factory manufacturing cottonseed oil also buys and sells cotton as a subsidiary activity. The purchase value of cotton sold is recorded in:
A) Block F, item 11 (purchase value of goods sold in the same condition as purchased)
B) Block H (indigenous inputs)
C) Block J (products)
D) Block G, item 1
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 189
EXP: Goods **resold without transformation** are recorded at **purchase value in F-11** and at **sale value in G-11**. The **net balance (G-11 − F-11)** is entered in **G-5** and counts in gross output.
SC: Resold goods: purchase in F-11, sale in G-11, margin in G-5.

Q: Why is "inward transportation cost" (Block F, item 12) NOT added while calculating total input in ASI?
A) It is already included in operating expenses (F-3) or in the delivered value of raw materials, so adding it would double count
B) It is not a real cost
C) It is part of gross output
D) It is borne by suppliers
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 189
EXP: Inward freight is already inside **F-3** or in the **delivered value** of materials in Blocks H/I. **Items 12 and 13 (inward and outward transport)** are collected **separately** only to estimate transport costs explicitly.
SC: F-12 and F-13 are memo items for measuring transport, not for totals.

@topic Block G: Other Output & Receipts
Q: Which of the following is EXCLUDED from "receipts from manufacturing services" (Block G, item 1)?
A) Imputed value of free after-sales services during the warranty period for own products
B) Job work done for others on their materials
C) Repair and maintenance of others' machinery
D) Sale value of waste left by the party
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 190
EXP: G-1 covers **job work** and repair services **for others**. It excludes **free warranty service** on own products, repairs to **own** fixed assets, and servicing on own account.
SC: Only services sold to others count. Free or self-service does not.

Q: For a factory (not an electricity undertaking) that generates and sells electricity, the value of electricity sold is recorded in:
A) Block G, item 3
B) Block J
C) Block H, item 15
D) Block F, item 11
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 190
EXP: Non-electricity factories report electricity sold in **G-3** (book value for sister concerns, market value otherwise). **Electricity undertakings** report electricity generated in **Block J** as their product.
SC: For a factory, electricity is a side output (G-3). For a power plant it is the main product (J).

Q: The variation in stock of semi-finished goods reported in Block G, item 7 is derived from:
A) closing minus opening values of semi-finished goods in Block D, item 5
B) Block C, item 9
C) Block J, column 5
D) Block H, item 21
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 191
EXP: **G-7 = column 4 − column 3 of D-5** (closing minus opening WIP). It enters gross output as the net value of semi-finished goods.
SC: The change in WIP (from Block D) feeds output (Block G).

Q: Interest received on fixed deposits of any tenure is recorded in ASI in:
A) Block G, item 10 (interest received)
B) Block F, item 10
C) Block D
D) excluded
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 191
EXP: **All interest received** on loans given by the factory is recorded in **G-10**, including **FD interest**, because FDs of any tenure are now treated as current assets.
SC: Interest received goes in G-10 and interest paid goes in F-10.

Q: A subsidy received from Government for employing physically handicapped persons or installing pollution control equipment is recorded in ASI as:
A) other production subsidies (Block G, item 12)
B) product subsidy in Block J
C) receipts from non-manufacturing services
D) reduction in wages
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 192
EXP: **Production subsidies** that are not tied to output units (disabled employment, pollution control, worker training) go in **G-12**. **Product subsidies** (per unit of output) go in **Block J**.
SC: Production subsidy goes in G-12. Product subsidy goes in Block J. This matches the national accounts split.

Q: The sale value of scrap and discarded material is recorded in ASI under:
A) sale value of goods sold in the same condition as purchased (Block G, item 11)
B) by-products in Block J
C) receipts from manufacturing services
D) other production subsidies
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 191
EXP: Scrap and discarded material are **generally not by-products** and are sold as they are, so their sale value may be included in **G-11**.
SC: Scrap is not a by-product. It is recorded as goods sold in the same condition.

@topic Blocks H, I & J: Inputs and Products
Q: In a cotton textile mill, yarn produced and then used for making cloth in the same mill is treated in ASI as:
A) an intermediate product, excluded from inputs consumed
B) a basic material in Block H
C) a product in Block J
D) a consumable store
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 192
EXP: Yarn produced and **consumed internally** is an **intermediate product**, so it is excluded from Block H. The same yarn becomes a **final product** in Block J if it is **sold**.
SC: Made and used inside is intermediate. Made and sold is final.

Q: Inputs in ASI Block H are valued at:
A) delivered value, i.e., purchase value plus transport, commission and taxes paid
B) ex-factory value of the supplier
C) market retail price
D) cost of production of the supplier
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 192
EXP: Inputs are valued at **delivered value**: purchase price + **transport to factory** + purchasing agents' commission + **taxes and duties**. Transfers from sister concerns use book value.
SC: Inputs are valued at the factory gate, i.e. delivered value.

Q: A cement factory obtains limestone from a leased quarry. The purchase value of limestone for Block H is taken as:
A) the annual lease money paid plus transport and incidental charges
B) market price of limestone
C) zero
D) royalty only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 192
EXP: For materials that are **not purchased** (for example from leased mines), the purchase value = **cost of production = annual lease money + transport + incidental charges**.
SC: Self-sourced inputs are valued at the cost of getting them.

Q: If electricity is generated from purchased water, the water is treated in ASI as:
A) a basic input in Block H (quantity and value)
B) consumable store
C) welfare expense
D) not recorded
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 193
EXP: **Purchased water** used to generate electricity is a **basic input** in Block H. Water taken **free from natural sources** is not recorded. **Drinking water** for staff is a **welfare expense** (E-12). Water for washing is a **consumable store** (H-21).
SC: Classify water by its use: basic input, stores or welfare.

Q: From ASI 2015-16 onwards, items in Blocks H, I and J are classified using:
A) the revised NPCMS-2011 (7-digit, based on CPC Ver. 2)
B) NIC-2008 5-digit
C) HS 8-digit
D) ASICC
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 193
EXP: The **revised NPCMS-2011** is used for input and output items. It is a 7-digit product classification based on **CPC Ver. 2**, with generic (not brand) descriptions.
SC: Items use NPCMS (products). Units use NIC (industry).

Q: "Additional requirement of electricity (unmet demand)" in ASI Block H is computed as:
A) total electricity requirement − (own generated + purchased and consumed)
B) purchased − own generated
C) own generated × 2
D) total requirement ÷ purchased
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 194
EXP: **Unmet demand = total requirement − (H-15 own generated + H-16 purchased and consumed)**. It is used to estimate production lost because of power shortages.
SC: Demand minus what was actually available.

Q: If a factory directly imports packing material, it is recorded in:
A) Block I (imported inputs), not Block H
B) Block H, item 14
C) Block F
D) Block J
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 194
EXP: **Any directly imported item**, whether basic material, consumable stores or packing, goes in **Block I**. Block H is for indigenous inputs only.
SC: If it is imported, it goes in Block I.

Q: In ASI Block J, gross sale value includes:
A) excise duty/GST paid and distributive expenses like discounts, outward carriage and selling commission
B) only the net amount received after discounts
C) only the cost of production
D) subsidies received
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 195
EXP: **Gross sale value** is what is charged to customers, **including GST or excise** and **distributive expenses** (discounts, rebates, outward carriage, selling agents' commission).
SC: Gross sale value is the full invoice amount including taxes and distribution costs.

Q: Per unit net sale value in ASI Block J is computed as:
A) [Gross sale value − (GST + other taxes + other distributive expenses − subsidy)] ÷ quantity sold
B) Gross sale value ÷ quantity manufactured
C) (Gross sale value + subsidy) ÷ quantity manufactured
D) Ex-factory value ÷ quantity sold
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 196
EXP: Per unit net sale value = **[col 7 − (col 8 + col 9 + col 10 − col 11)] ÷ col 6 (quantity sold)**, to two decimals. **Ex-factory value = per unit NSV × quantity manufactured (col 5)**.
SC: Remove taxes and distribution costs, add back the subsidy, then divide by quantity sold. Multiply by quantity made for ex-factory value.

Q: A product has gross sale value ₹1,200, GST ₹180, other distributive expenses ₹60, subsidy ₹40, quantity sold 10 units and quantity manufactured 12 units. The ex-factory value of output is:
A) ₹1,200
B) ₹1,000
C) ₹1,440
D) ₹960
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Hard
P: 196
EXP: Net sale value = 1,200 − (180 + 60 − 40) = 1,000. Per unit NSV = 1,000 ÷ 10 = ₹100. **Ex-factory value = 100 × 12 (manufactured) = ₹1,200**.
SC: Price per unit comes from units sold. Value comes from units made.

Q: If a factory produced an item during the year but did not sell any quantity of it, its ex-factory value in Block J is calculated at:
A) the cost price of producing the item
B) zero
C) the last year's sale price
D) the market price of competitors
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 197
EXP: If there were **no sales** of an item produced this year, output is valued **at cost of production**. If the item was **not produced** this year but sold from old stock, its sale value is **not counted**.
SC: Produced but unsold is valued at cost. Sold but not produced is not counted.

Q: Export insurance charges, if paid, are treated in ASI as:
A) part of distributive expenses in Block J
B) insurance charges in Block F, item 5
C) operating expenses
D) welfare expenses
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 196
EXP: **Export insurance** is part of **distributive expenses (Block J, col 10)**, not the general insurance charges in Block F.
SC: Insurance on goods sold (in transit or for export) is a distribution cost.

@topic Blocks K, L, Scrutiny & Part-II
Q: For ICT usage in ASI Block K, "narrowband" Internet access refers to advertised download speeds of less than:
A) 256 kbit/s
B) 2 Mbit/s
C) 64 kbit/s
D) 10 Mbit/s
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 198
EXP: **Narrowband** means below **256 kbit/s** (dial-up, ISDN, slow DSL, GPRS/WAP). **Fixed broadband** means DSL at 256 kbit/s or more, cable, fibre, leased lines and so on. **Mobile broadband** means W-CDMA/UMTS, HSDPA and similar.
SC: The narrowband/broadband cut-off is 256 kbit/s.

Q: In the ASI scrutiny sheet (Part A), the Scrutinising Officer must ensure that Gross Value Added calculated by the gross sale value approach tallies with GVA calculated by:
A) the ex-factory value approach
B) the income approach
C) the expenditure approach
D) the input-output approach
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 199
EXP: Scrutiny Part A, serial 4: **GVA by the gross sale value approach (serial 9)** must **tally** with **GVA by the ex-factory value approach (serial 14)**.
SC: Two routes to the same GVA must agree.

Q: Labour Bureau's reports based on ASI Part-II meet India's statutory obligation under Article 11 of Part-II of which ILO Convention?
A) Convention No. 160 on Labour Statistics, ratified by India on 1.4.1992
B) Convention No. 81 on Labour Inspection
C) Convention No. 144 on Tripartite Consultation
D) Convention No. 138 on Minimum Age
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 200
EXP: Labour Bureau reports (2 volumes) on **absenteeism, labour turnover, employment and labour cost** meet **Article 11, Part-II of ILO Convention No. 160 (Labour Statistics)**, which India ratified on **01.04.1992**.
SC: ILO C-160 is the labour statistics convention.

Q: The scope of ASI Part-II Block 2 (absenteeism and labour turnover) is limited to:
A) regular workers directly employed (permanent, probationers and temporary)
B) all workers including casual, badli and contract workers
C) supervisory staff only
D) apprentices only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 200
EXP: Block 2 covers **regular workers directly employed**. **Casual, badli (substitute) and contract workers** and **apprentices** are excluded. In tea factories, only regular factory-side workers are covered.
SC: Absenteeism and turnover are measured for regular direct workers only.

Q: In ASI Part-II, a worker who reports for duty for part of a shift is counted as:
A) having worked the full shift (not absent)
B) absent
C) half present
D) on leave
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 201
EXP: Anyone attending **even part of a shift** counts as having **worked the full shift** and is not absent. The manual's example: 50 reported in the morning and 5 left mid-shift, so attendance is 50.
SC: Partial attendance counts as full attendance for mandays.

Q: For absenteeism statistics in ASI Part-II, which of the following is NOT counted as absence?
A) Absence due to strike, lock-out, lay-off or weekly rest
B) Absence with prior permission
C) Absence without pay
D) Absence with pay
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 202
EXP: Absence means failing to report when **scheduled to work**, and it **includes** absence with prior permission, with or without pay. **Strikes, lock-outs, lay-offs, weekly rest and suspension are not absence**, because the worker was not scheduled to work.
SC: Absence counts only if the worker was scheduled to work.

Q: In ASI Part-II, separations due to death or retirement are recorded in column ___ and those due to other causes in column ___.
A) 8; 9
B) 9; 8
C) 7; 8
D) 5; 6
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 202
EXP: **Column 7 = accessions**, **column 8 = separations due to death or retirement**, **column 9 = separations for other reasons** (resignation, dismissal, transfer to another establishment, retrenchment).
SC: Column 7 is people joining, 8 is natural exits, 9 is all other exits.

@topic General Rules & Head Office
Q: A jail workshop registered as a factory, whose products are incidental to training of convicts and are not sold, is:
A) outside the purview of ASI
B) covered under ASI like any factory
C) covered under the census scheme
D) covered only in Part-II
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 203
EXP: Workshops in jails and training centres whose products have **no sale value** and are incidental to training are **outside ASI**. The facts are reported to the Statistics Authority. They are covered only if the products are **meant for sale**.
SC: No sale means no ASI coverage.

Q: If a head office controls factories in different States, its information in ASI is accounted for in:
A) the return of the factory situated in the same State as the head office
B) all factories, allocated proportionately
C) none of the returns
D) the largest factory, irrespective of State
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 204
EXP: Head office data go into **one factory in the same State**. If the HO is **outside the State** of its factories, its data are **not included**. HO data are **never allocated** across units, unless the HO itself distributes expenses, in which case the unit's share goes in Block F.
SC: The HO goes with a factory in the same State, is never split, and is dropped if it is out of State.

Q: If a factory as a whole has been rented out, the ASI return is filled from the point of view of:
A) the occupier
B) the owner
C) the CIF
D) the landlord's bank
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 203
EXP: If the whole factory is rented out, the return is filled **from the occupier's point of view**. If part is run by the owner and part by the occupier, **one return** covers both.
SC: Whoever runs production reports it.
