@topic NIC Coverage & Tabulation Plan
Q: Among agricultural activities, which NIC-2008 codes are covered under ASI as per Annexure VII?
A) 0163 (post-harvest crop activities) and 0164 (seed processing for propagation)
B) 0111 (growing of cereals) and 0112 (growing of rice)
C) 0141 (raising of cattle) and 0145 (raising of swine)
D) 0210 (silviculture) and 0220 (logging)
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 264
EXP: ASI covers some processing-type activities outside Section C: **0163 post-harvest crop activities**, **0164 seed processing (only 01640)** and **0893 salt extraction (only 08932)**. Crop growing and livestock rearing are not covered.
SC: Processing near agriculture is covered. Growing crops and raising animals are not.

Q: Which of the following service activities is covered under ASI as per the list of NIC-2008 codes?
A) 9601: Washing and (dry-)cleaning of textile and fur products
B) 4711: Retail sale in non-specialised stores
C) 6419: Other monetary intermediation
D) 8510: Pre-primary and primary education
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 268
EXP: Besides manufacturing, ASI covers certain **repair and processing services**: motion picture and sound recording (5911–5920), packaging, **repair of computers and household goods (95xx)** and **washing and dry-cleaning (9601)**. Retail trade, finance and education are outside ASI.
SC: Repair and processing services are in. Trade, finance and education are out.

Q: As per the ASI tabulation plan, "Invested Capital" is computed as:
A) Fixed Capital + Physical Working Capital
B) Fixed Capital + Working Capital
C) Working Capital − Outstanding loans
D) Gross value of P&M + Physical working capital
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 269
EXP: Tabulation serial 6: **Invested capital = serial 3 (fixed capital, closing net value of items except 8) + serial 4 (physical working capital, D items 1–6 except 4)**. Working capital is serial 5.
SC: Invested = fixed + physical WC, as in the concepts chapter.

Q: In the ASI tabulation plan, Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) is derived as:
A) Net Fixed Capital Formation + Depreciation
B) Gross value of additions − Depreciation
C) Fixed capital (closing) − Fixed capital (opening)
D) Total output − Total input
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 270
EXP: **NFCF** is built from the change in net fixed assets (closing net value − opening net value, leaving out revaluation). **GFCF = NFCF + depreciation**. **GCF = GFCF + addition in stock** (materials, semi-finished and finished goods).
SC: GFCF = NFCF + depreciation. GCF = GFCF + change in stocks.

Q: In the ASI tabulation plan, "Gross Capital Formation" equals:
A) GFCF + addition in stocks of materials/fuels, semi-finished goods and finished goods
B) GFCF − depreciation
C) Invested capital − working capital
D) NVA + depreciation
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 270
EXP: **GCF = GFCF + total addition in stocks**, where the stock change is (D1–3 closing − opening) + (D5 closing − opening) + (D6 closing − opening). This matches the national accounts definition of GCF as fixed capital formation plus change in inventories.
SC: GCF = fixed capital formation + inventory change.

Q: For the ASI tabulation "Number of factories", units with which status codes are counted?
A) 1, 2, 3 and 4
B) 1, 2 and 3 only
C) 1 only
D) 5, 7 and 8
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 271
EXP: "**No. of factories**" counts status codes **1–4**. "**Factories in operation**" and all other parameters count codes **1–3**.
SC: Factory count uses codes 1-4. Everything else uses codes 1-3.

Q: In the ASI tabulation plan, "Fuels consumed" is the sum of which Block H items?
A) Electricity own generated, electricity purchased, petroleum products, coal, gas and other fuel
B) Basic materials only
C) Consumable stores and packing
D) Non-basic chemicals
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 269
EXP: Fuels consumed = **H15 (own electricity) + H16 (purchased electricity) + H17 (petroleum) + H18 (coal) + H19 (gas) + H20 (other fuel)**. Materials consumed covers basic items, non-basic chemicals, packing, consumable stores and imported items.
SC: Fuel covers every energy item in H15-H20.

@topic Status Codes & ISO 14000
Q: As per Annexure IX, units with status codes 1, 2 and 3 are treated in calculation of weights as:
A) surveyed cases
B) casualties
C) zero cases
D) excluded from frame
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 272
EXP: Codes **1 (open), 2 (assets and staff, no production) and 3 (assets, no staff, no production)** are **surveyed cases**. Code **4** is a **zero case**. Codes **5, 7, 8** are **casualties**.
SC: 1-3 surveyed, 4 zero, 5/7/8 casualties.

Q: Which standard of the ISO 14000 family specifies requirements for an Environmental Management System (EMS)?
A) ISO 14001
B) ISO 14064
C) ISO 14040
D) ISO 9001
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 273
EXP: **ISO 14001:2015** is the core **EMS** standard. It aims at better environmental performance, meeting compliance obligations and achieving environmental objectives, and it applies to any organisation. It does **not** set specific performance criteria.
SC: 14001 is the EMS standard, just as 9001 is the QMS standard.

Q: The ISO 14040 series of standards deals with:
A) Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)
B) Greenhouse gas quantification
C) Environmental labels and declarations
D) Material flow cost accounting
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 274
EXP: **ISO 14040–14049** cover Life Cycle Assessment. **14020–14025** cover environmental labels. **14064–14069** cover greenhouse gases (14067 is the carbon footprint of products). **14051** is material flow cost accounting. **14031** is environmental performance evaluation.
SC: 14020s labels, 14040s LCA, 14060s GHG.

Q: ISO/TS 14067 relates to:
A) Carbon footprint of products
B) Eco-design
C) Environmental communication
D) Site assessment
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 274
EXP: **ISO/TS 14067:2013** covers the **carbon footprint of products** (quantification and communication). 14006 covers eco-design, 14063 environmental communication and 14015 environmental site assessment.
SC: 1406x is the GHG family, and 14067 is the product carbon footprint.

@topic ASI FAQs: Frame, JR & Accounts
Q: If a unit is registered and exists in the CIF list but is not in the ASI frame for a year, the ASI FAQ says it should be:
A) included in the frame as and when detected
B) ignored until the next Economic Census
C) treated as a casualty
D) deleted
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 275
EXP: FAQ 1: **yes, include it** in the frame for that year once it is detected, provided it is registered and in the CIF list.
SC: A registered and existing unit belongs in the frame.

Q: Two units recorded as a joint unit (as per DISH/CIF) are located in two different ROs of FOD but within the same State. As per the ASI FAQ:
A) a single joint return is to be filled in, taking all information together
B) two separate returns must be filled in
C) the units are excluded
D) each RO files half the data
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 275
EXP: FAQ 5: as long as the units are **within one State**, it is a **joint return**, and a **single return** covers both even if they fall in different ROs.
SC: JR is decided at the State level, not the RO level.

Q: If a factory prepares balance sheets under both the Companies Act and the Income Tax Act, which is used for ASI?
A) The one prepared under the Companies Act
B) The one prepared under the Income Tax Act
C) An average of both
D) Whichever shows higher output
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 276
EXP: FAQ 8: the **Companies Act** balance sheet is used, consistent with using Companies Act depreciation (FAQ 20).
SC: ASI uses the company books, not the tax books.

Q: If a unit's balance sheet covers an 18-month period, from ASI 2010-11 the apportioning to 12 months is done by:
A) the IS Wing, DQAD only
B) the SSO in the field
C) the factory owner
D) the Labour Bureau
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 276
EXP: FAQ 10: the information is apportioned to 12 months. **From ASI 2010-11, apportioning is done only by the IS Wing, DQAD**, for uniformity. The field reports the full 18-month figures.
SC: The field reports full figures and the IS Wing apportions.

Q: A sugar factory registered with the CIF has a captive power plant whose electricity sold in one year exceeds the value of sugar. As per the ASI FAQ, for that year:
A) the industry code is not changed; electricity sold is recorded in Block G, item 3
B) the industry code is changed to electricity generation immediately
C) the factory is deleted
D) electricity is recorded in Block H
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 276
EXP: FAQ 12: an incidental excess in **one year does not change** the industry. Electricity sold goes to **G-3**. If the unit is **registered as a power unit with the CIF (not the CEA)** or sells electricity **significantly over years**, the code changes and electricity is recorded in **Block J**.
SC: A one-year spike is a side output (G-3). A persistent pattern changes the industry code.

Q: As per the ASI FAQ, long-term leasehold land for a period of at least how many years is treated as owned land?
A) 30 years or more
B) 10 years or more
C) 99 years only
D) 5 years or more
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 277
EXP: FAQ 16: land is not depreciated, only depleted (mines, timber land). **Leasehold land of 30 years or more** is treated as **owned** (owner-like possession), and the lease amount is shown as the value of land in Block C.
SC: A lease of 30+ years counts as ownership.

Q: As per the ASI FAQ, a fire extinguisher vehicle is recorded under:
A) other fixed assets (Block C, item 7), not transport equipment
B) transport equipment (Block C, item 4)
C) plant & machinery
D) operating expenses
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 278
EXP: FAQ 19: a **fire extinguisher vehicle** goes under **other fixed assets (C-7)**, because its purpose is safety, not transport.
SC: Classify by function: a safety asset goes to C-7.

Q: A windmill not registered with the CIF or CEA, owned by a factory and selling electricity to the SEB, is treated in ASI as:
A) a captive plant and integral part of the factory, recorded in Block C, with electricity sale value in Block G, item 3
B) a separate electricity undertaking
C) outside the purview of ASI
D) a current asset
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 279
EXP: FAQ 22: if it is registered with **neither CIF nor CEA**, the windmill is a **captive plant** and **part of the factory (Block C)**. Electricity sold goes to **G-3**.
SC: An unregistered windmill is a captive plant, so it is a factory asset.

Q: "Amortization", as explained in the ASI FAQ, refers to:
A) depreciation applied to intangible assets
B) repayment of loans in instalments only
C) revaluation of land
D) write-off of bad debts
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 279
EXP: FAQ 23: **amortization** is spreading the capital cost of **intangible assets** over time, i.e. depreciation applied to intangibles.
SC: Depreciation is for tangibles, amortization for intangibles.

Q: Raw material in transit for which payment has NOT been made is treated in ASI as:
A) not considered at all
B) Block D, item 1
C) Block D, item 10
D) Block H
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 279
EXP: FAQ 27: raw material in transit that has been **paid for** goes to **D-1**. If it has **not been paid for**, it is **not considered**, because the factory does not own it yet.
SC: Paid means owned, so it is counted. Unpaid is not counted.

Q: As per ASI, doubtful debts (not yet written off) are:
A) included in current assets
B) excluded from current assets
C) recorded as bad debts
D) recorded as liabilities
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 280
EXP: FAQ 29: **all debts are current assets unless written off as bad debts**. Doubtful debts stay in current assets.
SC: Only written-off bad debts are excluded.

@topic ASI FAQs: Labour & Expenses
Q: A salesman stationed elsewhere but paid by the factory producing medicines is treated in ASI as:
A) other employee in Block E, with salary in wages/salaries
B) distributive expense in Block J
C) operating expense in Block F
D) outside the purview
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 280
EXP: FAQ 34: he is an **'other employee' in Block E** and his salary goes to **wages/salaries (E col. 8)**, even though some balance sheets show it as a distributive expense.
SC: An employee is an employee wherever posted, so he goes in Block E.

Q: Fringe Benefit Tax (FBT) paid by the employer is treated in ASI as:
A) a component of wages and salary in Block E
B) operating expense in Block F
C) outside the purview of ASI
D) distributive expense
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 281
EXP: FAQ 38: FBT is treated like income tax. If **paid by the employer**, it forms part of **wages and salary**. If paid by the employee, it is outside ASI.
SC: Taxes on employees paid by the employer count as salary.

Q: In ASI Block E, apprentices and probation employees are classified respectively as:
A) casual employees and regular employees
B) regular employees and casual employees
C) both as regular employees
D) both excluded
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 284
EXP: FAQ 62: an **apprentice** is a **casual** employee and a **probationer** is a **regular** employee. (Apprentices are excluded from Part-II absenteeism.)
SC: Apprentices are casual. Probationers are regular.

Q: Penalty paid for late submission of licence fee is treated in ASI as:
A) not considered, since fines are excluded; this affects profit but not GVA
B) operating expense
C) interest paid
D) rent paid
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 282
EXP: FAQ 45: **penalties and fines are not considered** for ASI. Leaving them out changes **profit** but **not GVA**, because fines are not inputs.
SC: Fines are transfers, not production costs.

Q: If a factory pays charges for using a common Effluent Treatment Plant (ETP) in its locality, the charges are recorded in ASI under:
A) operating expenses (Block F, item 3)
B) pollution control equipment (Block C, item 6)
C) repair and maintenance
D) welfare expenses
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 287
EXP: FAQ 75: a factory-owned plant that treats **effluent** is **pollution control equipment (C-6)**. One that treats **fresh water for production** is **P&M (C-3)**. Charges paid for a **common ETP** (not owned) are **operating expenses (F-3)**.
SC: Own ETP goes to C-6, own fresh-water plant to C-3, shared ETP charges to F-3.

Q: As per the ASI FAQ, royalty paid for a trademark and logo is recorded under:
A) Block F, item 3 (Operating expenses)
B) Block F, item 9 (Royalties on mines)
C) Block C (intangible assets)
D) Block G
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 284
EXP: FAQ 64: **royalty on trademark or logo** is an **operating expense (F-3)**. **F-9** is only for rent on leased land and **royalties on mines and quarries**.
SC: Brand royalty goes to F-3. Mining royalty goes to F-9.

Q: Transport cost of finished products from the unit to its own godown is recorded under ___, while transport from godown to distributors is recorded under ___.
A) Block F, item 3 (operating expenses); Block J (distributive expenses)
B) Block J; Block F, item 3
C) Block F, item 12; Block F, item 13
D) Block H; Block I
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 285
EXP: FAQ 65: **unit to own godown** is an internal movement, so it is an **operating expense (F-3)**. **Godown to distributors, stockists and retailers** is a **distributive expense (Block J)**.
SC: Internal transfers go to F-3. Delivery to buyers goes to J.

Q: Inward and outward transportation cost were introduced as separate items (Block F, items 12 and 13) in ASI from:
A) 2019-20
B) 2010-11
C) 2015-16
D) 2012-13
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 286
EXP: FAQ 69: **F-12 and F-13 were introduced from ASI 2019-20** to estimate **transport and logistics cost** explicitly. They are **not used** in input or output calculations, to avoid double counting.
SC: Transport memo items were added in 2019-20.

Q: As per the ASI FAQ (citing Section 44AB of the Income Tax Act), a proprietary firm must get its accounts audited if its total sales/turnover exceeds:

EXP: FAQ 68 cites **Section 44AB of the Income Tax Act**: a tax audit is required if turnover exceeds **₹1 crore**. That was the threshold in the source; later amendments raised limits for mostly digital transactions.

Q: A unit closed for 3 years and then not in production for another 3 years is to be:

EXP: FAQ 72: with **no manufacturing for 6 years**, the unit is **deleted (04)**. It can be re-added to the frame if it becomes operational later.

Q: For units with status of unit code 3, the number of months of operation (Block B, item 7) is recorded as:
A) 0

EXP: FAQ 57: code 3 units have no production and no staff, so **months of operation = 0**.

@topic Accounting Note & Ind AS
Q: A business entity produces which three financial statements at the end of the accounting year, as per the ASI accounting note?
A) Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss Account and Cash Flow Statement
B) Trial Balance, Journal and Ledger
C) Balance Sheet, Budget and Audit Report
D) Income Tax Return, GST Return and TDS Return
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 288
EXP: The three financial statements are the **Balance Sheet** (position on the closing day), the **P&L Account** (performance) and the **Cash Flow Statement**. The journal, ledger and trial balance are books and working statements.
SC: The statements are BS, P&L and Cash Flow.

Q: The fundamental accounting equation on which the Balance Sheet is based is:
A) Assets = Owners' Capital + Liabilities
B) Assets = Liabilities − Capital
C) Capital = Assets + Liabilities
D) Income = Expenses + Assets
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 288
EXP: **Assets (uses of funds) = Owners' capital + Liabilities (sources of funds)**. Every double entry keeps this balance.
SC: Uses of funds = sources of funds.

Q: As per the rules of debit and credit for a Nominal Account:
A) Expenses/losses are debited and incomes/gains are credited
B) What comes in is debited and what goes out is credited
C) The receiver is debited and the giver is credited
D) All entries are credited
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 289
EXP: **Real account** (assets): debit what comes in, credit what goes out. **Personal account**: debit the receiver, credit the giver. **Nominal account**: **debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains**.
SC: Real = in/out, Personal = receiver/giver, Nominal = expense/income.

Q: A debit entry broadly means which of the following?
A) Increase in assets or expenses, or decrease in liabilities or owner's equity
B) Increase in liabilities
C) Increase in sales revenue
D) Increase in owner's capital
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 289
EXP: **Debit**: increase in assets, expenses or dividends, or decrease in liabilities, equity or revenue. **Credit** is the reverse.
SC: DEAD CLIC: Debits increase Expenses, Assets and Drawings. Credits increase Liabilities, Income and Capital.

Q: Which is the correct sequence in the accounting cycle as per the ASI note?
A) Journal entry → Ledger posting → Trial Balance → Rectification → Balance Sheet and P&L
B) Trial Balance → Journal → Ledger → Balance Sheet
C) Ledger → Journal → Balance Sheet → Trial Balance
D) Balance Sheet → Trial Balance → Ledger → Journal
ANS: A
T: Chronology
D: Easy
P: 290
EXP: Transactions → (T-accounts) → **journal** (chronological, with narration) → **ledger** posting → **trial balance** → check and rectify errors → **Balance Sheet and P&L**.
SC: Journal (by date) → Ledger (by account) → Trial Balance (check) → Final accounts.

Q: Which of the following is a limitation of the Trial Balance?
A) Even a tallied Trial Balance does not ensure that all transactions have been correctly recorded and posted
B) It is part of the books of account
C) It is an account, not a statement
D) Debit and credit totals never match
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 292
EXP: A trial balance is a **statement**, not an account, and not part of the books. It checks **arithmetical accuracy only**. Errors such as omission, compensating errors or errors of principle can remain even when it tallies.
SC: A tallied trial balance is only prima facie evidence of accuracy.

Q: "Operating expenses" in the accounting sense refer to:
A) period costs incurred during the accounting period irrespective of profit or loss, as distinct from product costs
B) direct materials only
C) direct labour only
D) capital expenditure
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 293
EXP: **Product costs** are direct materials, direct labour and manufacturing overhead, and they are inventoried. **Operating expenses are period costs**, charged to the period regardless of profit or loss.
SC: Product costs go into inventory. Period costs go to the period.

Q: Which items of the Balance Sheet are EXCLUDED from the ASI schedule?
A) Share capital and reserves & surplus (owner's equity), non-current assets other than fixed capital, and non-current liabilities other than outstanding loans
B) Fixed assets
C) Inventories
D) Current liabilities
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 294
EXP: ASI uses BS items only in **Blocks C and D**: fixed assets, inventory, other current assets, current liabilities and **outstanding loans**. **Owner's equity** and other non-current items are excluded.
SC: ASI tracks production capital, not ownership or financing structure.

Q: Under Ind AS 116 (Right of Use assets), for a lessee factory, which leased-in assets are reported in ASI Block C?
A) Only assets under financial lease
B) Only assets under operating lease
C) Both financial and operating leases
D) Neither
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 295
EXP: **Ind AS 116** (replacing AS 17) puts both lease types on the balance sheet as ROU assets. **ASI still records only financial-lease ROU assets** in the lessee's Block C. Depreciation on operating-lease ROU assets and all lease expenses go to **Block F**.
SC: ASI keeps the economic ownership test: financial lease belongs to the lessee.

Q: Under Ind AS 40 (Investment Properties), for a lessor factory, which properties are reported in Block C?
A) All owned assets, including operationally leased-out properties, but not assets under financial lease
B) Only assets under financial lease
C) No investment properties
D) Only land
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 295
EXP: For **lessors**, all **owned** assets, including those held for appreciation or future use and **operationally leased-out** properties, are in Block C. Assets **given on financial lease** are **not** in the lessor's Block C, because the lessee is the economic owner.
SC: The lessor keeps operating-lease assets and gives up financial-lease assets.
