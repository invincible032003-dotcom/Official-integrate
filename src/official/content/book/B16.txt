@topic Natural Capital & NCA
Q: The term "natural capital" is attributed to economist E.F. Schumacher, who presented the concept in his 1973 book:
A) Small is Beautiful
B) The Limits to Growth
C) Silent Spring
D) Our Common Future
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 297
EXP: **E.F. Schumacher** introduced the idea in **"Small is Beautiful" (1973)**. The Limits to Growth (1972) was the Club of Rome report, Silent Spring (1962) was by Rachel Carson, and Our Common Future (1987) is the Brundtland Report.
SC: Schumacher → Small is Beautiful → natural capital (1973).

Q: Natural capital is generally considered to comprise which three principal categories?
A) Natural resource stocks, land and ecosystems
B) Minerals, energy and money
C) Forests, rivers and cities
D) Labour, capital and technology
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 297
EXP: As a natural asset providing resource inputs and environmental services, natural capital comprises **natural resource stocks, land and ecosystems**. It includes "invisible" ecosystem services such as pollination, flood protection and carbon storage.
SC: Resources + Land + Ecosystems.

Q: Which of the following statements about Natural Capital Accounts (NCA) vis-à-vis National Accounts is correct?
A) NCA follow SEEA and go beyond the SNA production boundary to cover non-market natural goods and services
B) NCA follow the SNA and are limited to the production boundary
C) NCA are compiled only in monetary terms
D) NCA replace GDP
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 299
EXP: **NCA follow the SEEA** (approved by the UN Statistical Commission). National accounts follow the **SNA** and stay **within the production boundary**. NCA go further and cover natural goods and services that are not traded, in **physical and monetary** terms. They complement GDP rather than replace it.
SC: SEEA goes beyond the SNA production boundary, in both physical and monetary terms.

@topic SEEA Framework
Q: The SEEA Central Framework was adopted by the UN Statistical Commission as the first international standard for environmental-economic accounting in:
A) 2012
B) 2008
C) 2014
D) 2021
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 300
EXP: **SEEA-CF was adopted in 2012**. **SEEA Ecosystem Accounting** was adopted at the **52nd session in March 2021**. SNA 2008 is the national accounts standard.
SC: CF 2012, EA 2021 (52nd UNSC).

Q: The SEEA Central Framework covers measurement in which three main areas?
A) Environmental flows, stocks of environmental assets and economic activity related to the environment
B) Biodiversity, climate and oceans
C) GDP, employment and prices
D) Ecosystem extent, condition and services
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 300
EXP: SEEA-CF covers **(a) environmental flows**, **(b) stocks of environmental assets** and **(c) economic activity related to the environment** (e.g. environmental protection expenditure). Extent, condition and services belong to **SEEA-EA**.
SC: CF = Flows + Stocks + Activity. EA = Extent + Condition + Services.

Q: SEEA is produced and released under the auspices of which of the following?
A) UN, European Commission, FAO, OECD, IMF and World Bank Group
B) UNEP alone
C) WTO and WIPO
D) IPCC and UNFCCC
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 300
EXP: SEEA is released jointly by the **UN, European Commission, FAO, OECD, IMF and World Bank Group**, the same group of agencies behind the SNA.
SC: The SNA sponsors plus FAO release the SEEA.

Q: The SEEA Ecosystem Accounting (SEEA EA) was adopted by the UN Statistical Commission at its:
A) 52nd session in March 2021
B) 43rd session in 2012
C) 48th session in 2017
D) 50th session in 2019
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 301
EXP: **SEEA EA** was adopted at the **52nd session (March 2021)**. It complements the CF with a **spatial, ecosystem** perspective on habitats, landscapes and ecosystem services.
SC: EA was adopted at the 52nd session in 2021.

Q: The "SEEA Applications and Extensions" document is:
A) a companion to the SEEA-CF showing how accounts can be used for analysis and indicators
B) a replacement for SEEA-EA
C) a sectoral framework for water
D) a manual for valuation of ecosystem services only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 301
EXP: **SEEA Applications and Extensions** is a **companion document to the SEEA-CF**. It shows compilers and users how to use the accounts for decision-making, policy and indicators such as resource use, environmental intensity and environmental protection activity.
SC: A&E is the user guide to the CF.

Q: In the SEEA-Energy framework, physical flows of energy are recorded in which common unit to aggregate across energy sources?
A) Joules
B) Tonnes of oil equivalent only
C) Kilowatt-hours only
D) Rupees
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 301
EXP: SEEA-Energy **physical flow accounts** record flows in **joules** so all energy sources can be added up. Monetary accounts run in parallel. **Asset accounts** track mineral and energy resource stocks.
SC: Energy is aggregated in joules, the SI unit.

Q: SEEA-Water comprises which three main types of accounts?
A) Physical flow accounts, physical asset accounts and economic accounts
B) Extent, condition and services accounts
C) Supply, use and input-output accounts
D) Stock, emission and thematic accounts
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 302
EXP: **SEEA-Water**: (i) **physical flow accounts** (abstraction, flows within the economy, return flows, emissions), (ii) **physical asset accounts** (the hydrological cycle and stocks), and (iii) **economic accounts** (water products, costs and financing).
SC: Water has three accounts: flow, asset and economic.

Q: SEEA-Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (AFF) accounts are most commonly compiled at the level of:
A) the individual product
B) the whole ecosystem
C) the household
D) the district only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 302
EXP: SEEA-AFF accounts are **most commonly compiled at the level of the individual product**, using **flow accounts** and **asset accounts** in physical and monetary terms.
SC: AFF is built product by product (crops, fish, timber).

Q: The SEEA responds to information needs in how many high-level policy areas, as per the EnviStats FAQ?
A) Four: economy and environment, people and environment, ecosystem health, and mitigating risks
B) Three: economy, society, environment
C) Five: the five Ps of the 2030 Agenda
D) Two: climate and biodiversity
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 304
EXP: The four policy areas are **economy & environment** (resource supply and demand), **people & environment** (access to services), **ecosystem health**, and **mitigating risks** (extreme events).
SC: Economy, People, Ecosystem, Risk.

Q: Which of the following is NOT listed as a limitation of environmental measurements in the EnviStats FAQ?
A) Environmental data can never be expressed in monetary terms
B) Measurements can be costly in technological and human resources
C) Working in silos hinders use of one agency's data by another
D) Some programmes require considerable time before results are available
ANS: A
D: Medium
T: Odd-one-out
P: 304
EXP: The listed limitations are **cost**, **silos** and **time lag**. Environmental data **can** be monetised: SEEA uses both physical and monetary terms.
SC: Monetisation is possible, so that option is the odd one out.

@topic Ecosystem Accounting
Q: The Convention on Biological Diversity defines an ecosystem as:
A) a dynamic complex of plant, animal and micro-organism communities and their non-living environment interacting as a functional unit
B) any protected area notified under law
C) a forest with more than 10% canopy cover
D) a group of species of the same genus
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 304
EXP: **CBD Article 2**: an ecosystem is "a **dynamic complex** of plant, animal and micro-organism communities and their **non-living environment** interacting as a **functional unit**."
SC: Living communities + non-living environment interacting as one functional unit.

Q: Why are ecosystem accounts described as "inherently spatial"?
A) Because an ecosystem's contribution to human well-being depends on its location
B) Because they are compiled only from satellite images
C) Because they cover only national parks
D) Because they ignore economic data
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 305
EXP: An ecosystem's contribution depends on **location**, for example how close it is to settlements. So ecosystem accounts are **spatial**, and **maps** are a common way to present them.
SC: Value depends on location, hence spatial accounts and maps.

Q: Which ecosystem account serves as the common starting point for ecosystem accounting and records the area of ecosystem types?
A) Ecosystem Extent Account
B) Ecosystem Condition Account
C) Monetary Ecosystem Asset Account
D) Thematic Account
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 305
EXP: The **Extent account** (area of forests, wetlands, agricultural areas, marine areas) is the **starting point**. It is followed by **Condition** (characteristics compared with a reference condition), **Services flow** (physical and monetary), **Monetary asset** (NPV) and **Thematic** accounts (biodiversity, climate, oceans, urban).
SC: Extent → Condition → Services → Monetary asset → Thematic.

Q: The Monetary Ecosystem Asset Account obtains opening and closing values of ecosystem assets using:
A) the net present value of ecosystem services
B) historical cost
C) replacement cost of buildings
D) market capitalisation
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 306
EXP: Monetary asset values are based on the **net present value** of the expected **ecosystem service flows**, giving opening and closing values for each accounting period.
SC: Asset value = discounted future service flows (NPV).

Q: Under SEEA EA, ecosystem services are classified into which three broad categories?
A) Provisioning, regulating and maintenance, and cultural services
B) Supporting, regulating and economic services
C) Market, non-market and hybrid services
D) Primary, secondary and tertiary services
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 306
EXP: SEEA EA uses **provisioning** (crops, wood, aquaculture), **regulating and maintenance** (climate regulation, air and water filtration, soil retention, flood control) and **cultural** services (recreation, visual amenity, education and research).
SC: Provide, Regulate, Culture.

Q: Air filtration, water filtration and flood control services provided by ecosystems are examples of:
A) regulating and maintenance services
B) provisioning services
C) cultural services
D) supporting services
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 306
EXP: These services **regulate** biological processes and cycles to **maintain** conditions that benefit people, so they are **regulating and maintenance** services.
SC: Filtering and controlling means regulating.

Q: The core valuation concept used in the SNA and applied in ecosystem accounting is:
A) exchange value
B) willingness to pay including consumer surplus
C) existence value
D) replacement cost only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 307
EXP: Both SNA and SEEA EA use **exchange value**, i.e. the value at which goods, services or assets are, or could be, exchanged. Valuation assigns value to the **contribution of ecosystems to benefits**, not to the benefits themselves.
SC: Exchange value, which excludes consumer surplus, keeps SEEA consistent with GDP.

Q: In SEEA, which method is prescribed for the valuation of crop provisioning services?
A) Resource Rent Method / Rental Price Method
B) Travel Cost Method
C) Hedonic Pricing Method
D) Contingent Valuation Method
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 307
EXP: Crop provisioning is a **final ecosystem service**, valued by the **Resource Rent (Rental Price) Method**: the ecosystem's share of the value of the harvested crop after deducting other inputs.
SC: Provisioning goods are valued by resource rent.

Q: How does "Non-Wood Forest Products" (NWFP) differ from "Non-Timber Forest Products" (NTFP)?
A) NWFP exclude all wood including fuelwood, whereas NTFP include wood used for purposes other than timber
B) NWFP include timber, NTFP do not
C) Both are identical
D) NWFP include minerals from forests
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 308
EXP: **NTFP** includes non-timber wood uses such as fuelwood. **NWFP excludes all wood**. **Rocks and minerals** from forests are **not NTFP**. NTFP examples: bamboo, lac, honey, tendu leaves, gum, resin.
SC: NWFP has no wood at all. NTFP excludes only timber.

Q: Soil erosion prevention service provided by croplands has been estimated as the difference between:
A) current soil loss and the probable soil loss if the cropland did not exist
B) soil formed and soil exported
C) fertiliser applied and fertiliser absorbed
D) rainfall and runoff
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 308
EXP: The service is the **avoided soil loss**: **probable loss without the cropland minus current loss**. It reflects the stabilising effect of vegetation.
SC: The service equals the damage avoided.

Q: Carbon retention services, as defined in the EnviStats FAQ, are estimated as:
A) annual service flow derived from carbon stocks using a suitable rate of return
B) total carbon stock in the ecosystem
C) carbon emitted by industries
D) carbon price multiplied by GDP
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 308
EXP: Carbon retention is the contribution of ecosystems to removing (**sequestration**) and storing (**retention**) carbon. The service is estimated as the **annual flow derived from carbon stocks using a suitable rate of return**.
SC: Stock × rate of return = annual service flow.

Q: Ecosystem assets under SEEA are classified into ecosystem types using which reference classification?
A) IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology
B) NIC-2008
C) FAO Land Cover Classification only
D) Köppen climate classification
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 309
EXP: Ecosystem assets (contiguous spaces of a specific ecosystem type) are classified using the **IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology**. They are measured by **extent, condition and service flows**.
SC: IUCN typology gives the ecosystem types.

@topic Mineral & Energy Resources, NPV & SEEA-SNA Link
Q: Under UNFC-2009, "Commercially Recoverable Resources" (including production projects and projects approved for development) fall in:
A) Class A
B) Class B
C) Class C
D) Class D
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 309
EXP: **Class A**: commercially recoverable. **Class B**: potentially commercially recoverable (development pending or on hold). **Class C**: non-commercial and other known deposits. Potential deposits with no expectation of viability are excluded.
SC: A = recoverable now, B = maybe, C = not commercial.

Q: As per the Indian Standard Procedure (ISP), coal resources falling within a 200 m radius of a borehole point are classified as:
A) Proved
B) Indicated
C) Inferred
D) Reserves only if unmined
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 310
EXP: **Proved** is within 200 m of a borehole. **Indicated** is between 200 m and 1 km. **Inferred** is between 1 km and 2 km. Only **economically mineable** occurrences are "reserves". The rest are "resources".
SC: 200 m proved, 1 km indicated, 2 km inferred.

Q: A change in known deposits because a government decision opens or closes a deposit to mining is termed:
A) reclassification
B) reappraisal
C) depletion
D) discovery
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 310
EXP: **Reclassification** is caused by government decisions on **access rights**. **Reappraisals** (up or down) reflect changes in geology, technology or price. All other quantity changes in known deposits count as reappraisals.
SC: Access rights changed = reclassification. New information = reappraisal.

Q: If the discount rate is 6%, an amount of 106 received next year corresponds to how much this year?
A) 100
B) 94
C) 106
D) 112.36
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
P: 311
EXP: PV = 106 ÷ 1.06 = **100**. The **NPV** of future resource rents, discounted this way, gives the **value of the resource in situ**.
SC: PV = FV/(1+r).

Q: In SEEA, the value of a mineral or energy resource in situ is assumed to correspond to:
A) the sum of discounted future resource rents (net present value of future extraction)
B) the current market price times total reserves, undiscounted
C) the cost of exploration
D) royalties paid last year
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 310
EXP: Yearly unit resource rents are **discounted back** to the reference year. Their sum, the **NPV**, is taken as the value of the resource in situ.
SC: Asset value = discounted future rents.

Q: The SEEA Central Framework follows the SNA's residence principle, meaning data are based on:
A) the residence of producer units rather than the territory where activity occurs
B) the territory where the activity physically occurs
C) the citizenship of owners
D) the location of head offices only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 311
EXP: SEEA-CF uses SNA conventions, including the **residence principle**: activity is attributed to **resident producer units**, not to territory. This is the same approach used for GDP.
SC: Residence, not territory, keeps SEEA consistent with GDP.

Q: In the "information pyramid" described in the EnviStats FAQ, accounting frameworks such as SNA and SEEA occupy:
A) the middle levels, integrating basic data to support indicators at the top
B) the base, as raw data
C) the top, as final indicators
D) no place in the pyramid
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 311
EXP: **Base**: basic data (economic, social, environmental). **Middle**: **accounts (SNA, SEEA)** that reconcile and integrate the data. **Top**: **indicators** such as GDP, national wealth and productivity.
SC: Data → Accounts → Indicators, from bottom to top.

@topic Biodiversity
Q: As per the CBD, "biological diversity" includes diversity:
1. within species
2. between species
3. of ecosystems
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 only
B) 1 and 2 only
C) 2 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Easy
P: 312
EXP: The CBD defines biodiversity as variability among living organisms from all sources, including **diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems**, i.e. genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.
SC: Genes, species and ecosystems: three levels.

Q: Which of the following factors influences the biodiversity of a region, as per the EnviStats FAQ?
A) Temperature, altitude, precipitation, soils and human pressures
B) Only latitude
C) Only protected area coverage
D) Only GDP per capita
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 312
EXP: Biodiversity is **unevenly distributed**. It is shaped by **temperature, altitude, precipitation, soils and pressures from human activities**. It covers both managed ecosystems (farms, plantations) and unmanaged ones (forests, national parks).
SC: Climate, terrain, soil and human pressure.

Q: The purpose of accounting for biodiversity, as per the FAQ, is primarily to:
A) mainstream biodiversity data in planning and decision making
B) price every species in the market
C) replace species surveys
D) restrict all land use
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 312
EXP: Biodiversity accounting aims at **mainstreaming** biodiversity data in planning and decisions. It informs conservation, ecosystem-service supply and responses such as **biodiversity finance**.
SC: The goal is mainstreaming, not pricing.

Q: Mountaineering, snorkeling, wildlife viewing and rafting fall under which ecosystem service as described in the EnviStats FAQ?
A) Nature-based tourism service
B) Provisioning service
C) Carbon retention
D) Soil retention
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 309
EXP: **Nature-based tourism** means providing opportunities for tourism and recreation that depend on natural landscapes and resources. It is a **cultural** ecosystem service.
SC: Recreation in nature is a cultural service (nature-based tourism).

Q: According to the FAQ, Natural Capital Accounting covers:
A) individual environmental assets (biotic and abiotic), ecosystem assets, biodiversity and ecosystem services, in physical and monetary terms
B) only minerals and energy in monetary terms
C) only forests in physical terms
D) only GDP adjustments
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 299
EXP: NCA covers **individual assets** (water, minerals, energy, timber, fish), **ecosystem assets** (forests, wetlands), **biodiversity** and **ecosystem services**, in **both physical and monetary** terms.
SC: NCA covers assets, ecosystems, biodiversity and services, physical and monetary.

Q: Why could a full-fledged environmental account for India not be compiled from earlier research studies?
A) Lack of comparability in methods and definitions used in those studies limited their aggregation
B) There were no studies at all
C) Studies were confidential
D) SNA prohibited such accounts

D: Medium
P: 298
EXP: India had rich studies on forests, wetlands, coasts and mangroves, but **differences in methods and definitions** meant they could not be **aggregated**. This led MoSPI to set up the Dasgupta group to build a standard framework.
SC: The studies were not comparable, so a standard framework was needed.
