@topic Health Data Systems
Q: The Health Management Information System (HMIS), a web-based facility reporting system for public health programmes, was launched under the:
A) National Rural Health Mission, in 2008
B) Ayushman Bharat programme, in 2018
C) Twelfth Five Year Plan, in 2012
D) National Health Policy, 1983
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **HMIS** was rolled out in **2008** under the **NRHM**. Facilities report monthly service data (ANC, deliveries, immunisation and so on). Unlike household surveys such as **NFHS**, it is **administrative** data.
SC: HMIS is the administrative monthly facility-reporting system of NRHM (2008).

Q: National Health Accounts (NHA) estimates for India, which track total and out-of-pocket health expenditure, are prepared by:
A) National Health Systems Resource Centre (NHA Technical Secretariat) for MoHFW
B) CBHI
C) NSO (NAD)
D) World Health Organization
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The **NHA estimates** are prepared by the **NHSRC**, designated as the **NHA Technical Secretariat**, following the WHO **System of Health Accounts (SHA 2011)**. They report **government health expenditure** and **out-of-pocket expenditure** shares.
SC: NHA comes from NHSRC using SHA 2011.

Q: Consider the following statements about sources of health statistics in India:
1. NFHS is a household sample survey providing State and district estimates.
2. HMIS is an administrative reporting system based on service delivery at health facilities.
3. SRS provides annual State-level estimates of IMR and MMR.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Easy
EXP: All three are correct: **NFHS** is a **survey** (State and district level); **HMIS** is **administrative facility data**; **SRS** gives **annual vital rates**, with **MMR** in special bulletins.
SC: Survey (NFHS), administrative (HMIS), vital registration sample (SRS): three distinct sources.

@topic NFHS-5 Headline Indicators
Q: As per NFHS-5 (2019-21), India's Total Fertility Rate was:
A) 2.0, below the replacement level
B) 2.2, at replacement level
C) 2.7
D) 1.6
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **NFHS-5 TFR = 2.0** (NFHS-4: 2.2), below the **replacement level of 2.1**. Only a few States, such as Bihar, UP, Jharkhand, Meghalaya and Manipur, remained above 2.1.
SC: NFHS-5 TFR was 2.0, below the replacement level of 2.1.

Q: As per NFHS-5, the percentage of institutional births in India was about:
A) 88.6%
B) 78.9%
C) 95.2%
D) 64.0%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Institutional births** rose from **78.9% (NFHS-4)** to **88.6% (NFHS-5)**.
SC: Institutional births went from about 79% to about 89%.

Q: As per NFHS-5, the prevalence of stunting, wasting and underweight among children under five years was respectively about:
A) 35.5%, 19.3% and 32.1%
B) 38.4%, 21.0% and 35.8%
C) 19.3%, 35.5% and 32.1%
D) 32.1%, 19.3% and 35.5%
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: NFHS-5: **stunting 35.5%**, **wasting 19.3%**, **underweight 32.1%**. NFHS-4 (option B) was 38.4 / 21.0 / 35.8. Stunting is low height-for-age (chronic); wasting is low weight-for-height (acute); underweight is low weight-for-age.
SC: NFHS-5 child malnutrition: stunting 35.5, wasting 19.3, underweight 32.1.

Q: As per NFHS-5, the prevalence of anaemia among women aged 15-49 years was about:
A) 57%
B) 35%
C) 67%
D) 25%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Anaemia**: **women 15-49: 57.0%** (up from 53.1%); **children 6-59 months: 67.1%** (up from 58.6%). Anaemia worsened between rounds.
SC: Anaemia: women 57%, children 67%, both up from NFHS-4.

Q: The sex ratio of the total population (females per 1,000 males) reported by NFHS-5, which crossed 1,000 for the first time in the NFHS series, was:
A) 1020
B) 991
C) 943
D) 929
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **NFHS-5 sex ratio = 1020** (NFHS-4: 991). The **sex ratio at birth** (last 5 years) was **929**. **943** is the **Census 2011** figure. NFHS counts the de facto population, so the 1020 should be read cautiously (absent male migrants).
SC: NFHS-5: 1020 overall, 929 at birth; Census 2011: 943.

Q: Stunting among children, as used in NFHS, refers to:
A) low height-for-age, reflecting chronic undernutrition
B) low weight-for-height, reflecting acute undernutrition
C) low weight-for-age
D) high weight-for-height
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Stunting = height-for-age < −2 SD** (chronic). **Wasting = weight-for-height < −2 SD** (acute). **Underweight = weight-for-age < −2 SD** (composite). The reference is the **WHO Child Growth Standards**.
SC: Stunted means short for age; wasted means thin for height.

@topic Mortality & Health Targets
Q: The Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) is expressed as:
A) maternal deaths per 1,00,000 live births
B) maternal deaths per 1,000 live births
C) maternal deaths per 1,00,000 women aged 15-49
D) maternal deaths per 1,000 women aged 15-49
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **MMR = maternal deaths per 1 lakh live births**. The **Maternal Mortality Rate** uses **1 lakh women aged 15-49**. SRS reported India's MMR at **97 (2018-20)**, below the NHP 2017 target of 100.
SC: The MMR ratio is per lakh live births; the MM rate is per lakh women.
TIP: The SRS special bulletin for 2019-21 put India's MMR at about 93. Check the latest bulletin before the exam.

Q: SDG target 3.1 aims to reduce the global maternal mortality ratio by 2030 to less than:
A) 70 per 1,00,000 live births
B) 100 per 1,00,000 live births
C) 25 per 1,000 live births
D) 12 per 1,000 live births
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **SDG 3.1**: MMR **below 70** per lakh live births. **SDG 3.2**: **NMR ≤ 12** and **U5MR ≤ 25** per 1,000 live births, by 2030.
SC: SDG 3: MMR < 70, NMR ≤ 12, U5MR ≤ 25.

Q: Which of the following were among the targets of India's National Health Policy 2017?
1. Increase life expectancy at birth to 70 years by 2025
2. Reduce TFR to 2.1 at national and sub-national levels by 2025
3. Reduce MMR to 100 by 2020
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
EXP: All three were **NHP 2017** targets: **LE 70 by 2025**, **TFR 2.1 by 2025**, **MMR 100 by 2020**. It also set **IMR 28 by 2019**, **U5MR 23 by 2025** and health spending of **2.5% of GDP by 2025**.
SC: NHP 2017: LE 70, TFR 2.1, MMR 100, IMR 28, health spend 2.5% of GDP.

Q: The Under-five Mortality Rate (U5MR) is defined as:
A) deaths of children below 5 years per 1,000 live births
B) deaths of children aged 1-4 years per 1,000 children aged 1-4
C) deaths of children below 5 years per 1,000 population
D) deaths of infants per 1,000 children under five
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **U5MR** is the probability of dying before age 5, expressed **per 1,000 live births**. The **child mortality rate** (ages 1-4) uses **children aged 1-4** in the denominator.
SC: U5MR is per 1,000 live births, like IMR.

Q: As per SRS 2020, India's IMR was:
A) 28 per 1,000 live births
B) 32 per 1,000 live births
C) 40 per 1,000 live births
D) 22 per 1,000 live births
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: SRS IMR fell from **32 (2018)** to **30 (2019)**, **28 (2020)** and **27 (2021)**. The long-run decline has continued.
SC: SRS IMR: 32 → 30 → 28 → 27 (2018-21).

@topic Education Statistics
Q: The Gender Parity Index (GPI) in education is defined as:
A) the ratio of the female GER to the male GER
B) the ratio of female to male literacy rates in the 15+ age group only
C) the number of girls per 100 boys enrolled
D) female enrolment minus male enrolment
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **GPI = female GER ÷ male GER**. A value of **1** means parity; above 1 favours girls. It is reported in UDISE+ and AISHE.
SC: GPI is girls' GER divided by boys' GER.

Q: The Pupil-Teacher Ratio (PTR) norm under the RTE Act, 2009 for primary classes is:
A) 30:1
B) 40:1
C) 35:1
D) 25:1
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **RTE Act schedule** sets **PTR 30:1 at primary** and **35:1 at upper primary**. The Act guarantees free and compulsory education for children aged **6-14 years**.
SC: RTE: PTR 30 (primary), 35 (upper primary), ages 6-14.

Q: The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 applies to children in the age group:
A) 6-14 years
B) 3-18 years
C) 5-15 years
D) 6-18 years
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: RTE covers ages **6-14**, under **Article 21A** (86th Amendment, 2002). **NEP 2020** proposes extending the scope to **3-18 years**.
SC: RTE covers 6-14; NEP 2020 envisions 3-18.

Q: NEP 2020 targets a Gross Enrolment Ratio in higher education (including vocational education) of:
A) 50% by 2035
B) 100% by 2030
C) 35% by 2025
D) 75% by 2040
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **NEP 2020**: **higher education GER 50% by 2035**, and **100% GER in school education (pre-school to secondary) by 2030**. Its school structure is **5+3+3+4**.
SC: NEP targets 50% higher-education GER by 2035 and universal schooling by 2030.

Q: In AISHE, the Gross Enrolment Ratio in higher education is computed for the population in the age group:
A) 18-23 years
B) 17-22 years
C) 15-24 years
D) 18-25 years
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **AISHE GER** = total higher-education enrolment ÷ population aged **18-23** × 100.
SC: The higher education GER uses ages 18-23.

Q: Under the Census definition, a person is literate if he/she:
A) is aged 7 or above and can both read and write with understanding in any language
B) has completed primary schooling
C) can sign his/her name
D) is aged 5 or above and can read in any language
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Census literacy covers **age 7+** and requires **reading and writing with understanding** in any language. Formal schooling is **not required**, and signing one's name alone is not enough. The **effective literacy rate** uses the **7+ population** as its denominator.
SC: 7+, read and write, with understanding, in any language.

Q: The difference between the "effective literacy rate" and the "crude literacy rate" lies in:
A) the denominator: the population aged 7+ versus the total population
B) the numerator: literates aged 15+ versus 7+
C) the language considered
D) the inclusion of school enrolment
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: **Effective literacy rate = literates ÷ population aged 7+**. **Crude literacy rate = literates ÷ total population**. Crude is lower because children aged 0-6 sit in its denominator. Census 2011 effective literacy was **74.04%**.
SC: Effective excludes children aged 0-6 from the denominator.

@topic Gender Statistics
Q: In India, the sex ratio is conventionally expressed as:
A) number of females per 1,000 males
B) number of males per 100 females
C) number of males per 1,000 females
D) percentage of females in the population
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: India uses **females per 1,000 males** (Census 2011: **943**). The international convention, as in the UN, is **males per 100 females**. The **child sex ratio** covers ages **0-6** (918 in 2011).
SC: India counts females per 1,000 males; the UN counts males per 100 females.

Q: The Gender Development Index (GDI) of UNDP is computed as:
A) the ratio of female HDI to male HDI
B) the HDI adjusted for inequality
C) a composite of reproductive health, empowerment and labour market
D) the gap between male and female incomes
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **GDI = female HDI ÷ male HDI**, using the same dimensions as HDI. The **GII** measures loss from inequality across **reproductive health** (MMR, adolescent birth rate), **empowerment** (parliamentary seats, secondary education) and the **labour market** (LFPR). The **IHDI** is the inequality-adjusted HDI.
SC: GDI is a ratio of HDIs; GII is a loss measure across three dimensions.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a dimension of UNDP's Gender Inequality Index (GII)?
A) Command over economic resources measured by earned income
B) Reproductive health
C) Empowerment
D) Labour market participation
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Medium
EXP: The **GII** dimensions are **reproductive health, empowerment and labour market**. **Earned income** enters the **GDI** (via the HDI's income dimension), not the GII.
SC: GII has no income dimension.

Q: The Global Gender Gap Index of the World Economic Forum is based on which four sub-indices?
A) Economic participation & opportunity, educational attainment, health & survival, political empowerment
B) Health, education, standard of living, security
C) Reproductive health, empowerment, labour market, income
D) Literacy, IMR, life expectancy, income
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **WEF GGGI** uses four sub-indices: **economic participation & opportunity, educational attainment, health & survival, political empowerment**. It scores from 0 to 1, where 1 is parity.
SC: GGGI covers economy, education, health and politics.

Q: Gender budgeting was formally introduced in India's Union Budget, with a Gender Budget Statement, in:
A) 2005-06
B) 1991-92
C) 2014-15
D) 2000-01
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **Gender Budget Statement** has appeared in the Union Budget since **2005-06**. **Part A** lists schemes with **100%** provision for women; **Part B** lists schemes with **at least 30%**.
SC: Gender budgeting started in 2005-06: Part A 100%, Part B 30%+.

Q: As per the Time Use Survey 2019, the average time spent per day by women (per participant) on unpaid domestic services for household members was about:
A) 299 minutes, compared with about 97 minutes for men
B) 97 minutes, compared with about 299 minutes for men
C) 180 minutes for both
D) 60 minutes, compared with 30 minutes for men
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: TUS 2019: **women about 299 minutes/day** against **men about 97 minutes/day** on **unpaid domestic services** (per participant). A second **Time Use Survey** was carried out in **2024**.
SC: Unpaid domestic work: women about 5 hours a day, men about 1.5 hours.

Q: Which of the following sources provides data on crimes against women in India?
A) NCRB's Crime in India
B) NFHS only
C) Census
D) PLFS
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **NCRB** reports **registered crimes** (administrative data). **NFHS** also collects **domestic violence** data through surveys, which capture unreported cases.
SC: NCRB gives registered crimes; NFHS gives self-reported violence.

@topic Disability, Elderly & Youth
Q: As per Census 2011, persons with disabilities constituted about what percentage of India's population?
A) 2.21%
B) 5.6%
C) 1.2%
D) 8.6%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Census 2011 counted **2.68 crore** persons with disabilities, **2.21%** of the population. The **NSS 76th round (2018)** estimated prevalence at about **2.2%**. **8.6%** is the share of **elderly (60+)** in 2011.
SC: Disability about 2.2% (Census and NSS); elderly 8.6%.

Q: The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 recognises how many types of disabilities?
A) 21
B) 7
C) 14
D) 12
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **RPwD Act 2016** expanded the list from **7** (PwD Act 1995) to **21** types of disability. These include acid-attack victims, autism spectrum, specific learning disabilities and others.
SC: 7 types in 1995, 21 in 2016.

Q: The Longitudinal Ageing Study in India (LASI), the country's first large survey on ageing, is conducted by:
A) IIPS, Mumbai, for MoHFW (with Harvard and USC partners)
B) NSO, MoSPI
C) HelpAge India
D) Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **LASI Wave 1 (2017-18)** was conducted by **IIPS** for **MoHFW**, covering persons aged **45+** and spouses. It gives data on the health, economic and social wellbeing of older adults.
SC: LASI is IIPS's ageing survey, like NFHS.

Q: As per the National Youth Policy 2014, "youth" is defined as persons in the age group:
A) 15-29 years
B) 13-35 years
C) 15-24 years
D) 18-30 years
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **National Youth Policy 2014** defines youth as **15-29 years** (the earlier policy used 13-35). The **UN's** statistical definition is **15-24 years**. PLFS youth unemployment uses **15-29**.
SC: India: youth 15-29; UN: 15-24.

Q: In official statistics, the elderly are usually defined as persons aged:
A) 60 years and above
B) 65 years and above
C) 55 years and above
D) 70 years and above
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: India's official definition of the elderly is **60+** (National Policy on Older Persons, 1999; MoSPI "Elderly in India"). Many international comparisons use **65+**. The **old-age dependency ratio** uses the 60+ (or 65+) population over the working-age population.
SC: India's elderly are 60+.

Q: The Child Sex Ratio in the Census refers to the number of females per 1,000 males in the age group:

@topic Concepts & Measurement Issues
Q: Assertion (A): Registered crime statistics may understate the actual incidence of crimes against women.
Reason (R): Administrative data capture only reported and registered cases, and under-reporting is common for sensitive crimes.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Easy
EXP: Administrative data such as NCRB's show only **reported and registered** crimes. **Victimisation surveys** or NFHS modules capture unreported cases, so R explains A.
SC: Administrative data miss what is never reported.

Q: Which of the following is an example of an "administrative" data source in the social sector?
A) UDISE+ school returns
B) NFHS
C) PLFS
D) NSS Household Social Consumption: Education survey
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **UDISE+** is compiled from **school returns**, a by-product of administration. **NFHS, PLFS and NSS** are **sample surveys** of households.
SC: School returns are administrative; household questionnaires are surveys.

Q: Why may GER exceed 100% at the primary level?
A) Because enrolment includes over-age and under-age children, while the denominator is only the official-age population
B) Because of double counting of schools
C) Because the Census undercounts children
D) GER can never exceed 100%
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: **GER** counts **all enrolled** children regardless of age, divided by the **official-age population**. Over-age and under-age pupils can push it **above 100%**. **NER** cannot exceed 100%.
SC: GER can exceed 100%; NER cannot.

Q: The Gender Parity Index for GER at the secondary level is 1.02. This means:
A) the female GER is slightly higher than the male GER
B) there are 102 girls per 100 boys in the population
C) girls' literacy exceeds boys' by 2%
D) the male GER is higher than the female GER
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: **GPI > 1** means the **female GER exceeds the male GER**. A GPI between 0.97 and 1.03 is generally regarded as parity.
SC: GPI above 1 favours girls.

Q: The Human Development Index of UNDP combines which dimensions?
A) Long and healthy life, knowledge, and a decent standard of living
B) Health, gender equality and security
C) Education, employment and environment
D) Income, inequality and poverty
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: HDI has **three dimensions**: **health** (life expectancy at birth), **knowledge** (expected and mean years of schooling) and **standard of living** (GNI per capita, PPP). They are aggregated by a **geometric mean**.
SC: HDI = health, knowledge, income, combined by GM.

Q: A State's IMR falls from 40 to 30 per 1,000 live births. The percentage decline is:
A) 25%
B) 33.3%
C) 10%
D) 75%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: The decline is **(40 − 30)/40 = 25%**. Percentage change is always measured on the starting value.
SC: Divide the change by the old value.

Q: Which survey is India's main source for district-level estimates of child nutrition, anaemia and family planning indicators?
A) NFHS (since NFHS-4)
B) SRS
C) Census
D) PLFS
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **NFHS-4 (2015-16)** and **NFHS-5 (2019-21)** give **district-level** estimates for key indicators. The earlier DLHS and AHS did so for some States. **SRS** gives **State** vital rates only.
SC: District health indicators come from NFHS.
