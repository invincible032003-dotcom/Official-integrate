@topic Poverty Lines in India
Q: Arrange the following expert groups on poverty estimation in chronological order:
1. Tendulkar Expert Group
2. Lakdawala Expert Group
3. Rangarajan Expert Group
4. Alagh Task Force
A) 4-2-1-3
B) 2-4-1-3
C) 4-1-2-3
D) 4-2-3-1
ANS: A
T: Chronology
D: Easy
EXP: The order is **Alagh (1979) → Lakdawala (1993) → Tendulkar (2009) → Rangarajan (2014)**. A 1962 Working Group had earlier set **₹20 per capita per month** at 1960-61 prices.
SC: 79 Alagh, 93 Lakdawala, 2009 Tendulkar, 2014 Rangarajan.

Q: The Alagh Task Force (1979) anchored poverty lines to calorie norms of:
A) 2,400 kcal (rural) and 2,100 kcal (urban) per capita per day
B) 2,100 kcal (rural) and 2,400 kcal (urban)
C) 2,250 kcal for both
D) 1,800 kcal (rural) and 1,600 kcal (urban)
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Alagh: 2,400 kcal rural, 2,100 kcal urban**, which gave poverty lines of ₹49.09 (rural) and ₹56.64 (urban) at 1973-74 prices. Rural norms are higher because of **heavier manual work**.
SC: Rural 2,400, urban 2,100: rural work is heavier.

Q: The Lakdawala Expert Group (1993) recommended that State-specific poverty lines be updated using:
A) CPI-AL for rural areas and CPI-IW for urban areas
B) WPI for both
C) CPI (Combined) for both
D) the GDP deflator
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Lakdawala** disaggregated to **State-specific** lines, updated with **CPI-AL (rural)** and **CPI-IW (urban)**. That is why CPI-AL is still cited for **rural poverty lines** and CPI-IW for **urban** ones.
SC: Lakdawala updates rural lines with CPI-AL and urban lines with CPI-IW.

Q: Which of the following statements about the Tendulkar Expert Group is/are correct?
1. It moved away from calorie anchoring of the poverty line.
2. It used a uniform poverty line basket (the urban PLB) for rural and urban areas.
3. It estimated poverty in 2011-12 at 21.9% using its methodology.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
EXP: All three are correct. **Tendulkar** dropped calorie anchoring, used a **uniform PLB** (the urban 2004-05 basket) on the **MRP** basis, and gave **37.2% (2004-05)** and **21.9% (2011-12)** poverty, with rural 25.7% and urban 13.7%.
SC: Tendulkar: no calories, one basket, 37.2 → 21.9.

Q: As per the Tendulkar methodology, the poverty lines for 2011-12 were about:
A) ₹816 (rural) and ₹1,000 (urban) per capita per month
B) ₹972 (rural) and ₹1,407 (urban) per capita per month
C) ₹32 (rural) and ₹47 (urban) per capita per day
D) ₹49 (rural) and ₹57 (urban) per capita per month
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **Tendulkar 2011-12: ₹816 rural, ₹1,000 urban** per month (about ₹27 and ₹33 a day). **Rangarajan 2011-12: ₹972 and ₹1,407** per month (about ₹32 and ₹47 a day).
SC: Tendulkar 816/1000; Rangarajan 972/1407.

Q: The Rangarajan Expert Group (2014) estimated the poverty ratio in India for 2011-12 at:
A) 29.5%
B) 21.9%
C) 37.2%
D) 25.7%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Rangarajan: 29.5% in 2011-12** (rural 30.9%, urban 26.4%). It used **MMRP** and revived nutrition-based norms (calories, protein, fat) plus non-food essentials. The Government did not formally adopt a newer line.
SC: Rangarajan 29.5%; Tendulkar 21.9% for the same year.

Q: Which reference period was used by the Rangarajan Expert Group for consumption data?
A) Modified Mixed Reference Period (MMRP)
B) Uniform Reference Period (URP)
C) Mixed Reference Period (MRP)
D) Current Weekly Status
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Rangarajan used MMRP**. **Tendulkar used MRP**, and earlier estimates (Lakdawala) used **URP**. MMRP gives **higher MPCE** for the same households.
SC: URP is Lakdawala's, MRP Tendulkar's, MMRP Rangarajan's.

@topic Poverty & Inequality Measures
Q: In the Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT) class of poverty measures, α = 0, 1 and 2 correspond respectively to:
A) headcount ratio, poverty gap index, squared poverty gap (severity)
B) poverty gap, headcount, Sen index
C) Gini, Theil, Atkinson
D) squared poverty gap, poverty gap, headcount ratio
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **FGT(α)**: **α = 0 is the headcount ratio** (incidence); **α = 1 is the poverty gap index** (depth); **α = 2 is the squared poverty gap** (severity, weighting the poorest more).
SC: α = 0 incidence, 1 depth, 2 severity.

Q: A limitation of the headcount ratio as a poverty measure is that it:
A) is insensitive to how far below the poverty line the poor are
B) cannot be computed from survey data
C) always overstates poverty
D) requires a price index
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: The **headcount ratio** counts the poor but ignores **depth**. A transfer from a very poor person to one just below the line can even **reduce** it. The **poverty gap** and **squared gap** address this.
SC: Headcount counts the poor but ignores how poor they are.

Q: The Gini coefficient derived from a Lorenz curve is equal to:
A) the area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve divided by the total area under the line of equality
B) the area under the Lorenz curve
C) the slope of the Lorenz curve at the median
D) the ratio of the top 10% share to the bottom 40% share
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Gini = A/(A + B)**, where A is the area between the diagonal and the Lorenz curve. Equivalently **Gini = 1 − 2 × (area under Lorenz)**. The **Palma ratio** is the **top 10% ÷ bottom 40%** share.
SC: Gini = gap area ÷ half-square = 1 − 2 × area under the Lorenz curve.

Q: If the area under a Lorenz curve (in the unit square) is 0.35, the Gini coefficient is:
A) 0.30
B) 0.35
C) 0.65
D) 0.70
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **Gini = 1 − 2 × 0.35 = 0.30**. With perfect equality the area is 0.5 and the Gini is 0.
SC: Gini = 1 − 2 × area.

Q: The Gini coefficient lies between:
A) 0 (perfect equality) and 1 (perfect inequality)
B) −1 and +1
C) 0 and 100, with 0 meaning perfect inequality
D) 1 and infinity
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The Gini runs from **0 (all equal)** to **1 (one person has everything)**. It is often reported as ×100.
SC: 0 means equal; 1 means one person has everything.

Q: The Palma ratio measures inequality as:
A) the income share of the top 10% divided by that of the bottom 40%
B) the share of the top 20% divided by the bottom 20%
C) the difference between mean and median income
D) the Gini coefficient times 100
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **Palma ratio** is **top 10% ÷ bottom 40%**, reflecting the observation that the middle 50% tends to keep a stable share. The **S80/S20** ratio compares the top and bottom quintiles.
SC: Palma = top 10 ÷ bottom 40.

@topic Multidimensional Poverty
Q: The global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) is jointly produced by:
A) UNDP and the Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative (OPHI)
B) World Bank and IMF
C) NITI Aayog and UNICEF
D) FAO and WFP
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **Global MPI** (since the **2010** HDR) is produced by **UNDP and OPHI**. It uses the **Alkire-Foster** method and replaced the **Human Poverty Index**.
SC: The Global MPI comes from UNDP and OPHI, using Alkire-Foster.

Q: In the MPI, the index is computed as:
A) MPI = H × A, where H is the headcount of the multidimensionally poor and A is the average intensity of their deprivation
B) MPI = H + A
C) MPI = H / A
D) MPI = geometric mean of the three dimension indices
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **MPI = H × A**. A person is **poor if deprived in at least 1/3 of the weighted indicators**. Health, education and living standards carry **equal weights (1/3 each)**.
SC: MPI = incidence × intensity; poor if deprived in at least a third.

Q: Compared with the 10 indicators of the global MPI, India's National MPI (NITI Aayog) adds which two indicators?
A) Maternal health and bank account
B) Child labour and crime
C) Internet access and electricity
D) Income and employment
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **National MPI** has **12 indicators**. It adds **maternal health** (under health) and **bank account** (under living standards) to the global 10. It uses **NFHS** data.
SC: National MPI = global 10 + maternal health + bank account.

Q: As per NITI Aayog's National MPI progress review (2023), the share of the multidimensionally poor in India fell between NFHS-4 (2015-16) and NFHS-5 (2019-21) from:
A) 24.85% to 14.96%, lifting about 13.5 crore people out of poverty
B) 37.2% to 21.9%
C) 29.5% to 21.9%
D) 55% to 28%
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **National MPI headcount: 24.85% → 14.96%**, with about **13.5 crore** people exiting multidimensional poverty. A **2024 NITI discussion paper** estimated a fall from about 29% (2013-14) to about 11% (2022-23).
SC: National MPI: about 25% to 15%, 13.5 crore people out.

Q: The Human Poverty Index was replaced in UNDP's Human Development Report by the MPI in:
A) 2010
B) 1997
C) 2000
D) 2015
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **2010 HDR** (20th anniversary) introduced the **MPI, IHDI and GII**, replacing the **HPI (1997)** and **GEM**.
SC: HDR 2010 brought in the MPI, IHDI and GII.

Q: The World Bank's international extreme poverty line, which was USD 2.15 a day at 2017 PPPs, was revised in 2025 (using 2021 PPPs) to:
A) USD 3.00 a day
B) USD 1.90 a day
C) USD 2.50 a day
D) USD 5.50 a day
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The extreme poverty line has moved **USD 1.90 (2011 PPP) → USD 2.15 (2017 PPP, from 2022) → USD 3.00 (2021 PPP, June 2025 update)**. Poverty comparisons shift with each **ICP round**.
SC: USD 1.90 → USD 2.15 → USD 3.00, following each new PPP round.

@topic Household Consumption Expenditure Survey
Q: The Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES) 2022-23 collected data using:
A) three separate questionnaires (food; consumables & services; durable goods) canvassed in three monthly visits
B) a single schedule canvassed in one visit
C) telephonic interviews only
D) administrative data from GST
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **HCES 2022-23** (August 2022 – July 2023) used **three questionnaires**: **FDQ** (food), **CSQ** (consumables & services) and **DGQ** (durable goods). Each was canvassed in a **separate monthly visit** to the same household, on the **MMRP** basis. Items received free through welfare schemes were **imputed** in a separate estimate.
SC: HCES 2022-23 used three questionnaires in three visits with MMRP.

Q: As per HCES 2022-23, the average monthly per capita consumption expenditure (MPCE, without imputation) was about:
A) ₹3,773 in rural and ₹6,459 in urban India
B) ₹1,430 in rural and ₹2,630 in urban India
C) ₹4,122 in rural and ₹6,996 in urban India
D) ₹2,500 in rural and ₹5,000 in urban India
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The MPCE figures are **HCES 2022-23: rural ₹3,773, urban ₹6,459**; **HCES 2023-24: rural ₹4,122, urban ₹6,996**; **2011-12 (MMRP): rural ₹1,430, urban ₹2,630**. The urban-rural gap narrowed from **84% (2011-12)** to **71% (2022-23)** and **70% (2023-24)**.
SC: 2022-23: 3.8k rural / 6.5k urban; 2023-24: 4.1k / 7.0k.

Q: The HCES results for 2022-23 showed that the share of food in total consumption expenditure was:
A) below 50% in both rural and urban areas
B) above 60% in rural areas
C) higher in urban than in rural areas
D) exactly 50% in both areas
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The food share fell to about **46% (rural)** and **39% (urban)**. The rural share stayed above the urban share, consistent with **Engel's law**. Within food, **beverages and processed food** took a rising share.
SC: Food fell below half the budget, lower in urban areas.

Q: Engel's law states that as household income rises:
A) the proportion of income spent on food falls, although absolute food spending may rise
B) absolute spending on food falls
C) the proportion of income spent on food rises
D) the proportion spent on all goods remains constant
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Engel's law**: the **food share (Engel ratio)** falls as income rises, and the **income elasticity of food demand is below 1**. The falling food share in HCES is consistent with rising living standards.
SC: The richer the household, the smaller its food share.

Q: Why was the NSS Consumer Expenditure Survey of 2017-18 not released?
A) The Government cited data quality concerns, including divergence from administrative data, and decided to improve survey methodology
B) The fieldwork was never started
C) It was merged into the Population Census
D) The data were released only to the RBI
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **2017-18 CES (NSS 75th round)** results were **withheld in 2019** over **data-quality concerns**. The next full survey was the redesigned **HCES 2022-23**.
SC: The 2017-18 CES was withheld; HCES 2022-23 was the redesigned successor.

Q: Consumption rather than income is generally used for measuring poverty in India because:
A) consumption is more stable and more reliably reported than income, particularly for self-employed and agricultural households
B) income data are illegal to collect
C) consumption is always higher than income
D) the Constitution requires it
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: **Consumption** is **smoother** (households smooth it over the year) and **better reported**, while incomes of the self-employed and farmers are **volatile** and under-reported. Poverty lines are therefore consumption-based.
SC: Consumption is smooth and well reported; income is volatile.

@topic Development Indices
Q: The first Human Development Report was published by UNDP in:
A) 1990
B) 1985
C) 1995
D) 2000
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The first **HDR (1990)** was led by **Mahbub ul Haq** with **Amartya Sen's** capability approach. It introduced the **HDI**.
SC: HDR began in 1990, led by Mahbub ul Haq.

Q: In the HDI, the education dimension is measured by:
A) mean years of schooling (adults 25+) and expected years of schooling (children of school-entry age)
B) literacy rate and gross enrolment ratio
C) GER and NER
D) number of schools per lakh population
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Since **2010**, the HDI uses **mean years of schooling (25+)** and **expected years of schooling**. It earlier used **adult literacy and combined GER**. Health uses **life expectancy at birth**; income uses **GNI per capita (PPP, log)**.
SC: HDI education = mean + expected years of schooling (since 2010).

Q: Since 2010, the three HDI dimension indices are combined using:
A) the geometric mean
B) the arithmetic mean
C) the harmonic mean
D) the median
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **geometric mean** (since 2010) means poor performance in one dimension is **not fully offset** by others. Earlier HDRs used the **arithmetic mean**.
SC: The GM stops strength in one dimension from masking weakness in another.

Q: With an HDI value of about 0.68, India falls in which UNDP human development category?
A) Medium human development
B) Low human development
C) High human development
D) Very high human development
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: UNDP categories: **very high ≥ 0.800**, **high 0.700-0.799**, **medium 0.550-0.699**, **low < 0.550**. India is in the **medium** group.
SC: 0.55-0.70 is medium.

Q: The Inequality-adjusted HDI (IHDI) will equal the HDI when:
A) there is no inequality in the distribution of achievements across people
B) the HDI is above 0.8
C) the GII is zero
D) income is the only dimension
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **IHDI** discounts each dimension by its **inequality (Atkinson measure)**. With **perfect equality, IHDI = HDI**. The gap measures the **loss due to inequality**.
SC: No inequality means IHDI = HDI.

Q: The SDG India Index, prepared by NITI Aayog since 2018, classifies States with a composite score of 65-99 as:
A) Front Runner
B) Achiever
C) Performer
D) Aspirant
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The categories are **Aspirant (0-49)**, **Performer (50-64)**, **Front Runner (65-99)** and **Achiever (100)**. India's composite score rose to **71** in the 2023-24 edition, with Kerala and Uttarakhand at the top.
SC: Aspirant < 50, Performer 50-64, Front Runner 65-99, Achiever 100.

Q: The Physical Quality of Life Index (PQLI), developed by Morris D. Morris, uses which indicators?
A) Literacy rate, infant mortality rate and life expectancy at age one
B) Life expectancy at birth, GNI per capita and schooling
C) IMR, MMR and literacy
D) GDP per capita, literacy and life expectancy
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **PQLI** combines **literacy**, **IMR** and **life expectancy at age 1**, each scaled 0-100 and averaged. It deliberately **excludes income**.
SC: PQLI = literacy + IMR + LE at age 1, with no income.

@topic Concepts & Numericals
Q: In a village of 1,000 persons, 200 are below the poverty line. The average shortfall of their consumption from the poverty line is 25% of the line. The poverty gap index is:
A) 0.05
B) 0.20
C) 0.25
D) 0.45
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Hard
EXP: **PGI = H × (average gap ratio among the poor) = 0.20 × 0.25 = 0.05**. Equivalently, it is the mean over the **whole population** of the normalised shortfall, counting zero for the non-poor.
SC: Poverty gap = headcount × average relative gap.

Q: Assertion (A): The poverty gap index can decline even if the headcount ratio remains unchanged.
Reason (R): The poverty gap index is sensitive to changes in the consumption of the poor below the poverty line.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Medium
EXP: If the poor gain but **stay below the line**, **H is unchanged** while the **gap shrinks**, so the PGI falls. The sensitivity in R is exactly why A holds.
SC: Depth can improve without the headcount moving.

Q: The Lorenz curve plots:
A) the cumulative percentage of population (from poorest) against the cumulative percentage of income or consumption
B) income against time
C) the poverty line against price index
D) mean income against median income
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **Lorenz curve** puts the **cumulative population share** (x-axis, ranked poorest first) against the **cumulative income share** (y-axis). The further it bows from the diagonal, the greater the inequality.
SC: The more the Lorenz curve bows, the more unequal.

Q: The Kuznets curve hypothesis relates:
A) income inequality to per capita income, suggesting inequality first rises and then falls with development
B) pollution to income, suggesting it always rises
C) poverty to inflation
D) unemployment to inflation
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **Kuznets curve** is an **inverted U**: **inequality rises, then falls** as per capita income grows. The **Environmental Kuznets Curve** applies the same idea to pollution. The unemployment-inflation link is the Phillips curve.
SC: Kuznets: inequality traces an inverted U against development.

Q: Which poverty measure satisfies the "transfer principle", rising when income is transferred from a poorer to a less poor person below the line?
A) Squared poverty gap index (FGT α = 2)
B) Headcount ratio
C) Poverty gap index
D) Number of poor
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **FGT α = 2 (severity)** gives more weight to the **poorest**, so a regressive transfer among the poor **raises** it. The **poverty gap (α = 1)** is unchanged by such transfers, and the **headcount** may even fall.
SC: Only the squared gap penalises transfers away from the poorest.

Q: The Tendulkar methodology estimated India's poverty ratio for 2004-05 at:
A) 37.2%
B) 27.5%
C) 21.9%
D) 29.8%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Tendulkar: 37.2% (2004-05)**, **29.8% (2009-10)** and **21.9% (2011-12)**. The Lakdawala method gave **27.5%** for 2004-05.
SC: Tendulkar: 37.2 → 29.8 → 21.9.

Q: The urban-rural gap in average MPCE (urban MPCE relative to rural) narrowed from about 84% in 2011-12 to about:
A) 70-71% in 2022-24
B) 100% in 2022-24
C) 40% in 2022-24
D) It widened to 95%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The gap was **2011-12: 84%**, **2022-23: 71%** and **2023-24: 70%**, meaning **rural consumption grew faster**.
SC: The urban-rural consumption gap shrank from 84% to about 70%.

Q: Which of the following is NOT an indicator in the global MPI?
A) Income below USD 3 per day
B) Nutrition
C) Years of schooling
D) Cooking fuel
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Easy
EXP: The **MPI is non-monetary**. Its 10 indicators are **nutrition, child mortality, years of schooling, school attendance, cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing and assets**. Income poverty is measured separately.
SC: The MPI contains no income indicator.
