@topic Census 2011: Headline Results
Q: As per Census 2011, the population of India was about:
A) 121.09 crore
B) 102.87 crore
C) 131.10 crore
D) 118.20 crore
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Census 2011 counted **121.09 crore** people (males 62.32 crore, females 58.75 crore). **102.87 crore** was the **2001** count.
SC: 2011: about 121 crore; 2001: about 103 crore.

Q: The decadal growth rate of India's population during 2001-2011 was about:
A) 17.7%
B) 21.5%
C) 23.9%
D) 12.3%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The decadal growth rate was **17.7% (2001-11)**, down from **21.5% (1991-2001)**. 2001-11 was the first decade since 1911-21 in which the absolute addition was lower than in the previous decade.
SC: Growth slowed from 21.5% to 17.7%.

Q: As per Census 2011, the population density of India was:
A) 382 persons per sq km
B) 325 persons per sq km
C) 267 persons per sq km
D) 420 persons per sq km
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Density was **382 per sq km (2011)**, up from **325 (2001)**. The most dense State was **Bihar (1,106)** and the least **Arunachal Pradesh (17)**. Among UTs, **Delhi** was the most dense.
SC: 382 nationally; Bihar highest, Arunachal lowest.

Q: As per Census 2011, the overall sex ratio and child sex ratio (0-6 years) of India were respectively:
A) 943 and 918
B) 933 and 927
C) 940 and 914
D) 918 and 943
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **2011: sex ratio 943, CSR 918**. **2001: 933 and 927**. The sex ratio improved while the CSR worsened.
SC: 2011: 943 overall, 918 for children.

Q: As per Census 2011, which State had the highest sex ratio and which had the lowest?

SC: Kerala tops the sex ratio; Haryana is lowest for both overall and child sex ratio.

Q: The Work Participation Rate (WPR) as per Census 2011 was about:

@topic Census Concepts & Operations
Q: In the Census, a "main worker" is one who has worked for:
A) 6 months (183 days) or more during the reference year
B) 30 days or more during the last 365 days
C) at least one hour on any day of the reference week
D) the majority of the last 7 days
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Census workers are **main workers (≥ 6 months, 183 days)** or **marginal workers (< 6 months)**. Marginal workers are further split into **3-6 months** and **< 3 months**. Distractors B and C are **PLFS** concepts (ss and CWS).
SC: Census main worker ≥ 183 days; marginal < 183 days.

Q: A "Census Town" in the 2011 Census must satisfy which criteria?
1. A minimum population of 5,000
2. At least 75% of the male main working population engaged in non-agricultural pursuits
3. A density of at least 400 persons per sq km
4. A municipality or cantonment board
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 2, 3 and 4 only
B) 1 and 4 only
C) 1, 2 and 3 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: C
T: Statement
D: Medium
EXP: A **Census Town** needs all three criteria: **5,000+ population**, **75%+ male main workers in non-agriculture** and **400+ per sq km**. Places with a **municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area** are **statutory towns**, whatever their size.
SC: Census town = 5,000 people, 75% non-farm, 400 per sq km, with no municipal status.

Q: As per Census 2011, the number of towns in India was 7,935. Of these, the numbers of statutory towns and census towns were respectively:
A) 4,041 and 3,894
B) 3,894 and 4,041
C) 5,161 and 2,774
D) 3,799 and 4,136
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: Census 2011 had **4,041 statutory towns** and **3,894 census towns**. Census towns grew sharply from **1,362 in 2001**. There were **6,40,867 villages**.
SC: 4,041 statutory + 3,894 census = 7,935 towns.

Q: As per Census 2011, the number of million-plus cities / urban agglomerations in India was:
A) 53
B) 35
C) 23
D) 75
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: There were **53 million-plus UAs/cities in 2011**, up from **35 in 2001**. Greater **Mumbai UA** was the largest, followed by **Delhi UA** and **Kolkata UA**.
SC: 35 million-plus cities in 2001, 53 in 2011.

Q: The reference date for Census 2011 population enumeration was:
A) 00:00 hours of 1 March 2011
B) 1 January 2011
C) 00:00 hours of 9 February 2011
D) 31 March 2011
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Enumeration ran **9-28 February 2011**, with a **revisional round** in early March. The reference moment was **00:00 hrs, 1 March 2011**. Snow-bound areas used **1 October 2010**. Houselisting took place **April-September 2010**.
SC: Enumeration in February; reference moment 00:00 on 1 March.

Q: Which of the following was introduced for the first time in the 2011 Census?
A) Preparation of the National Population Register along with the Houselisting operations
B) Counting of Scheduled Castes
C) Use of the canvasser method
D) Enumeration of literacy
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Census 2011** collected **NPR** schedules during **Houselisting** (2010), for the first time. SC enumeration, the canvasser method and literacy questions are all old. The tagline was **"Our Census, Our Future"**.
SC: The NPR first came with Census 2011 houselisting.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a statutory basis or feature of the Population Census in India?
A) It is conducted under the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008
B) It is conducted under the Census Act, 1948
C) Individual data collected are confidential under the Census Act
D) It is conducted by the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, MHA
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Medium
EXP: The **Census** runs under the **Census Act 1948** and **Census Rules 1990**, which include **confidentiality** (Section 15). The **CoS Act 2008** governs other statistical surveys such as the ASI.
SC: The Census has its own Act (1948); the CoS Act covers other surveys.

@topic Demographic Measures
Q: The Total Fertility Rate (TFR), using five-year age groups, is computed as:
A) 5 × Σ ASFR (per woman), summed over ages 15-49
B) Σ ASFR over single years × 1000
C) live births ÷ women 15-49 × 1000
D) CBR × 5
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **TFR = 5 × Σ ASFR** (ASFR per woman, 5-year groups 15-19 to 45-49). It is the number of children a woman would bear at current rates. **GFR** = births ÷ women 15-49 × 1000.
SC: TFR = 5 × the sum of 5-year ASFRs.

Q: The Gross Reproduction Rate (GRR) differs from the TFR in that the GRR:
A) counts only female births
B) accounts for mortality of mothers
C) uses married women only
D) is measured per 1,000 population
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **GRR = TFR × proportion of female births** (about 0.488). The **NRR** additionally adjusts for **female survival** to childbearing ages. **NRR = 1** is **replacement level**.
SC: GRR counts daughters; NRR counts surviving daughters.

Q: A Net Reproduction Rate (NRR) of 1 implies that:
A) each generation of women exactly replaces itself
B) population will double in one generation
C) TFR equals 1
D) population is declining
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **NRR = 1** means **replacement fertility**. It corresponds to **TFR of about 2.1** in low-mortality populations. The **National Population Policy 2000** aimed at NRR = 1 (TFR 2.1).
SC: NRR 1 means each mother is replaced by one daughter.

Q: Why is replacement-level TFR about 2.1 rather than exactly 2.0?
A) Because slightly more boys than girls are born and some girls die before reaching reproductive age
B) Because of migration
C) Because of multiple births
D) Because TFR is measured per 1,000 women
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: A **sex ratio at birth** above parity (about 105 boys per 100 girls) and **female mortality** before and during the reproductive ages push replacement above 2. It is about **2.1** where mortality is low and higher where mortality is high.
SC: Extra boys and female deaths push replacement above 2.

Q: The dependency ratio is defined as:
A) (population aged 0-14 + population aged 65+) ÷ population aged 15-64 × 100
B) population aged 15-64 ÷ total population
C) workers ÷ non-workers
D) population aged 65+ ÷ population aged 0-14
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **total dependency ratio** is **(young + old) ÷ working-age population**. Indian publications often use **0-14, 60+ and 15-59**. The **ageing index** is old ÷ young.
SC: Dependency = (young + old) ÷ working age.

Q: A population grows at a constant annual rate of 2%. Its approximate doubling time is:
A) 35 years
B) 50 years
C) 20 years
D) 70 years
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: By the **rule of 70**, doubling time ≈ **70 ÷ 2 = 35 years**. Exactly, it is ln 2/r = 0.693/0.02 ≈ 34.7.
SC: Doubling time = 70 ÷ growth %.

Q: Population was 100 crore in year t and 121 crore in year t + 10. The average annual exponential growth rate is about:
A) 1.9%
B) 2.1%
C) 1.2%
D) 2.4%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Hard
EXP: **r = ln(121/100)/10 = ln(1.21)/10 = 0.1906/10 ≈ 1.9%**. The simple average of 21%/10 = 2.1% overstates the compound rate.
SC: Exponential r = ln(P2/P1) ÷ years.

Q: The "demographic dividend" refers to:
A) the growth potential arising when the share of the working-age population rises relative to dependants
B) the revenue from a population tax
C) the decline in population after replacement fertility
D) the benefit from emigration
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: A **falling dependency ratio** (fewer children per worker as fertility declines) creates a **window** for faster growth, provided the extra workers are **employed and productive**.
SC: More workers per dependant opens a demographic window.

@topic Projections & Global Comparisons
Q: According to the UN (World Population Prospects), India overtook China as the world's most populous country in:
A) 2023
B) 2011
C) 2030
D) 2020
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **UN DESA** estimated India overtook China around **April 2023**. China's population had begun **declining** in 2022.
SC: India became the most populous country in 2023.

Q: As per the Report of the Technical Group on Population Projections (2020), India's population is projected to reach about what size by 2036?
A) 152 crore
B) 170 crore
C) 140 crore
D) 130 crore
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The **Technical Group (National Commission on Population)** projected India's population to rise from **121.1 crore (2011)** to about **152.2 crore (2036)**. It used the **cohort component method**, with **Census 2011 + SRS** inputs.
SC: Projection: about 152 crore by 2036.

Q: The cohort component method of population projection projects each age-sex cohort using:
A) fertility, mortality and migration assumptions
B) a fitted logistic curve to total population
C) linear extrapolation of total population
D) administrative Aadhaar enrolment counts
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **cohort component** method ages each cohort forward, applies **survival rates**, adds **births** (from ASFRs) and adjusts for **migration**. **Mathematical** methods (linear, exponential, logistic) are used for small units.
SC: Cohort method = births + deaths + migration, cohort by cohort.

Q: An Urban Agglomeration (UA) in the Census consists of:
A) a continuous urban spread of a town and its adjoining outgrowths, or two or more physically contiguous towns
B) any district headquarters
C) all villages within 10 km of a city
D) a municipal corporation only
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: A **UA** is a continuous urban spread: a **town + outgrowths (OGs)**, or **contiguous towns** with or without OGs. In 2011 the core had to be a **statutory town** and the UA needed a population of **at least 20,000**.
SC: A UA is contiguous urban spread around a statutory core.

Q: In the Census, the Houselisting and Housing Census collects information mainly on:
A) building use, housing condition, amenities and assets of households
B) individual age, education and migration
C) crop production
D) enterprises and employment by industry
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Houselisting** covers **buildings, census houses, amenities** (water, sanitation, fuel, electricity) and **assets**, and builds the **frame** for enumeration. **Population enumeration** collects the **individual** characteristics.
SC: Houselisting covers houses and amenities; enumeration covers persons.

Q: Assertion (A): Census data are used as the sampling frame for most household surveys in India.
Reason (R): The Census provides a complete list of villages and enumeration blocks with population counts.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Easy
EXP: The **Census village list and CEBs** give the **rural frame** (and NFHS's urban frame). NSS urban frames use **UFS blocks**. The complete listing in R is why the Census serves as the frame.
SC: A complete Census list makes the Census the frame.

Q: The sex ratio at birth (SRB) as per SRS is expressed in India as:
A) females born per 1,000 males born
B) males born per 100 females born
C) females per 1,000 males in the total population
D) girls aged 0-6 per 1,000 boys aged 0-6
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: India reports **SRB as females per 1,000 males born** (SRS, CRS, NFHS). The biologically normal SRB is about **950** by this convention (about 105 boys per 100 girls).
SC: SRB is girls per 1,000 boys at birth; a natural level is about 950.

Q: The Census sex ratio of India increased between 2001 and 2011 while the child sex ratio declined. This divergence is best explained by:
A) improving female survival at older ages alongside sex-selective practices at birth and in early childhood
B) emigration of men only
C) a change in the definition of sex ratio
D) undercounting of women in 2001
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: The **overall sex ratio (933 → 943)** rose as adult female survival and life expectancy improved. The **child sex ratio (927 → 918)** fell, reflecting **sex selection** at birth and **excess girl-child mortality**.
SC: Adult gains lifted the overall ratio while losses at birth cut the child ratio.
