@topic SNA Production Boundary
Q: Under SNA 2008, which of the following is INCLUDED in the production boundary (and hence GDP)?
A) Imputed rent of owner-occupied dwellings
B) Unpaid household cooking and cleaning by family members
C) Volunteer services within one's own household
D) Leisure activities
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: SNA includes **owner-occupied housing services** (imputed rent) and **own-account production of goods** (e.g. farm produce kept for home use, own-account construction). It **excludes** **own-account household services** such as unpaid cooking and cleaning, but includes **paid domestic staff**.
SC: Own-account goods are in; own-account household services are out, except imputed rent.

Q: Which of the following is EXCLUDED from national income?
A) Old-age pensions paid by the government
B) Wages of government employees
C) Value of farm produce retained for self-consumption
D) Imputed rent of owner-occupied houses
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Transfer payments** (pensions, scholarships, unemployment doles) have **no production counterpart**, so they are excluded. Wages of government employees count as **compensation**. **Retained farm produce** and **imputed rent** are included.
SC: Transfers are excluded; production for own use is included.

Q: In national income accounting, the sale of a second-hand car through a dealer contributes to current GDP only through:
A) the dealer's commission (service value added)
B) the full resale price
C) the original price of the car
D) nothing at all, including commission
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: A second-hand good was **counted when first produced**. Only the **dealer's service (commission or margin)** in the current period is new production. The same logic applies to **shares**: brokerage counts, but the share purchase is not investment in the GDP sense.
SC: For old goods, only the middleman's margin is new output.

Q: FISIM in national accounts refers to:
A) Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured, the implicit charge for intermediation earned through interest margins
B) a tax on financial transactions
C) foreign investment in securities market
D) financial inclusion index
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Banks charge implicitly by **borrowing low and lending high**. **FISIM** measures this service output, and **SNA 2008 recommends allocating it to user sectors**. Without it, bank value added would be understated.
SC: FISIM = the service value hidden in interest margins.

Q: The five institutional sectors of the SNA are:
A) non-financial corporations, financial corporations, general government, households, and NPISHs
B) agriculture, industry, services, government, and rest of world
C) public, private, joint, cooperative, and household
D) primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary, and government
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **institutional sectors** are **NFC, FC, GG, households and NPISH** (non-profit institutions serving households). The **rest of the world** is the counterpart for transactions with non-residents. Agriculture, industry and services are **industries**, not institutional sectors.
SC: Five sectors: NFC, FC, GG, HH, NPISH.

Q: "Mixed income" in the income approach to GDP refers to:
A) the surplus of unincorporated enterprises owned by households, which mixes returns to labour and capital
B) income from two jobs
C) government transfers plus wages
D) profits of joint ventures
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: For **self-employed / household enterprises**, owner labour and capital returns can't be separated, so their surplus is **mixed income**. Corporations generate **operating surplus**.
SC: The self-employed earn mixed income; firms earn operating surplus.

Q: Compensation of employees in the national accounts includes:
A) wages and salaries in cash and kind plus employers' social contributions
B) only cash wages
C) wages plus mixed income
D) wages plus pensions paid by the government
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **CE = wages and salaries (cash and kind) + employer's social contributions** (PF and similar). Government pensions to retirees are **transfers**.
SC: CE = pay + employer contributions.

@topic 2011-12 Series Features
Q: Which of the following was a key change introduced with the 2011-12 base-year series of national accounts?
A) Sectoral estimates presented as GVA at basic prices, and GDP at market prices as the headline measure
B) Abolition of the production approach
C) Use of the 1993 SNA
D) Exclusion of the unorganised sector
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The 2011-12 series moved from **GDP at factor cost** to **GVA at basic prices** (industry-wise) and **GDP at market prices** (headline). It adopted **SNA 2008** and used the **MCA-21 corporate database**.
SC: The 2011-12 series moved from factor cost to basic prices.

Q: In the 2011-12 series, estimates for the private corporate sector were based largely on:
A) the MCA-21 database of company filings with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs
B) the RBI sample of about 2,500 companies only
C) the Economic Census
D) the ASI alone
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **MCA-21** database of **company accounts** was used, moving to an **enterprise approach** for corporate manufacturing and services. Earlier series relied on the **RBI's company sample** and, for manufacturing, the **ASI** (establishments).
SC: The 2011-12 series used MCA-21 company data, an enterprise approach.

Q: In the 2011-12 series, the "effective labour input" method was used for:
A) estimating value added of the unorganised (household) segments, weighting workers by relative productivity
B) the corporate sector
C) government administration
D) the agriculture sector's crop output
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: For **unorganised segments**, the new series used **effective labour input**, weighting **owner, hired and helper** workers by their **relative productivity**. This improved on the simple labour input method.
SC: Unorganised GVA uses productivity-weighted labour.

@topic Deflation & Real Estimates
Q: The GDP deflator is calculated as:
A) nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100
B) real GDP ÷ nominal GDP × 100
C) CPI ÷ WPI × 100
D) (nominal GDP − real GDP) ÷ real GDP
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Deflator = nominal / real × 100**. It is **implicit** and has **current weights** (Paasche-type), and it covers **all domestic output**, including services.
SC: Deflator = nominal ÷ real.

Q: Nominal GDP rises by 10% and the GDP deflator rises by 4%. Real GDP growth is approximately:
A) 5.8%
B) 6.0%
C) 14.0%
D) 2.5%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: The exact figure is **1.10 / 1.04 − 1 = 5.77% ≈ 5.8%**. The approximation 10 − 4 = 6% is close but slightly high.
SC: Real growth = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1.

Q: "Double deflation" in estimating real value added means:
A) deflating output and intermediate inputs separately by their own price indices and then subtracting
B) deflating GVA twice by the same index
C) deflating by both CPI and WPI and averaging
D) deflating both GDP and population
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Double deflation**: real GVA = real output − real inputs. It is preferred when **output and input prices diverge**. **Single deflation** deflates GVA directly by an output price index, which is simpler and widely used in India for lack of input price indices.
SC: Double deflation deflates output and inputs separately.

Q: Official per capita income in India refers to:
A) per capita Net National Income
B) per capita GDP at market prices
C) per capita GVA at basic prices
D) per capita disposable income of households
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The official **per capita income** is **NNI ÷ population**, reported at current and constant prices. Per capita GDP is used in some international comparisons.
SC: Per capita income = NNI ÷ population.

Q: The net capital stock and consumption of fixed capital in India's national accounts are estimated mainly using:
A) the Perpetual Inventory Method (PIM)
B) the Economic Census
C) direct valuation surveys every year
D) the Laspeyres index
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **PIM** cumulates past **GFCF** (deflated) and subtracts **retirements and depreciation** by asset life. It gives **capital stock and CFC** estimates.
SC: Capital stock is accumulated GFCF minus retirements (PIM).

@topic Input-Output Analysis
Q: In an input-output table, the technical (input) coefficient a_ij is defined as:
A) the input from sector i required per unit of output of sector j (x_ij ÷ X_j)
B) the output of sector i sold to final demand
C) x_ij ÷ X_i
D) the value added of sector j
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **a_ij = x_ij / X_j**, the input from i per unit of j's output. The matrix **A** of these coefficients leads to the **Leontief inverse**.
SC: The column sector's output is the denominator.

Q: In the Leontief open model, the gross output vector X required to meet final demand F is given by:
A) X = (I − A)⁻¹ F
B) X = (I + A) F
C) X = A⁻¹ F
D) X = (A − I) F
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: From **X = AX + F**, we get **X = (I − A)⁻¹F**. The **Leontief inverse** gives **total (direct + indirect)** requirements, and its column sums are **output multipliers**.
SC: X = (I − A)⁻¹F: the Leontief inverse times final demand.

Q: The Hawkins-Simon condition in input-output analysis ensures:
A) that the system is viable, producing non-negative outputs for non-negative final demands
B) that prices are constant
C) that the table is at factor cost
D) that the number of sectors is finite
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **Hawkins-Simon**: all **leading principal minors of (I − A)** are positive. The economy can then produce a **surplus** and outputs are non-negative.
SC: Hawkins-Simon tests whether the I-O system is viable.

@topic Satellite, State & District Accounts
Q: Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) estimates are primarily compiled by:
A) the State Directorates of Economics and Statistics
B) the RBI
C) the Finance Commission
D) NITI Aayog
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **State DESs** compile **GSDP/GSVA** following NSO methodology, and NSO (NAD) coordinates for **comparability**. **District Domestic Product** estimates are also prepared by DESs.
SC: State income comes from State DESs, harmonised by NAD.

Q: A "satellite account" in national accounting is:
A) an extension of the core accounts for a specific area (e.g. tourism, health, environment) without altering the central framework
B) an account of satellite launches
C) a set of accounts for foreign branches
D) a quarterly revision of GDP
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Satellite accounts** (the **Tourism Satellite Account**, health accounts, **SEEA**) elaborate a field consistently with the SNA but **outside** the core accounts.
SC: Satellite accounts orbit the core SNA without changing it.

Q: India's Tourism Satellite Account (TSA) estimates the contribution of tourism to:
A) GDP and employment
B) only foreign exchange
C) only domestic trips
D) only hotel revenues
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **TSA**, prepared for the **Ministry of Tourism** (by NCAER), estimates **tourism direct and indirect GVA/GDP and jobs** using the SNA-consistent framework.
SC: The TSA measures tourism's GDP and jobs.

@topic Concepts & Numericals
Q: GDP at market prices = ₹200 lakh crore; net factor income from abroad = −₹2 lakh crore; consumption of fixed capital = ₹22 lakh crore; product taxes less product subsidies = ₹18 lakh crore. Net National Income at basic prices is closest to:
A) ₹158 lakh crore
B) ₹176 lakh crore
C) ₹180 lakh crore
D) ₹198 lakh crore
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Hard
EXP: **GNI = 200 − 2 = 198**. **NNI (market prices) = 198 − 22 = 176**. Removing **net product taxes** gives **176 − 18 = 158** at basic prices.
SC: GDP, then add NFIA, then subtract CFC, then subtract net product taxes.

Q: In an economy, PFCE = 60, GFCE = 12, GFCF = 30, change in stocks = 2, valuables = 1, exports = 20, imports = 25 (all ₹ lakh crore), and discrepancies are nil. GDP by the expenditure approach is:
A) 100
B) 150
C) 125
D) 95
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **GDP = PFCE + GFCE + GFCF + CIS + valuables + X − M = 60 + 12 + 30 + 2 + 1 + 20 − 25 = 100**.
SC: C + G + I (including stocks and valuables) + X − M.

Q: Which of the following statements about GDP and GNI is correct?
A) GNI exceeds GDP when net factor income from abroad is positive
B) GNI always exceeds GDP
C) GDP includes income of Indian residents working abroad
D) GNI measures production within territorial boundaries
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: **GNI = GDP + NFIA**. For India, **NFIA is negative** (net payments of interest, dividends and profits), so **GNI < GDP**.
SC: If NFIA is negative, GNI < GDP; for India, it is.

Q: The "value added method" avoids double counting by:
A) counting only the value added at each stage (output minus intermediate consumption)
B) counting only final goods sold to foreigners
C) counting intermediate goods twice
D) excluding services
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Summing **value added** across stages equals the value of **final goods**. Adding gross outputs would count intermediate goods repeatedly.
SC: Add value added, not gross sales.

Q: Assertion (A): In India's GDP by expenditure, a "discrepancies" item appears.
Reason (R): GDP estimated from the production side and the sum of expenditure components are compiled from different data sources and do not match exactly.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Medium
EXP: The **production-side GDP** is taken as the benchmark, and the expenditure side is estimated independently. The difference is shown as **discrepancies**. R explains A.
SC: The two approaches use different sources, so a balancing discrepancy appears.

Q: Household sector saving in India's national accounts consists of:
A) net financial saving plus saving in physical assets (and valuables)
B) only bank deposits
C) only gold purchases
D) only contributions to provident funds
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Household saving = net financial saving (gross financial assets − financial liabilities) + physical assets (dwellings, machinery) + valuables**. The **RBI** helps estimate the financial component.
SC: Household saving = financial (net of borrowing) + physical + gold.

Q: The gross domestic saving rate is expressed as:
A) gross saving as a percentage of GDP (or GNDI)
B) household saving ÷ household income
C) bank deposits ÷ GDP
D) GFCF ÷ GDP
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **saving rate** is **gross saving / GDP** (or GNDI). The **investment rate** is **GCF / GDP**, and the gap between them is covered by **net capital inflow**, which equals the current account deficit.
SC: Saving rate = saving ÷ GDP; investment rate = GCF ÷ GDP.

Q: Which of the following is NOT part of Gross Fixed Capital Formation?
A) Purchase of existing shares on the stock exchange
B) Construction of a new factory building
C) Purchase of new machinery
D) Own-account software development
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Easy
EXP: **Share purchases** are **financial transactions**, not the creation of new capital goods. GFCF includes **new buildings, machinery and intellectual property products** such as software and R&D.
SC: Buying paper assets is not investment in GDP terms.

Q: Under the 2008 SNA, expenditure on research and development is treated as:
A) capital formation (intellectual property product)
B) intermediate consumption
C) a transfer payment
D) excluded from accounts
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **SNA 2008** capitalises **R&D** as **intellectual property products** (with software, databases, mineral exploration and entertainment originals). SNA 1993 treated R&D as intermediate consumption.
SC: SNA 2008 treats R&D as investment.

Q: Which of the following is a correct statement about GVA at factor cost vs GVA at basic prices?
A) GVA at basic prices = GVA at factor cost + production taxes − production subsidies
B) GVA at factor cost = GVA at basic prices + production taxes
C) They are always equal
D) GVA at basic prices includes product taxes
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Factor cost + net production taxes = basic prices**. Adding **net product taxes** gives **market prices**.
SC: Factor cost → basic price (add production taxes) → market price (add product taxes).

Q: Gross National Disposable Income (GNDI) differs from GNI in that:
A) GNDI adds net current transfers from abroad (such as remittances) to GNI
B) GNDI subtracts depreciation
C) GNDI excludes taxes
D) GNDI equals GDP
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **GNDI = GNI + net current transfers from the rest of the world**, such as private remittances and grants. India is among the **world's largest remittance recipients**, so GNDI > GNI.
SC: GNDI adds remittances to GNI.

Q: The first official estimates of India's national income after Independence, for 1948-49, were prepared by:
A) the National Income Committee chaired by P.C. Mahalanobis
B) the RBI
C) V.K.R.V. Rao alone
D) the Planning Commission
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **NIC (1949)**, chaired by **Mahalanobis** with **Gadgil and V.K.R.V. Rao**, produced the first reports (1951, 1954). **Dadabhai Naoroji** (1867-68) and **V.K.R.V. Rao** (1931-32) made earlier non-official estimates.
SC: Naoroji and Rao made early estimates; Mahalanobis's committee made the first official ones.

Q: Dadabhai Naoroji's early estimate of India's per capita income appeared in his work:
A) Poverty and Un-British Rule in India
B) The Wealth of Nations
C) The National Income of British India
D) Planning and the Poor
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Naoroji** estimated per capita income for **1867-68** (about ₹20) in **"Poverty and Un-British Rule in India"**. **V.K.R.V. Rao's "The National Income of British India"** (1931-32) was the first scientific estimate.
SC: Naoroji wrote Poverty and Un-British Rule; Rao wrote National Income of British India.

Q: Constant-price estimates of GDP are used to measure:
A) real growth, by removing the effect of price changes
B) inflation
C) the size of the government
D) exchange rate movements
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Valuing output at **base-year prices** removes price effects, so changes reflect **volume**, i.e. **real growth**. The ratio of current to constant values is the **deflator**.
SC: Constant prices give volume, and hence real growth.

Q: Which of the following is recorded as final consumption of general government in national accounts?
A) Salaries of teachers in government schools providing free education
B) Interest paid on public debt
C) Subsidies to fertiliser companies
D) Old-age pensions
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **GFCE** is the government's own **production of non-market services** (valued at cost: CE + CFC + IC − sales), such as free education. **Interest, subsidies and pensions** are **transfers or property income**, not consumption.
SC: GFCE is services the government produces; transfers are not consumption.

Q: Net National Product at factor cost is obtained from Net National Product at market prices by:
A) subtracting net indirect taxes (indirect taxes minus subsidies)
B) adding depreciation
C) subtracting net factor income from abroad
D) adding transfer payments
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **NNP(FC) = NNP(MP) − (indirect taxes − subsidies)**. NNP at factor cost is the textbook **national income**.
SC: Market price minus net indirect taxes gives factor cost.

Q: Personal disposable income is obtained from personal income by deducting:
A) direct taxes paid by households and miscellaneous fees and fines
B) indirect taxes
C) depreciation
D) retained corporate profits
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Personal income** = national income − undistributed profits − corporate tax − employee social contributions + transfers. **PDI = personal income − personal direct taxes − fees and fines**. PDI is split between **consumption and saving**.
SC: PDI = PI − direct taxes.

Q: If NNI at current prices is ₹240 lakh crore and the population is 140 crore, per capita income is about:
A) ₹1.71 lakh
B) ₹17,100
C) ₹5.8 lakh
D) ₹58,000
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **240 × 10¹² ÷ 140 × 10⁷ = ₹1,71,428**, about **₹1.71 lakh**.
SC: Divide lakh crore by crore, and watch the powers of ten.

Q: Fiscal ratios such as the fiscal deficit to GDP ratio and the debt to GDP ratio use:
A) GDP at current market prices (nominal GDP)
B) GDP at constant prices
C) GVA at basic prices
D) NNI at constant prices
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Deficits and debt are **nominal** amounts, so they are scaled by **nominal GDP**. **Real GDP** growth is used for **growth** analysis.
SC: Nominal ratios need nominal GDP.

Q: When the base year of national accounts is revised, the level of GDP usually changes because:
A) new data sources, a wider coverage and updated weights and methods are incorporated
B) population has changed
C) the currency is redenominated
D) inflation is excluded
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: Base revisions bring in **new surveys, censuses and administrative data** (such as MCA-21), **updated weights** and **methodology**, so both **levels** and **growth rates** change.
SC: A new base brings new data and methods, and a new GDP level.
