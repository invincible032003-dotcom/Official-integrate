@topic Monetary Aggregates
Q: Reserve money (M0) consists of:
A) currency in circulation + bankers' deposits with the RBI + other deposits with the RBI
B) currency with the public + demand deposits with banks
C) M1 + time deposits with banks
D) currency with the public only
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **M0 (reserve money, high-powered money) = currency in circulation + bankers' deposits with the RBI + other deposits with the RBI**. M1 and M3 are measures held by the public.
SC: M0 is the RBI's monetary liabilities.

Q: Broad money (M3) in India is defined as:
A) M1 + time deposits with banks
B) M1 + savings deposits with post office savings banks
C) M3 + total post office deposits
D) currency + bankers' deposits with the RBI
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **M1 = currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI**. **M2 = M1 + post office savings deposits**. **M3 = M1 + time deposits with banks** (broad money). **M4 = M3 + total post office deposits** (excluding NSCs).
SC: M3 = M1 + bank time deposits.

Q: The new monetary aggregates (NM1, NM2, NM3) and liquidity aggregates (L1, L2, L3) were recommended by the Working Group on Money Supply chaired by:
A) Y.V. Reddy (1998)
B) C. Rangarajan (2000)
C) Sukhamoy Chakravarty (1985)
D) Urjit Patel (2014)
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The **Third Working Group on Money Supply (Y.V. Reddy, 1998)** introduced **NM aggregates** (based on a residency and liquidity approach) and **L1-L3**. **Chakravarty (1985)** reviewed the monetary system and recommended **monetary targeting**. **Urjit Patel (2014)** recommended **CPI inflation targeting**.
SC: Reddy: new aggregates; Chakravarty: monetary targeting; Patel: CPI targeting.

Q: The money multiplier is defined as:
A) the ratio of broad money (M3) to reserve money (M0)
B) the ratio of M0 to M3
C) the growth of M3 minus the growth of GDP
D) the repo rate divided by the CRR
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Money multiplier = M3 ÷ M0**. It depends on the **currency-deposit ratio** and the **reserve ratio**. Higher CRR or currency preference **lowers** it.
SC: Multiplier = broad money ÷ reserve money.

Q: Basic Statistical Returns (BSR), which provide granular data on bank credit and deposits by sector, occupation and region, are compiled by:
A) Reserve Bank of India
B) Indian Banks' Association
C) NSO
D) NABARD
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **RBI's BSR system** (BSR-1 on credit, BSR-2 on deposits) gives **account-level** banking statistics by **district, population group and sector**. It feeds the Handbook and other RBI publications.
SC: BSR is the RBI's granular banking statistics.

Q: The RBI's Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index), launched in 2021, has three parameters. Which carries the highest weight?
A) Usage
B) Access
C) Quality
D) Equity
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The **FI-Index** (0-100) weights **Access 35%**, **Usage 45%** and **Quality 20%** (financial literacy, consumer protection, inequalities). It is published annually for each March.
SC: FI-Index weights: Access 35, Usage 45, Quality 20.

@topic Fiscal Statistics
Q: The fiscal deficit of the Government is defined as:
A) total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts)
B) revenue expenditure − revenue receipts
C) fiscal deficit − interest payments
D) total expenditure − tax revenue
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts)**. It equals **total borrowing**. **Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts**. **Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest payments**.
SC: FD equals borrowing; PD = FD − interest.

Q: The effective revenue deficit is:
A) revenue deficit − grants for creation of capital assets
B) revenue deficit + interest payments
C) fiscal deficit − revenue deficit
D) revenue deficit − subsidies
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Effective revenue deficit = RD − grants for creation of capital assets** (grants to States and others that create assets). The concept was introduced in the **2011-12 Budget** and via FRBM amendments.
SC: ERD strips capital-asset grants out of the RD.

Q: The monthly accounts of the Union Government (receipts, expenditure and deficit) are published by:
A) Controller General of Accounts, Ministry of Finance
B) Comptroller and Auditor General
C) RBI
D) NSO
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **CGA** publishes **monthly accounts** of the Union Government. The **CAG** audits accounts. Budget documents come from **DEA**, and the **RBI** studies **State finances**.
SC: The CGA compiles accounts; the CAG audits them.

Q: The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act was enacted in:
A) 2003
B) 1991
C) 2008
D) 2017
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **FRBM Act 2003** set fiscal deficit and revenue deficit targets. The **N.K. Singh Committee (2017)** recommended a **debt anchor** (general government debt at 60% of GDP) and fiscal deficit as the operational target.
SC: FRBM 2003; the N.K. Singh review in 2017.

Q: A government has total expenditure ₹50 lakh crore, revenue receipts ₹30 lakh crore, non-debt capital receipts ₹2 lakh crore and interest payments ₹10 lakh crore. Its primary deficit is:
A) ₹8 lakh crore
B) ₹18 lakh crore
C) ₹20 lakh crore
D) ₹10 lakh crore
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **FD = 50 − (30 + 2) = 18**. **PD = 18 − 10 = 8** lakh crore. The primary deficit shows borrowing for **current needs** excluding past debt costs.
SC: PD = FD − interest.

@topic External Sector Statistics
Q: The balance of payments of India is compiled and published by:
A) RBI, quarterly, following BPM6
B) DGCI&S, monthly
C) Ministry of Commerce, annually
D) NSO, quarterly
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **RBI compiles the BoP quarterly** using **IMF BPM6**. DGCI&S publishes **merchandise trade** from customs data, which differs in **coverage and timing** from BoP goods.
SC: The RBI compiles the BoP quarterly on BPM6.

Q: Under BPM6, the current account of the balance of payments comprises:
A) goods, services, primary income and secondary income
B) goods, services, FDI and portfolio investment
C) goods and capital transfers only
D) services, loans and banking capital
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Current account = goods + services + primary income** (compensation, investment income) **+ secondary income** (current transfers such as remittances). FDI, portfolio, loans and banking go in the **financial account**.
SC: Current account = trade + income + transfers; the financial account holds investment flows.

Q: In India's BoP presentation, "invisibles" comprise:
A) services, primary income and secondary income (transfers)
B) only services
C) FDI and portfolio investment
D) gold imports
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Invisibles = services + income + transfers**. India's **invisibles surplus** (IT services exports, remittances) **offsets much of the merchandise trade deficit**.
SC: Invisibles are everything in the current account except goods.

Q: The net barter terms of trade are defined as:
A) (export unit value index ÷ import unit value index) × 100
B) (import price index ÷ export price index) × 100
C) export volume index × import volume index
D) exports ÷ imports in value terms
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **net barter ToT = export prices ÷ import prices × 100**. The **income ToT = net barter ToT × export volume index ÷ 100**. DGCI&S unit value indices support these.
SC: Terms of trade = export prices ÷ import prices.

Q: An increase in India's Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) index indicates:
A) a real appreciation of the rupee, implying a loss of external competitiveness
B) a real depreciation of the rupee
C) an increase in forex reserves
D) a fall in inflation
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The RBI's **NEER/REER** indices (6-currency and 40-currency baskets) are defined so that a **rise means appreciation**. The **REER** adjusts the NEER for **relative prices**.
SC: A rising REER means a real appreciation and weaker competitiveness.

Q: Data on FDI equity inflows by country and sector are released by:
A) DPIIT (and the RBI for BoP-based FDI)
B) SEBI
C) NSO
D) Ministry of External Affairs
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **DPIIT** publishes the **FDI Factsheet** (equity inflows by country and sector). The **RBI** reports FDI in the **BoP**, which also includes reinvested earnings and other capital. **SEBI** tracks **FPI** registrations.
SC: DPIIT publishes the FDI factsheet; the RBI publishes BoP FDI.

Q: The trade openness of an economy is commonly measured as:
A) (exports + imports) ÷ GDP
B) exports ÷ imports
C) current account deficit ÷ GDP
D) forex reserves ÷ imports
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Openness = (X + M) ÷ GDP**. **Import cover of reserves** is reserves ÷ monthly imports.
SC: Openness = (X + M) ÷ GDP.

Q: Assertion (A): Merchandise trade data of DGCI&S and the goods account of the RBI's BoP often differ.
Reason (R): They differ in coverage, timing and valuation, since DGCI&S records customs movements while the BoP records transactions between residents and non-residents.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Medium
EXP: **Customs records** (DGCI&S) differ from **BoP transactions** (RBI, change of ownership) in **timing, coverage** (e.g. defence imports, merchanting) and **valuation**. R explains A.
SC: Customs movement and change of ownership are different concepts.

@topic Energy Statistics
Q: One tonne of oil equivalent (toe) is conventionally equal to:
A) 41.868 gigajoules
B) 3.6 megajoules
C) 1,000 kWh
D) 10 megajoules
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **1 toe = 41.868 GJ = 10⁷ kcal** (about 11.63 MWh). **1 kWh = 3.6 MJ**. Energy Statistics India reports in **petajoules and KToE**.
SC: 1 toe = 41.868 GJ; 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ.

Q: 1 kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity is equal to:
A) 3.6 megajoules
B) 1 megajoule
C) 36 megajoules
D) 0.36 megajoules
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 3.6 MJ**.
SC: kW × hour = 1000 × 3600 J.

Q: In India's total primary energy supply, the single largest source is:
A) coal
B) crude oil
C) natural gas
D) hydro power
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Coal** is the largest source of primary energy supply, and dominates electricity generation, followed by **oil**. Energy Statistics India (MoSPI) presents this in the **energy balance**.
SC: Coal is India's largest energy source.

Q: An energy balance, as compiled in Energy Statistics India, shows:
A) supply, transformation and final consumption of all energy products in a common unit
B) the monthly electricity tariffs of States
C) only renewable energy capacity
D) the prices of petroleum products
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **energy balance** tracks **production + imports − exports ± stocks** (supply), then **transformation** (power plants, refineries), **losses** and **final consumption by sector**, all in a **common energy unit**.
SC: The energy balance is a supply-to-use table in joules.

Q: Installed capacity and generation statistics of renewable energy in India are primarily compiled by:
A) Central Electricity Authority and the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy
B) NSO only
C) Bureau of Indian Standards
D) Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **CEA** (generation, capacity) and **MNRE** (renewables programmes, capacity) are the primary sources. **PPAC** covers **petroleum** data, and **NSO** consolidates everything in Energy Statistics India.
SC: Power data come from the CEA and MNRE; oil data from PPAC.

@topic Housing, Environment & Other Indices
Q: NHB RESIDEX, India's first official residential housing price index, was launched by the National Housing Bank in:
A) 2007
B) 1991
C) 2015
D) 2001
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The **NHB RESIDEX** was launched in **2007** and covers residential prices across cities. The **RBI** separately compiles a **House Price Index** from registration data for major cities.

Q: As per the India State of Forest Report 2021, the total forest cover of India was about:
A) 21.7% of geographical area
B) 33% of geographical area
C) 15% of geographical area
D) 28% of geographical area
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **ISFR 2021**: **forest cover about 21.71%**, and **forest + tree cover about 24.62%**. The **National Forest Policy (1988)** goal is **33%** of geographical area under forest or tree cover.
SC: Forest cover is about 21.7%; the policy target is 33%.
TIP: ISFR 2023 put forest and tree cover at just over 25%. Check the latest ISFR before the exam.

Q: The National Forest Policy, 1988 aims for what proportion of the country's geographical area under forest or tree cover?
A) One-third (33%)
B) One-fourth (25%)
C) One-half (50%)
D) One-fifth (20%)
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The target is **33% nationally**, with **two-thirds** in hills and mountainous areas. The FSI's ISFR tracks progress.
SC: Forest target: one-third of land, two-thirds in hills.

Q: Ground water resource assessments in India are carried out jointly by:
A) Central Ground Water Board and State ground water departments
B) India Meteorological Department
C) NSO
D) National Remote Sensing Centre alone
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **CGWB (Ministry of Jal Shakti)** and State departments assess **dynamic ground water resources**, classifying blocks as **safe, semi-critical, critical or over-exploited**.
SC: Groundwater is assessed by the CGWB with the States.

Q: Which of the following agencies publishes insurance sector statistics in India?
A) IRDAI
B) SEBI
C) PFRDA
D) RBI
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The regulators each publish their sector's data: **IRDAI** (insurance), **SEBI** (securities), **PFRDA** (pensions, NPS) and **RBI** (banking, money).
SC: Each financial regulator publishes its own sector's data.

@topic Numericals & Concepts
Q: If M3 is ₹240 lakh crore and M0 is ₹48 lakh crore, the money multiplier is:
A) 5
B) 4.8
C) 0.2
D) 192
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: **Money multiplier = 240 ÷ 48 = 5**.
SC: Multiplier = M3 ÷ M0.

Q: The export unit value index rises from 100 to 120 and the import unit value index from 100 to 150. The net barter terms of trade:
A) fall to 80, indicating a deterioration
B) rise to 125
C) remain at 100
D) rise to 150
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: **ToT = 120 ÷ 150 × 100 = 80**. Import prices rose faster than export prices, so the terms of trade **deteriorated**.
SC: ToT below 100 means worse terms.

Q: Which of the following is a component of the capital account (as distinct from the financial account) under BPM6?
A) Capital transfers and acquisition/disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets
B) Foreign direct investment
C) External commercial borrowings
D) Portfolio investment
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **BPM6** separates a narrow **capital account** (capital transfers, non-produced non-financial assets) from the **financial account** (FDI, portfolio, other investment, reserves). In India's presentation, the "capital account" often loosely includes financial flows.
SC: The BPM6 capital account is tiny: capital transfers and non-produced assets.

Q: The status report on India's external debt is published annually by:
A) Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs), with quarterly data from the RBI
B) SEBI
C) Controller General of Accounts
D) NITI Aayog
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **DEA** publishes **"India's External Debt: A Status Report"** annually, and the **RBI** releases **quarterly external debt** data. The **Status Paper on Government Debt** is also from the **Ministry of Finance**.
SC: External debt reports come from MoF (annual) and the RBI (quarterly).

Q: A country's forex reserves are USD 600 billion and its annual merchandise imports are USD 720 billion. The import cover of reserves is about:
A) 10 months
B) 12 months
C) 6 months
D) 1.2 years
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: **Monthly imports = 720 ÷ 12 = 60**, so **cover = 600 ÷ 60 = 10 months**.
SC: Cover = reserves ÷ monthly imports.

Q: Which statement about the tax-GDP ratio is correct?
A) It is total tax revenue as a percentage of GDP at current market prices
B) It uses real GDP
C) It excludes direct taxes
D) It is computed on GVA at basic prices
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: The **tax-GDP ratio = tax revenue ÷ nominal GDP**. India's **combined (Centre + States)** ratio is commonly reported alongside the Centre's gross tax-GDP ratio.
SC: Nominal tax ÷ nominal GDP.

Q: Which of the following monetary aggregates is the most liquid?
A) M1
B) M2
C) M3
D) M4
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Liquidity **declines from M1 to M4**, because **time deposits** (M3) and **post office deposits** (M4) are less liquid. **M1** is money in its narrowest, most liquid sense.
SC: M1 is the most liquid; M4 the least.

@topic Energy Statistics
Q: Energy intensity of an economy is measured as:
A) energy consumption per unit of GDP
B) GDP per unit of energy consumption
C) energy consumption per capita
D) the share of renewables in energy supply
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Energy intensity = energy used ÷ GDP** (e.g. MJ per rupee of GDP). A **falling** intensity means more output per unit of energy. **Per capita consumption** is a separate indicator reported in Energy Statistics India.
SC: Intensity = energy ÷ GDP; lower is more efficient.

@topic External Sector Statistics
Q: A country has a current account deficit of USD 40 billion and GDP of USD 3.2 trillion. The CAD as a percentage of GDP is:
A) 1.25%
B) 12.5%
C) 0.125%
D) 4.0%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: **40 ÷ 3,200 × 100 = 1.25%**. CAD ratios around or below 2-2.5% of GDP are generally considered sustainable for India.
SC: Convert both to the same unit, then divide.
