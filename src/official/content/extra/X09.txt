@topic PLFS: Recent Results & Revamp
Q: As per the PLFS annual report for July 2023 – June 2024, the unemployment rate (usual status, ps+ss, age 15+) in India was about:
A) 3.2%
B) 6.1%
C) 4.8%
D) 7.8%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **PLFS UR (15+, usual status)** fell from **6.1% (2017-18)** to **3.2% (2022-23 and 2023-24)**. Over the same period **LFPR rose to about 60%** and **WPR to about 58%** in 2023-24.
SC: PLFS UR: 6.1% (2017-18) → 3.2% (2023-24).

Q: Which PLFS round recorded the highest unemployment rate (usual status, 15+) since PLFS began, widely reported as a multi-decade high?
A) 2017-18 (6.1%)
B) 2019-20
C) 2020-21
D) 2023-24
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **first PLFS (2017-18)** showed **6.1%**. Later rounds fell steadily: 5.8, 4.8, 4.2, 4.1 and then **3.2**. Note that PLFS is **not strictly comparable** with the older NSS EUS rounds.
SC: The first PLFS had the highest UR, 6.1%.

Q: The female Labour Force Participation Rate (15+, usual status) in PLFS rose sharply between 2017-18 and 2023-24, reaching about:
A) 42%
B) 23%
C) 60%
D) 30%
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The **female LFPR (15+, usual status)** rose from about **23% (2017-18)** to about **42% (2023-24)**. The rise was driven mainly by **rural women**, many as **self-employed helpers** in household enterprises.
SC: Female LFPR roughly doubled (about 23% → 42%), led by rural self-employment.

Q: The revamped PLFS design introduced from January 2025 provides:
A) monthly estimates of key indicators in CWS for rural and urban areas, and quarterly estimates for both rural and urban areas
B) only annual estimates for urban areas
C) estimates only from administrative EPFO data
D) district-level monthly estimates of wages
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **revamped PLFS (from January 2025)** gives **monthly** all-India **CWS** estimates (LFPR, WPR, UR) and **quarterly** estimates covering **rural + urban**. Earlier, quarterly bulletins covered **urban only**. Sample size and rotation were redesigned for this.
SC: New PLFS: monthly and quarterly estimates for rural + urban (earlier quarterly urban only).

@topic Vital & Health Trends
Q: As per SRS, India's Maternal Mortality Ratio for 2018-20 was:
A) 97 per lakh live births
B) 130 per lakh live births
C) 167 per lakh live births
D) 70 per lakh live births
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The SRS MMR fell from **167 (2011-13)** to **130 (2014-16)**, **113 (2016-18)**, **103 (2017-19)** and **97 (2018-20)**, with **93 (2019-21)** in a later bulletin. This met the **NHP 2017 target of 100 by 2020**. The SDG target is **below 70 by 2030**.
SC: MMR: 167 → 130 → 113 → 103 → 97 → 93.

Q: As per the SRS Statistical Report 2021, India's IMR and TFR were respectively:
A) 27 per 1,000 live births and 2.0
B) 32 and 2.2
C) 30 and 2.1
D) 22 and 1.8
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **SRS 2021: IMR 27, TFR 2.0**. The **CDR rose to about 7.5** in 2021 because of COVID-19 mortality. IMR has declined steadily (32 in 2018, 30 in 2019, 28 in 2020).
SC: SRS 2021: IMR 27, TFR 2.0, CDR up because of COVID.

Q: The sixth round of the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-6) had its fieldwork during:
A) 2023-24
B) 2019-21
C) 2021-22
D) 2025-26
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **NFHS-6** fieldwork was carried out in **2023-24**. **NFHS-5** covered **2019-21**. IIPS remains the nodal agency.
SC: NFHS-5 was 2019-21; NFHS-6 fieldwork was 2023-24.

@topic Consumption & Time Use
Q: The two consecutive Household Consumption Expenditure Surveys conducted by NSO after 2011-12 were for:
A) 2022-23 and 2023-24
B) 2017-18 and 2018-19
C) 2019-20 and 2020-21
D) 2014-15 and 2015-16
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **HCES 2022-23** (results February 2024) and **HCES 2023-24** (results December 2024) followed the **withheld 2017-18** CES. Both used **three questionnaires and three visits** with **MMRP**.
SC: HCES 2022-23 and 2023-24 came after a gap since 2011-12.

Q: As per HCES 2023-24, average monthly per capita consumption expenditure (without imputation) in rural and urban India was about:
A) ₹4,122 and ₹6,996
B) ₹3,773 and ₹6,459
C) ₹2,500 and ₹4,500
D) ₹1,430 and ₹2,630
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The MPCE figures (without imputation) are **2023-24: rural ₹4,122, urban ₹6,996**; **2022-23: ₹3,773 and ₹6,459**; **2011-12: ₹1,430 and ₹2,630** (MMRP). The urban-rural gap fell to about **70%**.
SC: 2023-24: about 4.1k rural and 7.0k urban.

Q: India's second nationwide Time Use Survey was conducted by NSO during:
A) January-December 2024
B) January-December 2019
C) July 2022 – June 2023
D) April 2020 – March 2021
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **first TUS** ran **January-December 2019**, and the **second** ran **January-December 2024**, with results in 2025. Both used a **24-hour recall from 4 AM to 4 AM** for persons aged **6+**.
SC: TUS 2019, then TUS 2024.

@topic Base Revision Programme
Q: Under MoSPI's recent base-revision programme, the proposed new base years are:
A) GDP 2022-23, CPI 2024, and IIP/WPI 2022-23
B) GDP 2017-18, CPI 2020, IIP 2017-18
C) GDP 2020-21, CPI 2021, IIP 2020-21
D) GDP 2011-12, CPI 2012 (no change)
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The programme targets **GDP base 2022-23**, **CPI base 2024** (weights from **HCES 2023-24**) and **IIP/WPI 2022-23**. Earlier plans for a **2017-18** base were dropped, partly because the 2017-18 CES was withheld.
SC: New bases: GDP 2022-23, CPI 2024, IIP/WPI 2022-23.
TIP: Check the latest release status of each new series, and know both the old and new bases for the exam.

Q: Why was 2020-21 not considered suitable as a base year for national accounts?
A) It was an abnormal year due to the COVID-19 pandemic
B) No data were collected in that year
C) The Census was conducted that year
D) It coincided with GST implementation
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: A base year must be **normal**. **2020-21** saw a **pandemic-driven contraction**, just as **2009-10** was ruled out after the global slowdown.
SC: Pandemic years can't be base years.

@topic Data Platforms & Governance
Q: MoSPI's integrated data portal "eSankhyiki", offering macro indicators and data catalogues, was launched on:
A) 29 June 2024 (Statistics Day)
B) 20 October 2020
C) 15 October 1999
D) 1 January 2016
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **eSankhyiki** was launched on **Statistics Day, 29 June 2024**. It provides **time series of macro indicators** and a **data catalogue** in machine-readable form. MoSPI also runs a **microdata portal** for survey unit-level data.
SC: eSankhyiki was launched on Statistics Day 2024.

Q: The National Data and Analytics Platform (NDAP), which aggregates government datasets in an interoperable format, was developed by:
A) NITI Aayog
B) MoSPI
C) MeitY
D) RBI
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **NDAP** (public launch **2022**) is **NITI Aayog's** platform standardising datasets from ministries. **data.gov.in** (Open Government Data platform) is run by **NIC/MeitY** under **NDSAP 2012**.
SC: NDAP is NITI's; data.gov.in is MeitY/NIC's.

Q: The National Data Sharing and Accessibility Policy (NDSAP), which underpins the Open Government Data platform data.gov.in, was notified in:
A) 2012
B) 2005
C) 2019
D) 2023
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **NDSAP (2012)**, under the **Department of Science & Technology**, promotes **proactive sharing of non-sensitive government data**. It led to **data.gov.in**, managed by **NIC**.
SC: NDSAP 2012 led to data.gov.in.

Q: The Data Governance Quality Index (DGQI), which assesses ministries' data preparedness for their Central Sector and Centrally Sponsored Schemes, is conducted by:
A) Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO), NITI Aayog
B) NSO
C) CAG
D) DARPG
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **DMEO (NITI Aayog)** assesses **ministries and departments** on data generation, quality, use and **data governance** of schemes through the **DGQI**.
SC: DGQI is DMEO's scorecard of ministries' data.

Q: The Aspirational Districts Programme (launched 2018) tracks 112 districts on key performance indicators grouped into five themes. Which two themes carry the highest weight (30% each)?
A) Health & Nutrition and Education
B) Agriculture & Water Resources and Basic Infrastructure
C) Financial Inclusion and Skill Development
D) Basic Infrastructure and Education
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **ADP** covers **112 districts** with about **49 KPIs**. Themes and weights: **Health & Nutrition 30%**, **Education 30%**, **Agriculture & Water Resources 20%**, **Financial Inclusion & Skill Development 10%**, **Basic Infrastructure 10%**. Progress is ranked **monthly** on the **Champions of Change** dashboard.
SC: ADP weights: Health 30, Education 30, Agriculture 20, Finance and Skills 10, Infrastructure 10.

Q: The Aspirational Blocks Programme, launched in 2023, covers approximately how many blocks?
A) 500
B) 112
C) 250
D) 1,000
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **Aspirational Blocks Programme (January 2023)** extends the ADP model to about **500 blocks**. The **ADP** covers **112 districts**.
SC: 112 aspirational districts; about 500 aspirational blocks.

@topic International Milestones
Q: The UN Statistical Commission adopted a new System of National Accounts, the successor to SNA 2008, in:
A) 2025
B) 2015
C) 2020
D) 2030
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **2025 SNA** was adopted by the UN Statistical Commission in **March 2025**, along with the IMF's **BPM7**. It covers digitalisation, wellbeing and sustainability, and the treatment of data as an asset. India's current series follows SNA 2008.
SC: The 2025 SNA and BPM7 were adopted in 2025.

Q: World Statistics Day is celebrated every five years on 20 October. The third World Statistics Day, in 2020, had the theme:
A) Connecting the world with data we can trust
B) Better data, better lives
C) Celebrating the many achievements of official statistics
D) Data for sustainable development
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The three WSD themes are **2010: celebrating the achievements of official statistics** (core values of service, integrity, professionalism), **2015: "Better data, better lives"** and **2020: "Connecting the world with data we can trust"**. The next is due in **2025**.
SC: WSD themes: 2010 achievements, 2015 better lives, 2020 trust.

Q: The International Comparison Program round whose results were released in 2024 used reference year:
A) 2021
B) 2017
C) 2011
D) 2023
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **ICP 2021** results were published in **2024**, following **ICP 2017** (released 2020). India remained the **third-largest economy in PPP** terms. These PPPs underlie the updated World Bank poverty lines.
SC: ICP 2017 → ICP 2021, released 2024.

@topic Recent Indian Survey Programme
Q: The Comprehensive Annual Modular Survey (CAMS) conducted in NSS 79th round was designed mainly to:
A) generate SDG and other key indicators (such as ICT skills and financial inclusion) needed by ministries, with globally comparable definitions
B) replace the Population Census
C) compute the GDP deflator
D) estimate crop yields
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **CAMS (July 2022 – June 2023)** built on the **78th round MIS**. It collected **SDG and other indicators** (ICT skills, internet use, financial inclusion, etc.) that regular surveys did not cover.
SC: CAMS fills SDG indicator gaps, following the MIS.

Q: The Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE) complements the ASI by covering:
A) unincorporated non-agricultural establishments in manufacturing, trade and other services
B) registered factories only
C) agricultural households
D) government departments
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **ASUSE** covers **unincorporated non-agricultural enterprises**: manufacturing (excluding ASI units), **trade** and **other services**. It gives annual estimates of **establishments, workers and GVA** of the informal sector.
SC: ASUSE is the informal counterpart of the ASI.

Q: Which of the following is the correct description of a "Comprehensive Modular Survey" in the NSS programme?
A) a short-duration, topic-focused survey canvassed to generate specific indicators quickly
B) a decennial census of enterprises
C) a survey of only government employees
D) a monthly price collection exercise
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: Modular surveys are **short, focused and quick**. They serve ministries' **indicator needs** (for example on telecom or education use) without a full-year thematic round.
SC: Modular surveys are short and quick, answering specific indicator needs.

@topic Concepts in Recent Releases
Q: Assertion (A): PLFS unemployment rates are not strictly comparable with those of the older NSS Employment-Unemployment Surveys.
Reason (R): PLFS uses a different sampling design (including stratification by education in second-stage strata) and CAPI-based data collection.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Hard
EXP: PLFS changed the **design** (second-stage strata based on **general education level** of household members), the **mode** (CAPI) and the **periodicity**. NSO itself cautions against direct comparison with the EUS. R explains A.
SC: A new design means the series is not strictly comparable.

Q: The CPI with base 2024 uses weights derived from:
A) HCES 2023-24
B) the NSS 68th round (2011-12)
C) the Census 2011
D) WCFIES 2016
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The new **CPI (2024 base)** takes its **consumption weights** from **HCES 2023-24**, replacing the **68th round (2011-12)** weights of the 2012 series. An updated basket reflects services and digital purchases.
SC: New CPI weights come from HCES 2023-24.

Q: Why did MoSPI impute the value of free items received through social welfare programmes in a separate HCES estimate?
A) To reflect the consumption actually enjoyed by households, including goods such as free food grains, laptops and bicycles
B) To inflate poverty estimates
C) Because HCES did not collect data on purchases
D) To comply with GST rules
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: With large **free distribution** (such as PMGKAY food grains), **purchase-based MPCE understates consumption**. Imputing their value gives a separate, more complete **MPCE (with imputation)**.
SC: Imputation adds free welfare goods to measured consumption.

Q: Which of the following statistics is released monthly?
A) Payroll data on EPF, ESI and NPS
B) HCES
C) Annual Survey of Industries
D) SRS Statistical Report
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Payroll reporting** is **monthly**. **HCES, ASI and the SRS report** are **annual or periodic**.
SC: Payroll is monthly; surveys are annual or periodic.

Q: MoSPI releases unit-level (micro) data of its surveys such as PLFS, HCES and ASUSE to users:
A) free of cost through its microdata portal, after anonymisation
B) only to government departments
C) on payment of a fee per variable
D) never, as they are confidential
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: MoSPI releases **anonymised unit-level data** of its surveys **free** through its **microdata portal**. This balances **confidentiality** (FPOS Principle 6) with **equal access** (Principle 1).
SC: Microdata are released free once anonymised.

@topic PLFS: Recent Results & Revamp
Q: As per the PLFS annual report 2023-24, the Worker Population Ratio (usual status, 15+) was about:
A) 58%
B) 38%
C) 47%
D) 70%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: In 2023-24, **WPR (15+) was about 58.2%** and **LFPR about 60.1%**, which gives **UR = 1 − 58.2/60.1 ≈ 3.2%**. The all-ages rates are lower because children sit in the denominator.
SC: 2023-24 (15+): LFPR about 60, WPR about 58, UR about 3.2.

Q: In PLFS, "youth" unemployment refers to persons in the age group:
A) 15-29 years
B) 15-24 years
C) 18-35 years
D) 14-30 years
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: PLFS reports youth indicators for **15-29 years**, following the **National Youth Policy 2014**. International comparisons often use **15-24**.
SC: PLFS youth = 15-29.
