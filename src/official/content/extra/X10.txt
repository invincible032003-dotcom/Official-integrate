@topic Nature, Need & Users
Q: Official statistics are best described as:
A) statistics produced and disseminated by the national statistical system as a public good, under professional standards
B) any statistics published in newspapers
C) statistics produced only by international agencies
D) confidential data of the government not meant for the public
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Official statistics** come from **national statistical agencies and ministries**. They are a **public good** produced under standards such as the **FPOS** and are used by government, business, researchers, international bodies and citizens.
SC: Official statistics are a public good made to professional standards.

Q: Which of the following is NOT usually regarded as a limitation of official statistics in India?
A) They are prepared under a legal and professional framework with published methodology
B) Time lags in availability of certain data
C) Coverage gaps in the informal sector
D) Non-sampling errors in large surveys
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Easy
EXP: A **legal and professional framework with published methods** is a **strength**. Common **limitations** are **time lags**, **informal-sector coverage**, **non-sampling errors**, **inconsistency between sources** and **gaps in sub-national data**.
SC: Lags, informal-sector gaps and non-sampling errors are limitations; transparent methods are a strength.

Q: The concept of "relevance" as a dimension of statistical quality means:
A) the degree to which statistics meet the current and potential needs of users
B) how quickly data are released
C) how close estimates are to true values
D) the ease of obtaining data
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The standard quality dimensions are **relevance** (meets user needs), **accuracy** (closeness to truth), **timeliness and punctuality**, **accessibility and clarity**, and **coherence and comparability**.
SC: Relevance = does it meet users' needs.

Q: Which of the following are dimensions in the IMF's Data Quality Assessment Framework (DQAF), besides the prerequisites of quality?
1. Assurances of integrity
2. Methodological soundness
3. Accuracy and reliability
4. Serviceability and accessibility
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2, 3 and 4 only
C) 1, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Hard
EXP: The **DQAF** has **prerequisites of quality** plus five dimensions: **integrity, methodological soundness, accuracy and reliability, serviceability, and accessibility**. The listed items cover all of these.
SC: DQAF = integrity, method, accuracy, serviceability, accessibility (+ prerequisites).

Q: Assertion (A): GDP is released first as advance estimates and later revised several times.
Reason (R): There is a trade-off between timeliness and accuracy; early estimates rely on indicators, while later estimates use fuller data.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Easy
EXP: Early estimates are **timely but indicator-based**. As **budgets, company accounts and surveys** arrive, estimates are **revised**. A published **revision policy** keeps this transparent. R explains A.
SC: Fast first, accurate later: that is why revisions exist.

Q: "Coherence" in statistical quality refers to:
A) the consistency of statistics across sources, over time and with standard definitions and classifications
B) the speed of release
C) the cost of data collection
D) the number of users of the data
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Coherence** means different sources, periods and domains can be **combined and compared** reliably, using common **concepts, classifications and methods**.
SC: Coherence = the data fit together.

@topic Errors in Statistics
Q: Which of the following statements about sampling and non-sampling errors is correct?
A) Non-sampling errors can arise in both censuses and sample surveys, and do not necessarily decrease with sample size
B) A census is free from all errors
C) Sampling errors increase with sample size
D) Non-sampling errors can be eliminated by increasing the sample size
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Sampling error** arises from observing only part of the population and **falls as n rises**. **Non-sampling errors** (coverage, non-response, measurement, processing) arise in **censuses too** and may even **rise** with larger operations.
SC: Sampling error shrinks with n; non-sampling error does not.

Q: Which of the following are non-sampling errors?
1. Omission of households from the frame
2. Non-response by selected households
3. Recall lapse by respondents
4. Data entry mistakes during processing
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2, 3 and 4 only
C) 1, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Easy
EXP: All four are non-sampling errors: **coverage** (frame omissions), **non-response**, **response or measurement** (recall lapse) and **processing** (data entry) errors.
SC: Frame, non-response, recall and processing are all non-sampling errors.

Q: A Post-Enumeration Survey (PES) is conducted after a Census primarily to:
A) estimate coverage and content errors in the Census
B) replace the Census results
C) collect new data on migration
D) prepare the NPR
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: A **PES** re-enumerates a **sample** independently and **matches** it with the Census to estimate **under- or over-count (coverage error)** and **content error**.
SC: A PES audits the Census count.

Q: Mahalanobis's technique of interpenetrating network of sub-samples (IPNS) is useful because it:
A) provides a direct estimate of sampling variance and helps detect non-sampling (investigator) errors
B) eliminates the need for stratification
C) reduces sample size to zero
D) replaces probability sampling
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: With **independent sub-samples** (often assigned to different investigators or agencies), the spread between sub-sample estimates gives a simple **variance estimate**. Differences also reveal **investigator bias**. NSS and the ASI use IPNS.
SC: IPNS gives variance and exposes investigator bias.

Q: In NSS surveys, when a selected household cannot be surveyed (e.g. it is locked or refuses), the usual practice is to:
A) substitute it by another household from the same second-stage stratum, following laid-down procedures
B) drop the entire FSU
C) impute all values as zero
D) double the weight of the FSU without substitution
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: NSS permits **substitution** of casualty households, by prescribed rules, from the **same second-stage stratum**. This maintains the sample size, although substitution can introduce bias if non-respondents differ systematically.
SC: NSS substitutes non-responding households within the same stratum.

Q: Response bias due to "telescoping" in surveys refers to:
A) respondents reporting events as having occurred within the reference period when they actually occurred earlier (or vice versa)
B) investigators skipping questions
C) data entry of wrong codes
D) excluding remote villages
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **Telescoping** moves remembered events **into or out of** the reference period, causing **over- or under-reporting**. Bounded recall and short reference periods (as in MMRP) help reduce it.
SC: Telescoping shifts dates across the reference window.

@topic Methods of Data Collection
Q: Which method of data collection is correctly matched with its example?
A) Registration method – Civil Registration System
B) Census method – Sample Registration System
C) Sample survey method – Population Census
D) Administrative records – NFHS
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The correct pairs are **Registration → CRS**, **Census → complete enumeration** (Population, Economic, Livestock), **Sample survey → NSS, NFHS, PLFS, SRS** and **Administrative → HMIS, UDISE+, EPFO**.
SC: Registration is continuous, a census counts everyone, a survey samples.

Q: Which of the following is an advantage of administrative data over sample surveys?
A) Low additional cost and potentially complete coverage of the administered population, with frequent availability
B) Concepts always match statistical definitions
C) Free from coverage gaps
D) Collected directly by statisticians for statistical purposes
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Administrative data are **cheap** (a by-product), **frequent** and **cover all units in the scheme**. But their **concepts** follow administrative needs, **coverage** is limited to registered units, and **quality** depends on recording practices.
SC: Administrative data are cheap and frequent, but their concepts follow administrative rules.

Q: Compared with a complete enumeration, a sample survey generally offers:
A) lower cost, greater speed and the possibility of more detailed information with better-trained staff
B) zero sampling error
C) estimates for every village
D) no need for a sampling frame
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Sample surveys are **cheaper, faster and more detailed**, and can have **lower non-sampling error** through trained staff. The cost is **sampling error** and **limited small-area estimates**.
SC: A sample is cheaper, faster and deeper; a census is complete.

Q: The "householder method" of data collection is suitable mainly when:
A) respondents are literate enough to fill schedules themselves
B) the population is largely illiterate
C) data are to be collected from remote tribal areas
D) investigators are highly trained
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Self-enumeration** needs **literate respondents**. India has traditionally used the **canvasser method**; **Census 2027** offers an optional **self-enumeration** online.
SC: Self-enumeration needs literate respondents.

Q: A pilot survey is conducted before a main survey mainly to:
A) test the questionnaire, field procedures and estimate costs and variability
B) replace the main survey
C) publish preliminary results
D) select the final sample households
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: A **pilot** tests **schedules, wording, instructions, logistics and timing**, and gives preliminary **variance** estimates for sample-size planning.
SC: A pilot is a trial run of questionnaire and field work.

Q: Remote sensing data are used in official statistics in India for:
A) crop area and yield forecasting (FASAL), forest cover (ISFR) and urban frame updates
B) measuring household consumption
C) computing CPI
D) counting population in the Census
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Remote sensing is used for **FASAL/MNCFC** crop forecasts, **FSI forest cover** and **UFS/GIS** mapping. Household characteristics still need **surveys**.
SC: Satellites cover crops, forests and maps; households need surveys.

@topic Processing & Confidentiality
Q: "Imputation" in survey data processing refers to:
A) assigning plausible values to missing or inconsistent items using defined rules or models
B) deleting all incomplete records
C) collecting fresh data
D) publishing raw data
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Editing** detects errors and **imputation** fills gaps, using methods such as hot-deck, mean or model-based imputation, so that estimates remain usable.
SC: Editing finds holes; imputation fills them.

Q: Statistical disclosure control techniques applied before releasing microdata include:
A) removing direct identifiers, top-coding extreme values and suppressing or coarsening small cells
B) publishing names and addresses
C) releasing data only in PDF
D) increasing the sample size
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **SDC** protects **confidentiality** (FPOS Principle 6; Census Act and CoS Act provisions) through **anonymisation, top-coding, aggregation or coarsening and perturbation**. It balances utility with privacy.
SC: SDC hides who someone is while keeping the data useful.

Q: Under the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008, information furnished by an informant can be used:
A) only for statistical purposes, and cannot be used as evidence against the informant except for prosecution under the Act
B) for tax assessment by the Income Tax Department
C) for any purpose the government decides
D) only by the police
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The CoS Act requires that information be used **only for statistical purposes**, kept **confidential**, and that identifiable information not be disclosed. This **builds trust** and improves response.
SC: Data given under the CoS Act are for statistics only.

Q: Metadata in official statistics refers to:
A) data about data, covering definitions, sources, methods, coverage, reference periods and quality
B) very large datasets
C) data with errors
D) only the file format
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Metadata** describe **concepts, methods, sources, coverage, periodicity and revisions**. They support **correct interpretation** (FPOS Principle 3) and comparability. MoSPI's **National Metadata Structure** standardises them.
SC: Metadata is the "about" layer of data.

@topic Dissemination & Transparency
Q: Which of the following practices enhances the transparency of official statistics?
1. Publishing an advance release calendar
2. Publishing methodology and revision policy
3. Pre-release access to selected users days before public release
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: A
T: Statement
D: Medium
EXP: **Release calendars** and published **methods and revisions** enhance transparency. **Selective pre-release access** undermines **equal access** (FPOS Principle 1) and is discouraged.
SC: Calendars and methods help; privileged early access hurts.

Q: Which of the following modes of dissemination is most suitable for researchers wishing to do their own analysis of NSS survey data?
A) Anonymised unit-level (micro) data
B) Press release
C) Summary tables in the annual report
D) Infographics
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Microdata** allow **custom tabulation and modelling**. Press releases and summary reports serve **general users** and the media.
SC: Researchers need microdata.

Q: "Punctuality" as a quality dimension refers to:
A) the time lag between the actual and the pre-announced release date
B) the time lag between the reference period and the release
C) the accuracy of the first estimate
D) the number of releases per year
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: **Punctuality** means releasing on the **announced date**. **Timeliness** is the gap between the **reference period** and the **release**. They are related but distinct.
SC: Punctual means on the promised date; timely means soon after the period.

@topic Concepts in Survey Design
Q: The "reference period" of a survey is:
A) the period to which the collected information relates
B) the period during which field work is conducted
C) the date of publication
D) the period of training of investigators
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **reference period** is what the data describe (e.g. **last 365 days, last 7 days**, a financial year). The **survey period** is when data are **collected**. In the ASI, the reference is the **financial year** and the survey runs **November to July** afterwards.
SC: Reference is what the data cover; survey is when they are collected.

Q: A sampling frame is:
A) the list or map of units from which the sample is drawn
B) the questionnaire used
C) the estimation formula
D) the set of tabulations
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: A **frame** lists or maps the population units: **Census villages**, **UFS blocks**, **CIF factory lists** (ASI), **EC establishments**. A **frame error** (omission, duplication) causes **coverage bias**.
SC: No good frame, no good sample.

Q: A rotational panel design, as used in PLFS urban areas, has the advantage of:
A) improving the precision of estimates of change between periods through overlapping samples
B) eliminating non-response
C) removing the need for a frame
D) giving district-level estimates
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: With **overlap (75% in PLFS)**, consecutive estimates are **positively correlated**, which **reduces the variance of estimated change**. Rotating units out limits **respondent fatigue**.
SC: Overlap sharpens estimates of change; rotation limits fatigue.

Q: Stratification in sample design improves precision when:
A) units within strata are homogeneous and strata differ from one another
B) strata are heterogeneous internally
C) the sample is drawn with replacement only
D) the population is very small
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: Precision improves when variation is **low within strata** and **between-stratum variation is removed** from sampling error. NSS strata are formed by district, rural or urban, and size.
SC: Homogeneous within strata, different between them.

@topic Uses & Interpretation
Q: Which of the following is a leading indicator of economic activity, rather than a lagging one?
A) New orders in a purchasing managers' survey (PMI)
B) Unemployment rate
C) Annual GDP growth
D) Inflation for the past year
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Leading indicators** (PMI new orders, business expectations, order books) **anticipate** turning points. **Unemployment** and **inflation** tend to **lag** the cycle.
SC: Orders lead; jobs and prices lag.

Q: Seasonal adjustment of a monthly time series is carried out to:
A) remove regular calendar-related fluctuations so that the underlying trend and cycle can be seen
B) remove inflation
C) remove sampling error
D) change the base year
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Seasonal adjustment removes predictable patterns (festivals, monsoon, number of working days) so that **month-to-month** comparisons are meaningful. **Year-on-year** changes partly neutralise seasonality.
SC: Seasonal adjustment removes calendar noise.

Q: Why are year-on-year growth rates often used for monthly indices such as IIP and CPI in India?
A) Comparing with the same month of the previous year largely neutralises seasonal effects
B) They are always smaller
C) They require no base year
D) They are required by the Constitution
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: **Y-o-y** comparison removes most **seasonality** without formal adjustment, although it responds slowly to turning points and is affected by **base effects**.
SC: Y-o-y cancels the season but carries base effects.

Q: A "base effect" in inflation refers to:
A) the influence of the previous year's index level on the current year-on-year inflation rate
B) the effect of changing the base year
C) the effect of weights on the index
D) the effect of exchange rates
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: If prices were **unusually high (low)** in the same month last year, current **y-o-y inflation** looks **lower (higher)** even with an unchanged monthly momentum.
SC: Last year's spike shrinks this year's rate.

Q: Which of the following best describes "big data" or alternative data sources for official statistics?
A) Large, high-frequency digital data such as GST filings, e-way bills, UPI transactions and satellite imagery, usable for nowcasting subject to quality checks
B) Only the Population Census
C) Data collected by postal questionnaires
D) Any dataset with more than 100 records
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Big and administrative digital data** (GST, e-way bills, digital payments, remote sensing, mobile data) offer **timeliness and granularity**. They also raise issues of **representativeness, concepts, access and privacy**, so they complement rather than replace surveys.
SC: Big data are fast and granular but need checks on representativeness and privacy.

Q: Assertion (A): Estimates from different sources, e.g. PLFS and EPFO payroll data, can show different employment trends.
Reason (R): They differ in concept and coverage: PLFS measures all employment from households, while payroll data count new formal-sector subscribers in certain schemes.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Medium
EXP: PLFS is a **household survey of all work**. Payroll data are **administrative records** of **new subscribers** (a flow). They also include **re-joiners and formalisation** of existing jobs. R explains A.
SC: Different concepts and coverage give different trends.

Q: The UN National Quality Assurance Framework (NQAF) template is intended to:
A) help national statistical systems develop their own quality assurance frameworks
B) rank countries by GDP
C) regulate statistical software
D) set tax rates
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **UN NQAF template** guides countries in **managing quality** across the statistical system. India adopted an NQAF (2018) aligned with it, and the NQAF-certified divisions are NAD, PSD and SDRD.
SC: NQAF is a template for managing quality.

Q: Which of the following is the correct sequence of stages in the statistical production process (GSBPM)?
A) Specify needs → Design → Build → Collect → Process → Analyse → Disseminate → Evaluate
B) Collect → Design → Specify needs → Process → Analyse → Disseminate
C) Design → Collect → Build → Analyse → Process → Evaluate
D) Specify needs → Collect → Design → Build → Disseminate → Analyse
ANS: A
T: Chronology
D: Easy
EXP: GSBPM (UNECE) has 8 phases: **Specify needs, Design, Build, Collect, Process, Analyse, Disseminate, Evaluate**. Evaluation feeds back into the next cycle.
SC: Specify, Design, Build, Collect, Process, Analyse, Disseminate, Evaluate.

Q: Why might a Census and a sample survey conducted in the same year give different estimates of the same characteristic (e.g. literacy)?
A) Because of differences in concepts, reference periods, sampling error in the survey and non-sampling errors in both
B) Because one of them must be falsified
C) Because censuses use samples
D) They can never differ
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
EXP: Differences arise from **definitions** (such as age groups), **reference dates**, **sampling error** (surveys only) and **non-sampling errors** (both). Metadata help reconcile them.
SC: Different concepts and different errors give different numbers.

@topic Nature, Need & Users
Q: "Accessibility and clarity" as a quality dimension means:
A) data are easy to find and obtain, in suitable formats, with adequate explanatory metadata
B) data are released within a week
C) data are free of all errors
D) data are consistent over time
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Accessibility** covers the ease of **finding and getting** data (portals, APIs, microdata). **Clarity** covers **metadata, documentation and help**, so that users interpret the data correctly.
SC: Accessible = easy to get; clear = easy to understand.

Q: Data collected by a researcher from published NSO reports would be classified, for that researcher, as:
A) secondary data
B) primary data
C) experimental data
D) administrative data
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Primary data** are collected first-hand for the purpose at hand. **Secondary data** have been collected by someone else. NSO survey data are primary for the NSO but **secondary** for the researcher using them.
SC: Primary or secondary depends on who collected the data.

Q: Which of the following is the main reason that statistics based on complete enumeration (census) can sometimes be less accurate than a well-designed sample survey?
A) The larger scale of a census makes non-sampling errors harder to control, while a survey can use fewer, better-trained staff
B) A census has larger sampling error
C) Surveys always cover more units
D) Censuses do not use questionnaires
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: A **census has no sampling error**, but its **huge scale** raises **non-sampling errors** (training, supervision, fatigue). A **smaller, better-controlled survey** can therefore sometimes be more accurate overall.
SC: A census has zero sampling error but more room for non-sampling error.
