@topic ILO Concepts
Q: As per the ILO (13th ICLS) standard definition, a person is "unemployed" if he/she is:
A) without work, currently available for work, and seeking work during the reference period
B) without work and not seeking work
C) working less than 40 hours a week
D) working without pay in a family enterprise
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The ILO's **three criteria** are **without work + available + seeking**. Unpaid family helpers are **employed**. Working fewer hours than desired is **time-related underemployment**, not unemployment.
SC: Unemployed = no work + available + seeking.

Q: The "potential labour force" introduced by the 19th ICLS refers to persons not in employment who:
A) are either seeking work but not currently available, or available but not seeking work
B) are working part-time
C) are students
D) are retired but receive pensions
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The **potential labour force** includes **unavailable job-seekers** and **available potential job-seekers** (including **discouraged workers**). Together with the unemployed they form part of **labour underutilisation**.
SC: Potential LF = seeking but unavailable, or available but not seeking.

Q: Under the ILO labour underutilisation measures, LU1 is:
A) the unemployment rate
B) the combined rate of time-related underemployment and unemployment
C) the combined rate of unemployment and the potential labour force
D) the composite measure of labour underutilisation
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The measures are **LU1 = unemployment rate**; **LU2 = unemployment + time-related underemployment**; **LU3 = unemployment + potential labour force**; **LU4 = composite of all three**.
SC: LU1 is unemployment, LU2 adds underemployment, LU3 adds potential LF, LU4 adds everything.

Q: The ILO minimum threshold of work used to classify a person as "employed" in the reference week is:
A) at least one hour
B) at least 4 hours on any day
C) at least 15 hours
D) at least 40 hours
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The ILO **one-hour criterion** gives complete coverage of employment. PLFS **CWS** uses **1 hour on any day of the week**. The **4-hour** rule is for CDS **full days**.
SC: One hour of work in the week makes a person employed.

Q: Which of the following is classified as employed under ILO and PLFS concepts?
A) An unpaid helper working in the family's shop
B) A full-time student not seeking work
C) A person doing only unpaid domestic chores for the household
D) A retired person living on a pension
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Unpaid helpers in household enterprises** produce for the market, so they are **employed** (self-employed, code 21). Students, domestic-duty persons and pensioners are **outside the labour force**.
SC: Helping in the family business counts as work; home chores do not (in PLFS).

@topic Unemployment Types & Measures
Q: Disguised unemployment is characterised by:
A) more workers engaged in an activity than required, so that the marginal productivity of some workers is zero or near zero
B) workers searching between jobs
C) unemployment due to recession
D) unemployment during the lean agricultural season only
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Disguised unemployment** is typical of **family farms**: removing some workers does **not reduce output**. **Frictional** means between jobs; **cyclical** means recession; **seasonal** means lean seasons; **structural** means a skills or technology mismatch.
SC: Disguised = extra hands adding nothing.

Q: Among the NSS/PLFS approaches, the unemployment rate is generally:
A) highest under Current Daily Status and lowest under Usual Status
B) highest under Usual Status and lowest under Current Daily Status
C) identical under all approaches
D) highest under Current Weekly Status
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **CDS** counts **unemployed person-days**, capturing intermittent unemployment, so its rate is **highest**. **Usual status** (a year-long major-time criterion) captures only **chronic** unemployment and is **lowest**. The typical ranking is **UR(CDS) > UR(CWS) > UR(US)**.
SC: The shorter the reference period, the more unemployment is captured: CDS > CWS > US.

Q: Usual status unemployment is often described as a measure of:
A) chronic (long-term) unemployment
B) weekly unemployment
C) daily underemployment
D) seasonal employment only
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **usual principal status** uses **365 days** and the **major time** criterion, so it shows **chronic open unemployment**. **CWS** gives a **weekly** picture and **CDS** gives **person-days**, which includes underemployment.
SC: Usual status shows chronic unemployment; CDS shows daily-level unemployment.

Q: Time-related underemployment refers to persons who:
A) are employed but work fewer hours than a threshold and are willing and available to work more
B) have no work at all
C) are overqualified for their jobs
D) are working more than 48 hours
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Time-related (visible) underemployment**: insufficient hours, plus willingness and availability for more. **Skill mismatch** (overqualification) is sometimes called **invisible underemployment**.
SC: Too few hours plus wanting more = underemployment.

Q: Unemployment arising from a mismatch between the skills of workers and the requirements of available jobs, often due to technological change, is called:
A) structural unemployment
B) frictional unemployment
C) cyclical unemployment
D) voluntary unemployment
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Structural** unemployment is a **skills or location mismatch** that lasts a long time. **Frictional** is short-term job search. **Cyclical** follows business cycles.
SC: Mismatch is structural; search is frictional; recession is cyclical.

@topic SDG 8 & Youth
Q: The NEET rate (SDG indicator 8.6.1) measures the proportion of youth who are:
A) not in employment, education or training
B) not enrolled in elementary education
C) employed in the informal sector
D) not eligible for employment training
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **NEET = youth not in employment, education or training**, as a share of all youth. It captures youth **disengagement** better than the unemployment rate alone.
SC: NEET = not earning or learning.

Q: Which SDG indicator corresponds to the unemployment rate by sex, age and persons with disabilities?
A) 8.5.2
B) 8.3.1
C) 8.6.1
D) 1.1.1
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: The SDG 8 indicators are **8.5.2: unemployment rate**; **8.3.1: proportion of informal employment**; **8.6.1: NEET rate**. **1.1.1** is **extreme poverty**.
SC: 8.5.2 unemployment, 8.3.1 informality, 8.6.1 NEET.

@topic Structure of Employment
Q: As per recent PLFS annual reports, the largest category of workers by status in employment in India is:
A) self-employed (about three-fifths of workers)
B) regular wage/salaried employees
C) casual labourers
D) unpaid apprentices
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: In recent PLFS rounds, about **58% of workers are self-employed**, about **22% regular wage/salaried** and about **20% casual labour**. Self-employment includes own-account workers, employers and **unpaid helpers**.
SC: Roughly 58 : 22 : 20 for self-employed : regular : casual.

Q: As per recent PLFS results, approximately what share of India's workers (usual status) is engaged in agriculture?
A) about 46%
B) about 25%
C) about 65%
D) about 10%
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: About **46%** of workers are in **agriculture**, although agriculture's share of **GVA** is much smaller (under a fifth). This gap reflects **low agricultural labour productivity** and **disguised unemployment**.
SC: About 46% of jobs but under a fifth of GVA are in agriculture.

Q: Informal employment, as per the 17th ICLS, includes:
A) all jobs lacking social protection or employment benefits, whether in informal sector enterprises, formal sector enterprises or households
B) only jobs in informal sector enterprises
C) only unpaid family work
D) only jobs in the government sector
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Informal employment** is a **job-based** concept (17th ICLS). It covers informal jobs **in the formal sector**, in **the informal sector** and in **households**. The **informal sector** (15th ICLS) is **enterprise-based**.
SC: Informal employment is about jobs; the informal sector is about enterprises.

Q: A regular salaried employee in a large company with no written contract, no paid leave and no social security benefits is classified as:
A) informally employed in the formal sector
B) formally employed in the informal sector
C) not in the labour force
D) self-employed
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **enterprise is formal**, but the **job lacks protection**, so this is **informal employment in the formal sector**. PLFS tracks this through **contract, paid leave and social security** indicators.
SC: A formal firm with an unprotected job means informal employment in the formal sector.

Q: As per NITI Aayog's report on India's gig and platform economy (2022), the gig workforce is projected to reach about what size by 2029-30?
A) 2.35 crore
B) 77 lakh
C) 10 crore
D) 50 lakh
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: NITI (2022) estimated **77 lakh gig workers in 2020-21**, projected to rise to about **2.35 crore by 2029-30**. The **Code on Social Security 2020** recognises **gig and platform workers**.
SC: Gig workers: 77 lakh (2020-21) → about 2.35 crore (2029-30).

@topic Labour Law & Data Systems
Q: The e-Shram portal, launched in August 2021 by the Ministry of Labour & Employment, is:
A) a national database of unorganised workers, seeded with Aadhaar
B) a portal for EPF withdrawals
C) a labour force survey
D) a database of Government employees
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **e-Shram** registers **unorganised workers**, including construction, migrant, gig and agricultural workers, and issues a **UAN**. It supports the delivery of social security. It is an **administrative** database, not a survey.
SC: e-Shram is a registry of unorganised workers.

Q: The four Labour Codes (Wages; Industrial Relations; Social Security; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions) consolidate approximately how many central labour laws?
A) 29
B) 44
C) 12
D) 100
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **four codes** subsume **29 central labour laws**: the Code on **Wages 2019**, plus the **IR**, **SS** and **OSH** Codes of **2020**. The Code on Wages introduced a **national floor wage**.
SC: Four codes replace 29 laws.

Q: The Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016:
A) prohibits employment of children below 14 in all occupations and processes (except helping family, after school hours), and of adolescents (14-18) in hazardous occupations
B) permits child labour in all non-hazardous industries
C) applies only to factories
D) defines a child as a person below 18 for all purposes
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **2016 amendment** bans all employment of **children under 14**, with exceptions for family enterprises and entertainment (outside school hours). It bans **adolescents (14-18)** from **hazardous** occupations.
SC: Under 14: no work (with narrow exceptions); 14-18: no hazardous work.

Q: As per Census 2011, the number of child workers (5-14 years) in India was about:
A) 1.01 crore
B) 3.5 crore
C) 25 lakh
D) 5 crore
ANS: A
D: Hard
EXP: Census 2011 counted about **1.01 crore** working children aged 5-14 (main + marginal), down from about **1.26 crore in 2001**.
SC: Child workers: about 1.26 crore (2001) → 1.01 crore (2011).

@topic Numericals & Concepts
Q: If employment grows by 1.5% when GDP grows by 6%, the employment elasticity of output is:
A) 0.25
B) 4.0
C) 0.9
D) 7.5
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: **Employment elasticity = % change in employment ÷ % change in output = 1.5/6 = 0.25**. Low elasticity is associated with **"jobless growth"**.
SC: Elasticity = job growth ÷ GDP growth.

Q: In a population of working age 1,000, there are 520 employed, 30 unemployed and 450 outside the labour force. The LFPR, WPR and UR are respectively:
A) 55%, 52% and about 5.5%
B) 52%, 55% and 3%
C) 55%, 52% and 3%
D) 58%, 52% and 5.5%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **LF = 550**. **LFPR = 55%**, **WPR = 52%**, **UR = 30/550 = 5.45% ≈ 5.5%**. The 3% option divides by the population, which is the common error.
SC: UR divides by the labour force, not the population.

Q: Assertion (A): A rise in the labour force participation rate can increase the unemployment rate even when employment is growing.
Reason (R): The unemployment rate depends on both employment and the size of the labour force.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Medium
EXP: If **new entrants** outnumber the **jobs created**, the UR rises even as employment rises, because **UR = (LF − E)/LF**. R explains A.
SC: More entrants than jobs pushes the UR up.

Q: Which of the following persons would be counted as "discouraged workers"?
A) Persons available for work but not seeking it because they believe no work is available
B) Persons working part-time
C) Persons seeking work but not currently available
D) Retired persons
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Discouraged workers** want work and are **available** but have **stopped searching**. Under the ILO framework they are part of the **potential labour force**. Note that **PLFS CWS** counts those available but not seeking (because they believe no work is available) as **unemployed**.
SC: Discouraged = available but gave up searching.

Q: The Labour Force Participation Rate for females is typically lower in urban than in rural India. The most common explanation offered is:
A) fewer opportunities for suitable non-farm work near home combined with social norms, and income effects in urban households
B) urban women are counted as students only
C) urban surveys exclude women
D) rural women are paid more
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
EXP: The low **urban female LFPR** reflects **limited suitable jobs, safety and mobility constraints, social norms**, and **income effects** (women withdraw as household income rises). Rural women often work on **family farms**, which counts as employment.
SC: Rural women farm; urban women face fewer suitable jobs and stronger norms.

Q: The main difference between the Quarterly Employment Survey (QES) and PLFS is that:
A) QES is an establishment-based (demand-side) survey of selected sectors, while PLFS is a household-based (supply-side) survey of all workers
B) both are household surveys
C) QES covers agriculture while PLFS does not
D) PLFS is conducted by the Labour Bureau
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **QES/AQEES** (Labour Bureau) survey **establishments** in 9 sectors. **PLFS** (NSO) surveys **households**, covering all activities, **including agriculture and the informal sector**.
SC: QES counts jobs from employers; PLFS counts workers from households.

Q: Which organisation compiles and publishes "Wage Rates in Rural India" for various agricultural and non-agricultural occupations?
A) Labour Bureau
B) DES, MoA&FW
C) NSO
D) RBI
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **Labour Bureau** publishes **Wage Rates in Rural India** (monthly). The **DES** publishes **Agricultural Wages in India**. Both are used for rural wage trend analysis, for example in the RBI's Handbook.
SC: Rural occupational wages come from the Labour Bureau; farm wages from DES.

Q: The Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) payroll data mainly capture:
A) formal-sector workers in establishments covered under the EPF & MP Act (generally with 20 or more employees)
B) all workers in the economy
C) agricultural workers
D) government employees only
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **EPF & MP Act 1952** covers **establishments with 20+ employees** (in notified industries). EPFO data therefore reflect **formal** employment, **plus formalisation** of existing jobs.
SC: EPFO covers formal establishments with 20+ employees.

Q: Which of the following is a supply-side source of employment data?
A) PLFS
B) QES
C) EPFO payroll data
D) ASI employment data
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **PLFS** asks **households** about their work, so it measures **labour supply**. **QES, EPFO and ASI** come from **establishments or administrative records**, so they are **demand-side**.
SC: Households are the supply side; establishments are the demand side.

Q: In the Census, a "marginal worker" is one who worked for:
A) less than six months (183 days) in the reference year
B) less than 30 days in the reference year
C) less than one hour in the reference week
D) only in household industry
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: A Census **main worker** worked **183+ days** and a **marginal worker** **fewer than 183 days** (split into 3-6 months and under 3 months). Census concepts differ from **PLFS** concepts.
SC: Main 183+ days, marginal fewer.

Q: The minimum wage fixation under the Minimum Wages Act, 1948 (now subsumed in the Code on Wages, 2019) is indexed to price changes using mainly:
A) CPI-IW (and CPI-AL/RL for agricultural and rural employments) through variable dearness allowance
B) WPI
C) GDP deflator
D) Sensex
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Variable DA** adjusts minimum wages with **CPI-IW** for industrial employments and **CPI-AL/RL** for agricultural ones. The **Wage Rate Index** tracks actual wage rates.
SC: Minimum wages are indexed by CPI-IW and CPI-AL/RL.

@topic Unemployment Types & Measures
Q: Unemployment that arises because agricultural work is available only in certain months of the year is called:
A) seasonal unemployment
B) structural unemployment
C) frictional unemployment
D) open unemployment in urban areas
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Seasonal unemployment** follows the **crop calendar** (lean seasons). It is picked up better by **CWS/CDS** than by the **usual status**, which uses a 365-day major-time criterion.
SC: Lean season, no work: seasonal unemployment.

Q: Labour productivity at the aggregate level is commonly measured as:
A) GVA (at constant prices) per worker or per hour worked
B) wages per worker
C) employment ÷ population
D) output ÷ capital stock
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **Labour productivity = real GVA ÷ employment** (or ÷ hours worked). **Output ÷ capital** is **capital productivity**. Total factor productivity is the residual after all inputs.
SC: Productivity = real value added per worker.

Q: The "labour force" is also referred to in official statistics as:
A) the economically active population
B) the working-age population
C) the dependent population
D) the total population aged 15-64
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: The **labour force** is the **economically active population**: the **employed plus the unemployed**. The **working-age population** (15+ or 15-64) also includes those **outside the labour force**.
SC: Labour force = employed + unemployed = economically active.

Q: ILOSTAT, the international database of labour statistics, is maintained by:
A) International Labour Organization
B) World Bank
C) OECD
D) UNSD
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: **ILOSTAT** holds labour data and **modelled estimates** for all countries, and the ILO publishes the **World Employment and Social Outlook (WESO)**. India reports to it through PLFS and related data.
SC: Labour data worldwide come from ILOSTAT.

@topic Structure of Employment
Q: Which employment status category in PLFS typically has the lowest average earnings?
A) Casual labour
B) Regular wage/salaried
C) Self-employed employers
D) Government employees
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: **Casual labourers** earn **daily wages** with **no job security**. Their average earnings are lower than those of **regular salaried** workers and of most self-employed persons. PLFS reports earnings by status.
SC: Casual labourers earn the least.

Q: The share of workers in regular wage/salaried employment is an indicator often used as a proxy for:
A) the quality and formality of employment
B) the unemployment rate
C) the dependency ratio
D) the literacy rate
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: A rising share of **regular salaried** work usually signals **better quality, more formal jobs**. It is examined with **social security coverage** and **written contracts** to assess formality.
SC: More regular jobs usually means better, more formal jobs.

@topic Labour Law & Data Systems
Q: Under the Code on Social Security, 2020, which categories of workers were recognised for the first time under central labour law?
A) Gig workers and platform workers
B) Government employees
C) Defence personnel
D) Agricultural landowners
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **Code on Social Security 2020** defines **gig workers** and **platform workers** and provides for social security schemes for them. Registration on **e-Shram** helps identify them.
SC: The SS Code 2020 first recognised gig and platform workers.

Q: Which data source would be most appropriate to estimate the number of workers in agriculture?
A) PLFS (household survey)
B) EPFO payroll data
C) ASI
D) QES
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Agriculture is largely **self-employed and informal**, outside **EPFO, ASI and QES** coverage. **PLFS** (and the Census) capture it through **households**.
SC: Farm workers show up in household surveys, not payrolls.
