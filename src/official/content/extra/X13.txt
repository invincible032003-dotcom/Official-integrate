Q: Which of the following is correct about the Census Act, 1948?
A) Both the Housing Census and the Population Census are taken under it; the Central Government may take a census whenever it considers it necessary, in the whole or any part of India
B) It mandates a census strictly every ten years, which cannot be postponed
C) It applies only to the Population Census and not to the Housing Census
D) It was repealed by the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: The **Census Act 1948** empowers the Centre to take a census **whenever necessary or desirable**, in **India or any part of it**, covering both **housing and population**. It was **amended in 1994**. The **decennial** practice is convention, not a statutory rule, which is why the 2021 round could shift to 2027. The **CoS Act 2008** repealed the **CoS Act 1953**, not the Census Act.
SC: The Census Act allows a census whenever needed; ten-yearly is convention; amended 1994.

Q: In the Indian Census, a "Census House" is defined as:
A) a building or part of a building used or recognised as a separate unit because it has a separate main entrance, used for residential or non-residential purposes or both
B) a house used only for residence
C) a group of persons eating from a common kitchen
D) any structure with a municipal house number
ANS: A
D: Medium
EXP: A **Census House** is a building or part of one with a **separate main entrance** (from a road, courtyard or staircase), used for **residential, non-residential or both**. A **household** is a group living together and **eating from a common kitchen**.
SC: A census house is identified by a separate entrance; a household by a common kitchen.

Q: Wages earned by a resident of country A from an enterprise resident in country B are included in:

@topic Nature of Official Statistics (PYQ angles)
Q: Official statistics are called a "public good" because:
1. They are largely financed from general tax revenue.
2. Their use by one person does not reduce their availability to others.
3. They are costly to produce but can be disseminated widely at very low additional cost.
4. Private persons are denied access to them.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1, 2 and 3 only
B) 1 and 2 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: A
T: Statement
D: Easy
EXP: Public goods are **non-rival** (and non-excludable) and **tax-financed**, with **high fixed and near-zero marginal cost**. Denying access contradicts **FPOS equal access**.
SC: A public good is non-rival and open to all.

Q: A "longitudinal panel survey" is one in which:
A) a fixed set of respondents is surveyed repeatedly over time
B) a new set of respondents is surveyed each time
C) a random set of respondents is surveyed only once
D) respondents are selected by quota
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: A **panel** follows the **same units** over time (e.g. **LASI**). A **repeated cross-section** draws a fresh sample each time (e.g. NSS rounds). A **rotational panel** (PLFS urban) mixes both by rotating part of the sample.
SC: Panel = same people again; cross-section = new people each time.

@topic Index Numbers (PYQ angles)
Q: For a set of commodities, Σp1q0 = 2,400, Σp0q0 = 2,000, Σp1q1 = 2,640 and Σp0q1 = 2,200. Fisher's ideal price index is:
A) 120
B) 110
C) 125
D) 115
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **L = 2400/2000 = 120**, **P = 2640/2200 = 120**, so **F = √(120 × 120) = 120**. When L = P, Fisher equals both.
SC: If L = P, Fisher equals them too.

Q: The simple aggregative price index for four commodities with base prices (10, 20, 30, 40) and current prices (15, 22, 36, 47) is:
A) 120
B) 125
C) 115
D) 130
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
EXP: **Σp1/Σp0 × 100 = (15 + 22 + 36 + 47)/(10 + 20 + 30 + 40) × 100 = 120/100 × 100 = 120**.
SC: Simple aggregative = sum of current ÷ sum of base.

Q: Index numbers primarily measure:
A) changes in the magnitude of a group of related variables over time or space
B) dispersion of values around the mean
C) correlation between two variables
D) the median of a distribution
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: Index numbers measure **relative change** in a group of related variables (prices, quantities, values) **over time or across places**. **Dispersion** is measured by the SD or CV.
SC: Index numbers measure change, not spread.

Q: If the consumption pattern of a society remains stable over a long period, which formula is the most convenient and economical for compiling a price index?
A) Laspeyres' formula
B) Paasche's formula
C) Fisher's formula
D) Marshall-Edgeworth formula
ANS: A
D: Easy
EXP: With **stable consumption**, **base weights** stay representative. **Laspeyres** then needs only **current prices** each period, while Paasche and Fisher need **current quantities** every time.
SC: Stable pattern means fixed base weights, so Laspeyres is simplest.

Q: The money wage of a worker is ₹12,000 and the CPI (2012 = 100) is 240. Which of the following is correct?
A) The real wage at 2012 prices is ₹5,000, and one rupee now is worth about 42 paise of 2012
B) The real wage is ₹28,800
C) The purchasing power of a rupee has risen
D) The real wage equals the money wage
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
EXP: **Real wage = 12,000 × 100/240 = ₹5,000**. **Purchasing power = 100/240 = 0.4167**, about **42 paise**.
SC: Real = money ÷ index × 100; a rupee is worth 100 ÷ index.
