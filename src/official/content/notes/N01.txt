@topic MoSPI: Structure & History

D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: MoSPI was formed on **15.10.1999** by merging the **Department of Statistics** and the **Department of Programme Implementation**. The CSO and NSSO merged later, into the **NSO**, in 2019.
SC: Statistics + Programme Implementation = MoSPI.

Q: The Programme Implementation Wing of MoSPI has which two Divisions?

C) Survey Design; Field Operations
D) National Accounts; Price Statistics
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1

SC: Two PI divisions: Projects (IPMD) and MP funds (MPLADS).

Q: Which committee, appointed in 1949, noticed large gaps in India's statistical database and recommended improvements in data collection?
A) National Income Committee
B) Mahalanobis Committee on Distribution of Income
C) Rangarajan Commission
D) Bowley-Robertson Committee
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: The **National Income Committee (1949)**, chaired by P.C. Mahalanobis, found gaps in the database. This led to the **Central Statistical Unit (1949)**, CSO (1951), Directorate of NSS (1950), the Computer Centre (1967) and NSSO (1970).
SC: The 1949 NIC found the gaps, and the CSU (1949) and NSS (1950) were created to fill them.

@topic Features of the Indian Statistical System

@topic Constitutional Provisions & Laws
Q: The subject "Statistics" is covered in the Seventh Schedule under:
A) Entry 94 of the Union List and Entry 45 of the Concurrent List
B) Entry 69 of the Union List only
C) Entry 30 of the State List
D) Entry 20 of the Union List
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: "Statistics" appears in **Union List Entry 94** and **Concurrent List Entry 45** (inquiries, surveys and statistics). Related entries: **Union Entry 69 (Census)**, **Concurrent Entry 20 (economic and social planning)** and **Concurrent Entry 30 (vital statistics)**.
SC: Statistics = Union 94 + Concurrent 45. Census = Union 69.

Q: Since "statistics" is not covered under the State List, statistics on matters in the State List fall under:
A) the Concurrent jurisdiction of the Centre and the States
B) exclusive State jurisdiction
C) exclusive Union jurisdiction
D) the jurisdiction of local bodies
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: There is no statistics entry in List II. So under **Concurrent Entry 45**, statistics for State List matters are a **concurrent** subject, and both the Centre and the States can legislate.
SC: No entry in the State List means the matter goes to the Concurrent List through Entry 45.

Q: "Economic and social planning" is listed in which entry of the Concurrent List?
A) Entry 20
B) Entry 30
C) Entry 45
D) Entry 94
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 4
EXP: **Concurrent List Entry 20** is economic and social planning. Entry 30 is vital statistics and Entry 45 is inquiries and statistics. Entry 94 is in the Union List.
SC: Concurrent 20 is planning, 30 is vital statistics, 45 is statistics.

@topic National Sample Survey: Design
Q: The National Sample Survey was initiated in 1950 on the proposal of:
A) Prof. P.C. Mahalanobis
B) Prof. P.V. Sukhatme
C) Dr. V.K.R.V. Rao
D) Prof. C.R. Rao
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 6
EXP: The **NSS (1950)** was set up on **Mahalanobis's proposal** to fill data gaps for planning through sample surveys. It is described as the **largest repetitive survey operation in the world**.
SC: Mahalanobis → NSS (1950) → sample surveys for planning.

Q: The standard NSS sampling design used almost from its inception is described as:
A) inter-penetrating, multi-stage, stratified, probability-proportional-to-size, circular systematic
B) simple random sampling without replacement
C) cluster sampling with equal probability
D) quota sampling
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: NSS has long used an **inter-penetrating, multi-stage, stratified, PPS, circular systematic** design. Inter-penetrating sub-samples allow the sampling error to be estimated easily.
SC: IPNS + multi-stage + stratified + PPS + CSS.

Q: The notes criticise the NSS's circular systematic sampling because:
A) although it allows error estimation through inter-penetrating sub-samples, it cannot relate the error to the sample size
B) it is too expensive
C) it does not allow stratification
D) it gives biased estimates of totals
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 6
EXP: Circular systematic sampling was a fine innovation in the 1950s. However, although sub-samples give an estimate of error, **the error cannot be related to sample size**. The notes also note that no methodological studies were done to review the design.
SC: The drawback is that error cannot be linked to n, so sample size is hard to plan.

Q: The objective of MoSPI's e-SIGMA is to:
A) convert data collection from paper schedules to CAPI with in-built validation, integrating SDRD, DQAD, FOD and SCD
B) digitise the Census
C) compute GDP in real time
D) monitor MPLADS works
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: **e-SIGMA** (e-Survey Instrument and Generalised Multimodal Application) moves data collection from paper to **CAPI** with built-in validation. It integrates **SDRD, DQAD, FOD and SCD** for faster, better-quality data.
SC: e-SIGMA = paper to CAPI across the NSO survey divisions.

Q: e-SIGMA is described as "multimodal" because it can support which survey modes?
A) CAPI, CATI and CAWI
B) PAPI only
C) Postal and telephonic only
D) Satellite and drone surveys
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: e-SIGMA can handle **CAPI** (personal interview), **CATI** (telephone interview) and **CAWI** (web interview).
SC: The three CA-modes: Personal, Telephone, Web.
