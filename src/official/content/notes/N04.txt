@topic NSC: Establishment & Composition
Q: The National Statistical Commission was set up through a Government Resolution dated ___ and constituted with effect from ___.
A) 1 June 2005; 12 July 2006
B) 12 July 2006; 1 June 2005
C) 15 October 1999; 1 June 2005
D) 5 November 2019; 10 January 2020
ANS: A
T: Chronology
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: The NSC was **set up by resolution on 1 June 2005** on the Rangarajan Commission's recommendation, and **constituted with effect from 12 July 2006**. Its mandate is to evolve policies, priorities and standards in statistical matters.
SC: Resolution in 2005, constituted in 2006.

Q: As per the notes, the composition of the NSC is:

C) Secretary MoSPI as Chairman + 4 DGs
D) Prime Minister as Chairman + all Chief Ministers
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: NSC = **part-time Chairman (MoS rank) + 4 part-time Members + CEO, NITI Aayog (ex officio)**. The **CSI & Secretary, MoSPI** is the **Secretary** of the NSC but **not a member**.
SC: 1 + 4 + 1 ex officio. The CSI is its Secretary, not a member.

@topic NSC: Functions & Powers
Q: The NSC's mandate consists of how many functions (clauses)?
A) 13 (clauses a to m)
B) 10
C) 8
D) 17
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: The NSC has a **13-point mandate (a to m)**, from identifying core statistics (a) to monitoring and reviewing the statistical system (m).
SC: The clauses run a to m, which is 13.

Q: Which of the following powers does the NSC have, as per the notes?
1. Require production of any document serving statistical purposes
2. Require statistical agencies to provide details of concepts, methodology, quality standards and errors for core statistics
3. Require attendance of any person including a public servant on matters connected with core statistics

A) 1 and 2 only
B) 2 and 3 only
C) 1 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: All three are NSC powers. It can also **issue notices for examination of witnesses and documents** on core statistics.
SC: The NSC can call for documents, details and persons, so all three apply.

Q: Which clause of the NSC mandate deals with advising the Government on legislative measures, including the statute for the NSC?

B) Clause (a)
C) Clause (j)
D) Clause (e)
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: **(l)**: advise on legislative measures, including the NSC statute. **(a)**: core statistics. **(e)**: strategies and release calendar. **(j)**: statistical audit.
SC: l for Legislation, j for audit (Judging quality), a for core statistics.

Q: The NSC function of evolving national strategies for the collection, tabulation and dissemination of core statistics includes:

@topic NSC: Background, Merger & Draft Bill
Q: The Rangarajan Commission, set up in January 2000, recommended that the permanent National Commission on Statistics be set up initially through:
A) a Government order (resolution)
B) a Constitutional amendment
C) an Act of Parliament
D) a Presidential ordinance
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: The Commission (report August 2001) recommended a permanent **National Commission on Statistics** as the nodal empowered body, **set up initially through a Government order**, with a statute to follow. This is why the NSC exists by resolution and an NSC Bill has been under discussion.
SC: The NSC was created by order first, with a statute to come later.

Q: The Government notification stating that CSO and NSSO would be merged into a single entity (NSO) acting as the executive wing per NSC policies is dated:
A) 5 November 2019
B) 1 June 2005
C) 23 May 2019
D) 10 January 2020
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 4
EXP: The notes cite the **Gazette notification (Government resolution) dated 5 November 2019**. It states that the NSO, headed by a Secretary-rank **CSI** who is also **Secretary of the NSC**, is the executive wing that follows NSC policies. The administrative merger order came in May 2019.
SC: The executive order came in May 2019 and the gazette resolution on 5 November 2019.

Q: When did MoSPI release the draft National Statistical Commission Bill, 2019?
A) 10 January 2020
B) 1 June 2005
C) 12 July 2006
D) 29 June 2019
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The **draft NSC Bill, 2019** was released on **10 January 2020**. It proposes a **nodal, autonomous statutory NSC** as a **body corporate**.
SC: Draft Bill dated 2019, released in January 2020.

Q: As per the draft NSC Bill, 2019, which of the following would be members of the Commission?
A) Deputy Governor (RBI) as member, Chief Economic Adviser as ex-officio member, and CSI as member
B) RBI Governor as Chairman
C) Finance Minister as ex-officio Chairman
D) Only academic members
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The draft Bill proposes a **full-time Chairman** and eminent members, including the **Deputy Governor (RBI)**, the **CEA (ex officio)** and the **CSI as a member**. Under the current resolution the CSI is only the NSC's Secretary.
SC: The Bill makes the CSI a member and adds the RBI Deputy Governor and the CEA.

Q: The draft NSC Bill provides for a National Statistical Audit and Assessment Organisation within the NSC, headed by:
A) a Chief Statistical Auditor in the rank of Secretary to GoI
B) the Comptroller and Auditor General
C) the Chief Statistician of India
D) the Director, ISI
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The Bill creates a **National Statistical Audit and Assessment Organisation** in the NSC, headed by a **Chief Statistical Auditor (Secretary rank)**. It also provides an **independent secretariat** under a Secretary-rank officer.
SC: The statistical audit body is headed by the Chief Statistical Auditor.

Q: To give the NSC financial autonomy, the draft Bill envisages:
A) an independent National Statistical Fund
B) a grant from NITI Aayog
C) user fees on data
D) a share of GST
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The Bill envisages an **independent National Statistical Fund** to support the NSC's financial autonomy.
SC: A separate fund gives financial independence.

Q: Under the draft NSC Bill, which function allows the NSC to take suo-motu cognisance of legislative, administrative and technology issues of Centre and States and advise the government?
A) Advisory
B) Rationalisation
C) Standardisation
D) Core statistics
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: The Bill's functions are **core statistics** (mandatory standards), **standardisation** (priorities and audit), **rationalisation** (linking budget and statistical programmes) and **advisory**, which includes **suo motu** action.
SC: Suo motu advice is the Advisory function.

Q: The "Rationalisation" function under the draft NSC Bill aims to:
A) ensure strong coordination through close linkage between budget and statistical programming agencies
B) reduce the number of statistical staff
C) merge all surveys into one
D) privatise data collection
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 5
EXP: **Rationalisation** means close **linkage between budget and statistical programming agencies** to produce robust data without duplication.
SC: Rationalisation links budgets to statistical programmes.

Q: Which of the following is cited in the notes as a significance of the NSC Bill?
A) It provides statutory backing to data-collecting agencies to increase credibility and legitimacy of data
B) It abolishes the NSO
C) It makes all data confidential
D) It transfers the Census to the NSC
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 5
EXP: The Bill **gives teeth** to the NSC, supports **credible data** against fake or unreliable figures, provides **statutory backing** to data-collecting agencies, and lets the NSC work at **arm's length from government**.
SC: Statutory backing brings credibility and independence.

Q: The notes give which example to illustrate the need for data sharing under the NSC Bill?
A) Data on homeless people could not be shared with the agency providing housing
B) Census data could not be shared with States
C) GDP data could not be shared with the IMF
D) Tax data could not be shared with courts
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 5
EXP: The notes cite **data on homeless people** that **could not be shared with the housing agency**. This shows why structured, statutory data sharing is needed.
SC: The example is homeless data not reaching the housing agency.

@topic FOD: Network & Crop Statistics
Q: The Field Operations Division of NSO has its headquarters at:
A) Delhi/Faridabad
B) Kolkata
C) Nagpur
D) Greater Noida
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **FOD HQ: Delhi/Faridabad**, with **6 Zonal, 53 Regional and 116 Sub-Regional Offices**. It collects primary data for MoSPI surveys.
SC: The field HQ is in Delhi/Faridabad.

Q: Under the ICS scheme, FOD undertakes sample checks on area enumeration in approximately how many villages in an agricultural season?
A) 5,000
B) 16,000
C) 1,181
D) 12,800
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: Under **ICS**, FOD checks **area enumeration in about 5,000 villages per season** and **supervises about 16,000 crop-cutting experiments** each agricultural year.
SC: 5,000 villages for area, 16,000 CCEs for yield.

Q: Under the ICS scheme, FOD supervises about how many crop-cutting experiments in each agricultural year?
A) 16,000
B) 5,000
C) 50,000
D) 1,000
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: About **16,000 CCEs** are supervised each year. The data are also used to compute **yield rates** of specified crops under ICS, and a season-wise status report goes to each State government.
SC: 16k CCEs supervised, 5k villages checked.

Q: The objective of the ICS scheme is to:
A) locate deficiencies in the system of collection of crop statistics by supervising primary field work and suggest remedies
B) replace State crop estimates with FOD estimates
C) compute MSP
D) issue crop insurance
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **ICS** exercises **meaningful supervision** over primary field work (area enumeration, CCEs) to **find deficiencies** and **suggest remedies** that strengthen area and yield statistics.
SC: ICS is supervision to improve State crop data.

@topic FOD: Surveys & Price Collection

B) every year in all towns
C) once in 10 years with the Census
D) only in metro cities
ANS: A

SC: UFS runs as a 5-year phased cycle.

Q: NSO conducted the first national Time Use Survey during:
A) January 2019 to December 2019
B) July 1998 to June 1999
C) July 2017 to June 2018
D) January 2024 to December 2024
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: The **first national TUS** was held from **January to December 2019**. It measured participation of men, women and other groups in **paid and unpaid activities**, including unpaid care, volunteer work and domestic services. The 1998-99 survey was a six-State pilot.
SC: TUS national in 2019. The 1998-99 survey was a pilot.

Q: The primary objective of the Time Use Survey is to:
A) measure participation of men, women and other groups in paid and unpaid activities
B) measure household consumption
C) estimate industrial output
D) measure internet usage
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: TUS measures **time spent on paid and unpaid activities**: unpaid care, volunteering, domestic services, and also learning, socialising, leisure and self-care.
SC: Time use shows unpaid work, especially women's.

Q: Price collection for CPI (Urban) by FOD on behalf of PSD started in:
A) May 2008
B) September 2018
C) January 2012
D) April 2017
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: FOD started **CPI (Urban)** price collection in **May 2008**. It took over **CPI (Rural)** from the **Department of Posts in September 2018**. It also collects rural retail prices for **CPI-AL/RL** (compiled by the **Labour Bureau, Shimla**).
SC: Urban from 2008, rural from 2018 (taken over from DoP).

Q: CPI (AL/RL), for which FOD collects monthly rural retail prices, is compiled and published by:
A) Labour Bureau, Shimla
B) PSD, NSO
C) Office of the Economic Adviser
D) RBI
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: FOD collects the prices, but the **Labour Bureau, Shimla** (Ministry of Labour & Employment) **compiles and publishes CPI-AL/RL**.
SC: FOD collects and the Labour Bureau compiles.

D: Medium

EXP: FOD **facilitates WPI data collection and transmission** from organised-sector factories. The **OEA, DPIIT** compiles and releases the WPI monthly.
