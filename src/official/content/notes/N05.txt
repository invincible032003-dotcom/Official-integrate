Q: The standard sampling design used at present in NSS surveys is:
A) two- or three-stage stratified random sampling with circular systematic PPS selection
B) single-stage simple random sampling without replacement
C) cluster sampling with equal probability selection
D) quota sampling with purposive selection of villages
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: NSS uses a **stratified multi-stage (two- or three-stage)** design. Units are selected by the **circular systematic method with probability proportional to size (PPS)**.
SC: NSS = stratified multi-stage, circular systematic PPS.

ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 7

SC: First stage is the village or block; the last stage is the household or enterprise.

Q: In the Time Use Survey, "average time spent in a day per person" for an activity is calculated by:
A) considering all persons irrespective of whether they participated in the activity
B) considering only the persons who participated in the activity
C) dividing participants by total population
D) considering only the working-age population
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: **Per person** uses **all persons** as the denominator, so the full **1,440 minutes** are distributed across activities. **Per participant** uses **only the participants**. The **participation rate** is the **% of persons** doing the activity in the day.
SC: Per person counts everyone; per participant counts only the doers.

Q: The distribution of total time of 1440 minutes of a day across activities in the TUS is obtained from:
A) average time spent in a day per person
B) average time spent in a day per participant
C) participation rate
D) principal status activity
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: Averaging over **all persons** makes the activity times add up to **1440 minutes**, a full day. The **per-participant** averages do not add up to 1440.
SC: Only the per-person averages sum to 1440 minutes.

D: Medium

EXP: **365 days** applies to **income, usual status, durables/clothing/footwear in CES, and enterprise inputs/outputs**. **Current weekly status** uses **7 days**, and routine consumer expenditure uses **30 days**.
SC: Items marked usual or durable use 365 days; weekly status uses 7.

Q: A moving reference period, as adopted by NSO, has the advantage that it:
A) ensures coverage of the entire period and eliminates the seasonality effect when aggregated
B) removes all recall bias
C) eliminates the need for sub-rounds
D) makes the data comparable with the Census
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 8
EXP: A **moving reference period**, spread over the whole survey year, **covers the entire period** and **eliminates seasonality** once aggregated. If the period is too long it causes **recall lapse**; if too short it misses items.
SC: A moving window averages out the seasons.

Q: Under the Uniform Reference Period (URP), consumer expenditure data are collected for each item for the last:
A) 30 days
B) 365 days
C) 7 days
D) 90 days
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 8
EXP: **URP** uses **30 days for all items**. **MRP** uses **365 days** for clothing, bedding, footwear, institutional medical, education and durables, and **30 days** for the rest.
SC: Uniform means 30 days for everything.

Q: Under the Modified Mixed Reference Period (MMRP), which of the following items have a 7-day reference period?
A) Edible oil, egg, fish and meat, vegetables, fruits, spices, beverages, processed foods, pan, tobacco and intoxicants
B) Clothing, bedding and footwear
C) Cereals, pulses and fuel and light
D) Education and institutional medical expenses
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 9
EXP: **MMRP: 7 days** for high-frequency foods (edible oil, egg/fish/meat, vegetables, fruits, spices, beverages, processed food, pan/tobacco). **365 days** for clothing, bedding, footwear, education, institutional medical and durables. **30 days** for all other food, fuel and light, misc goods and services, non-institutional medical, and rents and taxes.
SC: MMRP: perishables 7 days, durables 365, everything else 30.

Q: Under the MMRP, cereals, pulses, and fuel and light fall under which reference period?
A) 30 days
B) 7 days
C) 365 days
D) 1 day
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 9
EXP: Under MMRP only the listed high-frequency foods take **7 days**. **All other food** (cereals, pulses, milk, sugar), **fuel and light**, misc goods and services, non-institutional medical, and rents and taxes take **30 days**.
SC: Anything not on the 7-day or 365-day lists defaults to 30 days.

Q: Estimates based on MMRP are considered more robust and are internationally used to compile CPI weights because:
A) short reference periods for high-frequency food items reduce memory lapse, and longer periods suit infrequent purchases
B) MMRP uses the same 30-day period for all items
C) MMRP excludes durable goods
D) MMRP is based on administrative data
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 9
EXP: **MMRP** uses **short recall (7 days)** for **frequent purchases**, reducing **memory lapse**, and **long recall (365 days)** for **infrequent** ones. This makes it the most robust of the three, and it is **internationally practised** for compiling **CPI weights**.
SC: MMRP matches the recall length to how often an item is bought, which is why CPI weights use it.

Q: Assertion (A): In NSS inquiries, the reference period should not be too long.
Reason (R): A long reference period lowers data quality because of recall lapse on the part of the informant.
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Medium
P: 8
EXP: A **long** reference period brings in **recall lapse**. A **too short** one may **miss relevant items**. So its length depends on the item, e.g. **7 days** for CWS, **30 days** for most consumption and **365 days** for durables and usual status. R explains A.
SC: Too long causes recall error; too short misses items; the length fits the item.
