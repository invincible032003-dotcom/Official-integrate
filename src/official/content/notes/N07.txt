@topic Agricultural Statistics System & Crop Area
Q: The agricultural statistics system in India is best described as:
A) decentralised both horizontally and vertically, with primary data collected by States and consolidated by DES, MoA&FW
B) fully centralised with NSO collecting all primary data
C) decentralised only vertically, with districts reporting directly to NSO
D) based entirely on remote sensing by ISRO
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **States collect** primary statistics through the **State Agricultural Statistics Authority (SASA)**. The **Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES)**, Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, **consolidates** them for India. DES provides statistics on area, production, land use, cost, yield and MSP.
SC: States collect through SASA and DES consolidates.

Q: In the temporarily settled (land record) States, crop area statistics are compiled through a complete enumeration of all fields in every village each season, known as:
A) Girdawari
B) Khasra
C) Jamabandi
D) Chowkidari
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **Girdawari** is the **complete field-by-field enumeration** by the **patwari** (village revenue agency) each crop season. It covers **land use, irrigation and crop area**. The **Khasra register** is the **village crop register** where these entries are recorded.
SC: Girdawari is the patwari's field-by-field crop count.

Q: The land record (temporarily settled) system for crop area statistics is followed in:
A) 18 States and 5 UTs
B) 16 States and 3 UTs
C) 22 States and 2 UTs
D) 12 States only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 1
EXP: The **land record system (patwari/girdawari)** covers **18 States and 5 UTs**. **TRS** is implemented in **16 land-record States**.
SC: Land records cover 18 States + 5 UTs; TRS runs in 16 States.

Q: Which of the following were the "permanently settled" States with no village-level land revenue agency, where crop area statistics are collected through sample surveys?
A) Kerala, Odisha and West Bengal
B) Punjab, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh
C) Assam, Manipur and Meghalaya
D) Bihar, Jharkhand and Odisha
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: The **permanently settled States are Kerala, Odisha and West Bengal**. Here specially appointed staff under **EARAS** collect crop area data. Girdawari is done in a **20% random sample of villages**, so the whole State is covered in **5 years**.
SC: Kerala, Odisha, West Bengal = the permanently settled States (EARAS).

Q: The three categories of States (land record, permanently settled, conventional estimates) account for what percentages, respectively, of the total reporting area?
A) 86, 9 and 5
B) 70, 20 and 10
C) 80, 15 and 5
D) 90, 7 and 3
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: **Land record States: 86%**, **permanently settled: 9%**, **conventional estimates: 5%** of the reporting area. The 86% figure recurs: in the Agriculture Census, land-record retabulation also covers about **86%** of reported area.
SC: 86-9-5 by reporting area.

Q: In which of the following areas are only conventional crop estimates, based on personal assessment such as by village chowkidars, available?
A) Hill districts of Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, and A&N Islands and Lakshadweep
B) Kerala, Odisha and West Bengal

EXP: **Conventional estimates** apply to the **NE hill areas** (hill districts of Assam, Arunachal, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland; parts of Sikkim and Tripura) and to **A&N and Lakshadweep**. Tripura and Sikkim are mostly **cadastrally surveyed**.
SC: Conventional estimates cover the NE hills and the islands.

Q: Land-use statistics in India follow a classification of how many categories?
A) Nine
B) Seven
C) Five
D) Twelve
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: The **nine-fold classification**: forests; non-agricultural use; barren and unculturable; permanent pastures and grazing; miscellaneous tree crops; **culturable waste**; fallow other than current fallows; **current fallows**; **net area sown**.
SC: Land use has a 9-fold classification from patwari records.

Q: Which of the following is NOT one of the categories of the nine-fold land-use classification?
A) Area under double cropping
B) Culturable waste land
C) Current fallows
D) Miscellaneous tree crops
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **Area sown more than once** is derived from gross cropped area minus net area sown. It is not one of the nine categories. The nine include **culturable waste**, **current fallows**, **misc tree crops** and **net area sown**.
SC: Double-cropped area is derived, not a land-use category.

@topic Crop Production, GCES & Forecasts
Q: Crop production estimates in India are obtained as:
A) area under the crop × yield rate estimated from crop-cutting experiments under GCES
B) market arrivals × a conversion factor
C) satellite imagery alone
D) farmers' self-reported production
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **Production = Area × Yield**. The **yield rate** comes from **crop-cutting experiments (CCEs)** under the **General Crop Estimation Survey (GCES)**.
SC: Production is area from girdawari times yield from CCE.

Q: The sampling design of the General Crop Estimation Survey is:
A) stratified three-stage random sampling with tehsil/taluka as stratum, village as FSU, field as SSU and plot as the ultimate unit
B) two-stage sampling with district as stratum and village as USU
C) simple random sampling of fields
D) systematic sampling of farmers
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **GCES** uses a **stratified 3-stage** design: **stratum = tehsil/taluka**; **FSU = village**; **SSU = field growing the crop**; **USU = plot (usually 5 m × 5 m)**. The produce of the plot is harvested and weighed. Precision is set to give reliable yields at the **State and major-district** level.
SC: GCES goes tehsil → village → field → 5×5 m plot.

Q: The usual size of the plot harvested in a crop-cutting experiment under GCES is:
A) 5 m × 5 m
B) 10 m × 10 m
C) 1 m × 1 m
D) 2 m × 5 m
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: The ultimate unit is a plot of **5 m × 5 m (25 sq m)**. Marking, harvesting and weighing its produce gives the basic data for yield.
SC: A CCE plot is 5 × 5 m.

Q: Which crop forecast is mostly based on reports by States guided by the visual observation of field officials?
A) First forecast (kharif)
B) Second forecast
C) Third forecast
D) Fourth forecast
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The **1st forecast (kharif)** rests on **visual observation**. The **2nd** adds input data, pests and diseases, weekly State reports and **remote sensing**. The **3rd** adds **Market Intelligence Units, IMD and the Crop Weather Watch Group (CWWG)**. The **4th** uses **firm SASA figures** from GCES yields.
SC: 1st visual, 2nd adds remote sensing, 3rd adds MIU/IMD/CWWG, 4th uses firm GCES figures.

Q: The fourth crop forecast is based on:
A) firm figures supplied by State Agricultural Statistics Authorities with yield estimates through GCES
B) visual observation by field officials
C) econometric models of IEG only
D) information from the Crop Weather Watch Group only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: By the **4th forecast** the SASAs have **dependable GCES yields**, so the figures are "firm". After the forecasts come the **Final Estimates**, and then the **"Fully Revised"** estimates in the next crop year for late State revisions.
SC: The 4th forecast is firm because the CCE yields are in.

Q: As per the notes' table, the DES advance estimate released in September covers:
A) only kharif crops
B) kharif and rabi crops
C) only rabi crops
D) horticulture crops only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: Per the notes' table: **1st AE (September): kharif only**. **2nd AE (February): kharif + rabi**. **3rd AE (April-May): rabi area, better kharif yields**. **4th AE (July-August): area and yield of both seasons**. The **Final Estimate** comes in February of the following year. Coverage: **28 major crops** (AE) and **28 major + 3 minor** (final).
SC: The September AE is kharif only; the rest add rabi.
TIP: DES has revised its release calendar in recent years. For current practice, check the latest DES release schedule.

1. Arhar (tur)
2. Soybean
3. Chickpea (gram)
4. Pearl millet (bajra)
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1, 2 and 4 only
B) 1 and 3 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4

@topic Agriculture Census & Livestock Census
Q: The Agriculture Census in India has been conducted quinquennially since:
A) 1970-71, as part of the FAO World Census of Agriculture programme
B) 1951, as part of the Five-Year Plans
C) 1919-20
D) 1980-81
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: The Agriculture Census has been **quinquennial since 1970-71** under the **FAO's World Census of Agriculture (WCA)**, which itself is **decennial**. India runs it every 5 years because holding structure changes fast (**fragmentation, urbanisation, industrialisation**).
SC: India's Agriculture Census runs every 5 years from 1970-71; the FAO's WCA every 10.

Q: The basic statistical unit for data collection in the Agriculture Census is:
A) operational holding
B) ownership holding
C) household
D) village
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 5
EXP: The unit is the **operational holding**, because **farm decisions are taken by those who operate the land**, not by those who own it. The **Livestock Census** unit is the **household**.
SC: The Agriculture Census counts operators, not owners.

Q: As per the notes, an operational holding is defined as all land used wholly or partly for agricultural production and operated as one technical unit by one person alone or with others:
A) without regard to title, legal form, size or location
B) only if owned by the operator
C) only if located in one village
D) only if its size exceeds 0.5 hectare
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 13
EXP: An **operational holding** = all land used wholly or partly for agricultural production and **operated as one technical unit**, **without regard to title, legal form, size or location**.
SC: Operation decides a holding; title, size and location do not matter.

Land-record States retabulate khasra data (about 86% of area); others use a sample household enquiry. **Phase 2**: **20% sample villages** for tenancy, land use, irrigation and cropping pattern. **Phase 3 (Input Survey)**: **7% villages** for input use.
SC: The phases are ownership (retabulation), 20% detail, then a 7% input survey.

@topic IAS Scheme: TRS, EARAS, ICS
Q: The scheme for Improvement of Agricultural Statistics (IAS), in operation since the early 1970s, has which three components?
A) TRS, EARAS and ICS
B) GCES, FASAL and MNCFC
C) TRS, GCES and CCS
D) EARAS, NADAMS and FASAL
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 9
EXP: The **IAS** scheme has three parts: **TRS (Timely Reporting Scheme)**, **EARAS (Establishment of an Agency for Reporting Agricultural Statistics)** and **ICS (Improvement of Crop Statistics)**.
SC: IAS = TRS + EARAS + ICS.

Q: Under the Timely Reporting Scheme (TRS), the patwari completes girdawari on priority in a random sample of what percentage of villages?
A) 20 per cent
B) 10 per cent
C) 7 per cent
D) 50 per cent
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 9
EXP: **TRS**, in **16 land-record States**, uses a **20% random sample of villages** to give **season-wise area** of principal crops, split by irrigated/unirrigated and traditional/HYV, **by a set date**. The sample rotates so the whole temporarily settled area is covered in **5 years**. It also gives the **frame for CCE field selection**.
SC: TRS uses a 20% sample and rotates over 5 years.

Q: The EARAS component of IAS is implemented in:
A) Kerala, Odisha, West Bengal and four NE States: Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura
B) 16 land-record States
C) all States and UTs
D) only the Union Territories
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 9
EXP: **EARAS** covers **3 non-land-record States (Kerala, Odisha, West Bengal)** plus **4 NE States (Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura)**. There a **full-time agency** collects area, production and yield using the **TRS methodology**.
SC: EARAS covers Kerala, Odisha, West Bengal and 4 NE States.

Q: Under the ICS scheme, supervisors physically verify the patwari's girdawari in a sub-sample of TRS villages covering:
A) four clusters of five survey numbers each
B) five clusters of four survey numbers each

ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 10
EXP: **ICS** supervisors check **4 clusters × 5 survey numbers** in sub-sampled TRS villages. They measure the **discrepancies** between supervisor and patwari area entries, and check the village crop abstract for **totalling errors** and **timely dispatch**.
SC: ICS checks 4 clusters of 5 survey numbers.

Q: The cost of cultivation and production estimates generated under the Comprehensive Scheme are used for which of the following?

D: Easy

EXP: The data are used by **CACP for MSP**, by **MoSPI for NAS**, and for compiling the **marketed surplus ratio**.
SC: Cost of cultivation data serve MSP, NAS and the marketed surplus ratio.

Q: In the cost of cultivation survey, the first-stage and second-stage units are selected by:
A) PPSWR with size measure "area under the crop complex"
B) SRSWOR
C) systematic sampling with equal probability
D) PPSWOR with size measure "population"
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 10
EXP: Each **agro-climatic zone is a stratum**. **Stage 1: tehsils/blocks** by **PPSWR** (size = area under the crop complex). **Stage 2: villages/clusters** by **PPSWR** (same size measure). **Stage 3: operational holdings** by **SRSWOR**, **2 from each of 5 size classes**.
SC: Tehsils and villages by PPSWR on crop area; holdings by SRSWOR, 2 × 5.

Q: In the cost of cultivation survey, how many operational holdings are selected from each selected village?
A) 10 (2 from each of 5 land-size classes)
B) 5 (1 from each of 5 size classes)
C) 20 (4 from each of 5 size classes)
D) 8 (2 from each of 4 size classes)
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 11
EXP: There are **5 size classes**: <1 ha, 1-2 ha, 2-4 ha, 4-6 ha and >6 ha. **2 holdings per class by SRSWOR** gives **10 holdings per village**. Data come through the **cost accounting method**, with daily records of inputs, outputs and prices.
SC: 5 classes × 2 holdings = 10.

Q: The fieldwork in the cost of cultivation scheme is carried out using:
A) the cost accounting method, with day-to-day records
B) a one-time recall interview at harvest
C) the crop-cutting method
D) satellite estimation
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 11
EXP: The **cost accounting method** keeps a **detailed day-to-day record** of inputs, outputs, prices paid and prices received for each sample holding.
SC: Cost of cultivation relies on daily cost accounts.

Q: The synthetic method of small area estimation is based on the assumption that:
A) the small area behaves similarly to the large area
B) the small area has a larger sample than the large area
C) no auxiliary information is needed
D) small area estimates are always unbiased
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 13
EXP: A **synthetic estimate** scales an **unbiased large-area estimate** down to sub-areas using **auxiliary information**. This is the **scaling-down** approach. It assumes the **small area behaves like the large area**; if that fails, the estimate can be **heavily biased**.
SC: Synthetic means scaled down, and it is biased if the small area differs.
