@topic CPI for Industrial Workers
Q: The current series of CPI for Industrial Workers (CPI-IW) has base year:
A) 2016 = 100
B) 2001 = 100
C) 2012 = 100
D) 1986-87 = 100
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **CPI-IW: base 2016**, compiled by the **Labour Bureau (MoLE)**. Its weights come from the **Working Class Family Income and Expenditure Survey (WCFIES)**. It replaced the **2001 = 100** series.
SC: CPI-IW is 2016, Labour Bureau, WCFIES.

Q: Under CPI-IW, a "working class family" must satisfy which of the following conditions?
1. It is located within the centre for industrial workers.
2. At least one member works as a manual worker in an establishment in any of the seven covered sectors.
3. It derived 50 per cent or more of its income during the preceding calendar month through manual work.
4. At least one member is a Government employee.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 1, 2 and 3 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: B
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: The working class family has three conditions: **located in the centre**, **one manual worker in the 7 sectors**, and **≥ 50% of income from manual work** in the preceding month. There is no Government-employee condition, although CPI-IW is used for **Government DA**.
SC: The conditions are the centre, a manual worker, and 50% of income from manual work.

Q: Which of the following is NOT one of the seven sectors of employment covered for defining the working class family under CPI-IW?
A) Construction
B) Plantations
C) Ports and docks
D) Railways
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: The **7 sectors** are **factories, plantations, mining, ports and docks, public motor transport undertakings, electricity generation and distribution establishments, and railways**. **Construction is not included**.
SC: The seven sectors are factories, plantations, mines, ports, buses, power and rail; construction is not among them.

Q: CPI-IW is mainly used for:
A) determination of Dearness Allowance for Government employees and industrial workers, and fixation of minimum wages in scheduled employments
B) fixation of minimum wages in the agricultural sector
C) the RBI's inflation targeting
D) deflating GDP at the national level
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **CPI-IW → DA** of Central and State Government employees and industrial workers, **minimum wages** in scheduled employments, and moving **urban poverty lines**. **CPI-AL → agricultural minimum wages** and **rural poverty lines**. The **RBI targets CPI (Combined)**.
SC: CPI-IW is used for DA and urban poverty lines; CPI-AL for farm wages and rural poverty lines.

Q: The weighting diagram of CPI-IW is derived from:
A) the Working Class Family Income and Expenditure Survey
B) the NSS 68th round consumer expenditure survey
C) the NSS 38th round
D) the Annual Survey of Industries
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: **CPI-IW** weights come from the **WCFIES** of the Labour Bureau. **CPI-R/U/C** weights come from the **NSS 68th round (2011-12)**. **CPI-AL/RL** weights come from the **NSS 38th round (1983)**.
SC: IW uses WCFIES, R/U/C the 68th round, AL/RL the 38th round.

@topic CPI for Agricultural & Rural Labourers
Q: A rural labour household is classified as an agricultural labour household if it earns what share of its total income from manual labour in agriculture during the last 365 days?
A) 50 per cent or more
B) More than 75 per cent
C) At least 25 per cent
D) 100 per cent
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: A **rural labour household** derives its **major income from manual rural labour** in the last 365 days. Those earning **≥ 50% from agricultural manual labour** are **agricultural labour households**, a major **subset** of rural labour households.
SC: Rural labour households are the whole set; those with ≥ 50% from farm labour are agricultural labour households.

Q: The weights of CPI (AL/RL) with base 1986-87 were derived from the average consumer expenditure per household in:
A) the NSS 38th round (1983)
B) the NSS 50th round (1993-94)
C) the NSS 68th round (2011-12)
D) the NSS 61st round (2004-05)
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: **CPI-AL/RL (1986-87 = 100)** uses weights from the **NSS 38th round CES (1983)**. That survey ran over the agricultural year (July-June) and was quinquennial. This old weight base is often cited as a reason CPI-AL/RL needs revision.
SC: AL/RL weights come from the 38th round (1983).

Q: Which of the following statements about CPI (AL/RL) is/are correct?
1. It is compiled by the Labour Bureau.
2. It is used for revising minimum wages in the agricultural sector.
3. It is used to move base-year rural poverty lines to subsequent years.

B) 1 and 2 only

D) 1, 2 and 3

P: 2
EXP: All three statements are correct: **Labour Bureau**, **agricultural minimum wages** and **rural poverty lines**.
SC: AL is used for rural wages and rural poverty.

Q: Rural labourers, for the purpose of CPI-RL, include:
A) both agricultural labourers and other (non-agricultural) manual labourers in rural areas
B) only agricultural labourers

@topic CPI (Rural / Urban / Combined)
Q: The CPI (Rural), CPI (Urban) and CPI (Combined) series were first started by the CSO in January 2011 with base year:
A) 2010
B) 2012
C) 2011
D) 2004-05
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: The CPI (R/U/C) **started in January 2011 with base 2010**. It was **revised to base 2012**, with the revised series released from **January 2015**, and is compiled by the **NSO (PSD), MoSPI**.
SC: CPI-R/U/C first had base 2010 (from 2011); now base 2012 (from 2015).

Q: The weights of CPI (R/U/C) with base 2012 are derived from:
A) the NSS 68th round consumer expenditure survey (2011-12)
B) the NSS 61st round (2004-05)
C) the Working Class Family Income and Expenditure Survey
D) the Population Census 2011
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: The weights are the **average monthly consumer expenditure** of urban and rural households from the **68th round CES (2011-12)**, on the **MMRP** basis.
SC: CPI 2012 uses 68th-round (2011-12) weights.

Q: Price data for CPI (R/U/C) are collected from:
A) 1114 urban markets and 1181 villages on a weekly roster by FOD field staff
B) 1181 urban markets and 1114 villages monthly by State DES
C) 78 centres by the Labour Bureau
D) 600 villages by the Department of Posts only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 3
EXP: CPI prices are collected from **1114 urban markets and 1181 villages** in all States and UTs. **FOD** field staff collect them by personal visits on a **weekly roster**. Rural prices were collected by the **Department of Posts** until **September 2018**, when **FOD** took over.
SC: 1114 urban markets, 1181 villages; urban has fewer.

Q: All-India inflation based on CPI is calculated on a point-to-point basis, which means:
A) current month's index over the same month of the previous year
B) current month's index over the previous month
C) average of 12 months over the previous 12 months
D) current month's index over the base year
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **Point-to-point (y-o-y)** inflation compares the **current month with the same month last year**, e.g. February 2022 over February 2021. Month-over-month would be a sequential rate.
SC: Point-to-point means year-on-year for the same month.

Q: In CPI (Combined) base 2012, which group has the highest weight?

B) Miscellaneous

Q: The "Housing" group in CPI (2012 series) is compiled for:
A) urban areas only

@topic CPI (UNME) — Discontinued

EXP: **CPI-UNME** was released by the **CSO from January 1961**. Its **last base was 1984-85**, with weights from the **family living survey of 1982-83 in 59 urban centres**. It was released on the **25th of the following month**.
SC: UNME ran from 1961, last base 1984-85, 59 centres.

Q: The weighting diagram for the last series of CPI (UNME), base 1984-85, was prepared from a family living survey conducted during 1982-83 in how many urban centres?
A) 59
B) 78
C) 88
D) 1114
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 4
EXP: The **family living survey for UNME (1982-83)** was conducted in **59 urban centres**. For comparison, the old CPI-IW (2001) series used **78 centres** and the CPI-IW 2016 series **88 centres**.
SC: UNME 59, old IW 78, new IW 88.

Q: In February 2008, the NSC decided to:
A) discontinue CPI (UNME) and compile linked CPI (UNME) numbers using CPI-IW sub-group indices with UNME weights until the new CPI (Urban) is released
B) merge CPI (UNME) with WPI
C) revise the base of CPI (UNME) to 2001
D) transfer CPI (UNME) to the Labour Bureau
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 4
EXP: Because of the **outdated base** and the need to redeploy price-collection staff for the new **CPI (Urban)**, the NSC (February 2008) decided to: (i) **discontinue UNME**; (ii) compile a **link index** by the **ratio method** from **CPI-IW sub-group indices** using **UNME weights**; (iii) publish **linked UNME** until the new CPI (U).
SC: UNME was stopped in 2008 and linked through CPI-IW until CPI (U) arrived.

D: Hard

EXP: The linked UNME series covered **April 2008 to December 2010**. From **January 2011**, the new **CPI (Urban)** with base 2010 took over.

Q: The target population of the discontinued CPI (UNME) was a family deriving 50% or more of its income from:
A) non-manual work in the non-agricultural sector in urban areas

Q: Which of the following statements about WPI (2011-12) is/are correct?
1. It does not cover services.
2. It does not include indirect taxes.
3. It comprises 697 items.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 only
B) 1 and 2 only
C) 2 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: **WPI excludes services and indirect taxes** and covers **697 items**. Of these, **564** are manufactured products, **117** primary articles and **16** fuel and power.
SC: WPI covers 697 goods only, with no services and no taxes.

Q: The weight of an item in WPI is based on its:
A) net traded value in the base year (output + imports − exports)
B) share in household consumption expenditure
C) share in GVA
D) share in exports
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: **WPI weight ∝ Net Traded Value = Gross value of output + Imports − Exports** in the base year. **CPI** weights come from **household consumption (CES)**.
SC: WPI weights by production and trade; CPI by consumption.

Q: WPI is useful for policy because:
A) it estimates inflation at the wholesale transaction level, enabling timely intervention before price increases spill over to retail prices
B) it includes services and taxes
C) it measures cost of living of households
D) it is the RBI's official inflation target
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: WPI tracks **first-stage bulk transaction** prices. It acts as a **leading signal**, so the Government can act **before** price rises reach **retail (CPI)**. The RBI's inflation target is **CPI-C (4% ± 2%)**.
SC: WPI gives an early warning; CPI is the target.

@topic CPI vs WPI & Concepts
Q: Which of the following correctly distinguishes CPI from WPI?
A) CPI prices are retail prices paid by consumers; WPI prices are collected at ex-factory, ex-mine and mandi level
B) Both include services
C) WPI weights are from CES; CPI weights from production values
D) CPI has a lower weight for food than WPI
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
P: 6
EXP: **CPI**: retail prices, **CES weights**, includes **services** (housing, education, health), **food about 39%**. **WPI**: first-stage bulk prices at ex-factory, ex-mine and mandi level, **production-value weights**, **no services**, **food about 24%**.
SC: CPI is retail, consumption-weighted and includes services; WPI is wholesale, production-weighted and covers goods only.

Q: Which of the following is included in CPI but not in WPI?
A) Education and medical care services
B) Manufactured food products
C) Fuel and power
D) Minerals
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 6
EXP: CPI includes **services** (housing, education, medical care, recreation, transport). WPI covers **goods only**.
SC: If it is a service, only CPI has it.

Q: "Price reference year" in the context of CPI means:
A) the year whose average prices are taken as base prices and equated to 100
B) the year whose CES data are used to compute weights
C) the year of release of the index
D) the year in which the item basket was last revised
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: **Price reference year** = the year whose **average prices = 100**, e.g. 2012 for CPI-C. **Weight reference year** = the year whose **CES data** give the weights, e.g. 2011-12 for CPI-C. The two can differ.
SC: The price year sets the base of 100; the weight year supplies the weights.

Q: For CPI (Combined) base 2012, the price reference year and weight reference year are respectively:
A) 2012 and 2011-12
B) 2011-12 and 2012
C) 2012 and 2012
D) 2010 and 2011-12
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: **Price reference = 2012** (calendar year average prices = 100). **Weight reference = 2011-12** (68th round CES).
SC: Prices from 2012, weights from 2011-12.

Q: A "weighting diagram" of a CPI gives:
A) the share of each item in the total consumption expenditure as per a CES
B) the geographic distribution of markets
C) the time series of inflation
D) the ratio of wholesale to retail prices
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
P: 6
EXP: The **weighting diagram** gives each item's **share in total consumption expenditure** in the CES. Weights usually come from **NSS rounds**.
SC: The weighting diagram is item shares of the budget.
