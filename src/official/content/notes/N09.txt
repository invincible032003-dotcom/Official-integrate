@topic Wages: OWS & Wage Rate Index
Q: The first Occupational Wage Survey (OWS) was conducted in:
A) 1958-59
B) 1969
C) 1963-65
D) 2016
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: The **first OWS was in 1958-59**. It collects **occupation-wise employment, wage rates and DA** to build **WRI base weights**, and **payroll earnings** to study wage differentials in services, plantations, mining and manufacturing.
SC: OWS began in 1958-59 and builds the WRI weights.

Q: In the Occupational Wage Survey, the first-stage and second-stage sampling units are respectively:
A) registered factories and workers in different occupations
B) districts and factories
C) villages and households
D) industries and factories
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: OWS uses a two-stage design: **FSU = registered factories**, **SSU = workers by occupation**. Estimates must lie within **5% of true values** with a **95% confidence coefficient**.
SC: OWS goes factory, then worker; within 5% at 95% confidence.

Q: The QES under AQEES is described as a "demand side" survey because:

ANS: A

D: Medium

EXP: **Demand side** = how much labour **establishments employ or demand**, as in QES/AQEES. **Supply side** = how much labour force is **available**, as in PLFS. A noted limitation of QES is that it covers only the **formal** segment of 9 sectors and **leaves out over 90% of informal workers**, such as agricultural labour.
SC: QES measures demand from establishments; PLFS measures supply from households.

@topic PLFS: Activity Status Concepts
Q: Which of the following correctly defines the Worker Population Ratio (WPR) in PLFS?
A) Percentage of employed persons in the population
B) Percentage of persons in the labour force in the population
C) Percentage of unemployed persons among the labour force
D) Percentage of employed persons in the labour force
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: **WPR = employed / population × 100**. **LFPR = (employed + unemployed) / population × 100**. **UR = unemployed / labour force × 100**.
SC: WPR and LFPR use the population as denominator; UR uses the labour force.

Q: If LFPR = 50% and WPR = 47%, the unemployment rate (UR) is approximately:
A) 6.0%
B) 3.0%
C) 94.0%
D) 5.0%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: **UR = (LFPR − WPR) / LFPR × 100 = (50 − 47) / 50 × 100 = 6%**. Both rates share the population denominator, so they cancel out.
SC: UR = 1 − WPR/LFPR = 1 − 0.94 = 6%.

Q: The usual principal activity status (ps) of a person is determined on the basis of:
A) the major time criterion over the 365 days preceding the date of survey
B) the priority criterion over the last 7 days
C) activity on each half-day of the reference week
D) activity pursued for 30 days or more in the last 365 days
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **ps = major time criterion over 365 days**. **ss = economic activity for ≥ 30 days** within the same 365 days, in addition to ps. **CWS = priority-cum-major-time over 7 days**. **CDS = each half-day** of the week.
SC: ps uses major time over a year; ss needs 30 or more days.

Q: Under the current weekly status (CWS), the priority criterion means:
A) 'working' gets priority over 'seeking/available for work', which gets priority over 'neither working nor available'
B) 'not in labour force' gets priority over 'working'
C) the status with the most days in the week prevails regardless of work
D) 'seeking work' gets priority over 'working'
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: CWS uses **priority-cum-major time**: **working > unemployed > not in labour force**. Even **1 hour of work on any 1 day** in the 7 days makes a person **employed**.
SC: Working beats seeking, and seeking beats being out of the labour force.

Q: Under current daily status (CDS), a person who worked for 2 hours on a day and was seeking/available for work for the rest of the day is classified for that day as:
A) employed for half day and unemployed for the other half day
B) employed for the full day
C) unemployed for the full day
D) not in labour force for the full day
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: CDS rules: work **≥ 4 hours → employed for the full day**. Work **1-4 hours → employed for half a day**, and the other half is unemployed or not in labour force depending on availability. Here 2 hours falls in the 1-4 hour band.
SC: CDS: 4 or more hours is a full day; 1 to 4 hours is half a day.

Q: Under current daily status, a person who did no work even for 1 hour on a day but was seeking/available for work for 4 hours or more is considered:
A) unemployed for the entire day
B) unemployed for half day and not in labour force for half day
C) employed for half day
D) not in labour force for the entire day
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: With no work, availability for **≥ 4 hours means unemployed for the full day**. Availability for **1-4 hours** means **unemployed for half a day** and **not in labour force** for the other half.
SC: Without work, 4 or more hours of seeking is a full unemployed day.

Q: In CDS, the intensity assigned to an activity done for a "full day" and a "half day" is respectively:
A) 1.0 and 0.5
B) 2 and 1
C) 8 and 4
D) 1.0 and 0.25
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 4
EXP: **Full day = 1.0**, **half day = 0.5**. Summing intensities over 7 days gives **person-days**, from which **CDS rates** are built.
SC: CDS intensities are 1 and ½.

Q: Which of the following is the correct composition of "employed (working)" in PLFS?
A) Self-employed + regular wage/salaried employees + casual labour
B) Self-employed + unemployed
C) Regular wage employees only
D) Casual labour + students
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 4
EXP: **Employed = self-employed + regular wage/salaried + casual labour**. **Labour force = employed + unemployed**. **Not in labour force = neither working nor available**.
SC: Employed = SE + RWS + CL.

Q: A person who neither worked nor was available for work at any time during the reference week is, under CWS, considered:
A) not in labour force
B) unemployed
C) underemployed
D) employed
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **Neither working nor available** in the week means **not in labour force**, i.e. engaged in **non-economic activities** such as attending education, domestic duties or rentier income.
SC: No work and no availability means outside the labour force.

@topic PLFS: Activity Status Concepts
Q: In a population of 1,000 persons, 400 are employed and 50 are unemployed (seeking/available for work). The LFPR and UR are respectively:
A) 45% and about 11.1%
B) 40% and 5%
C) 45% and 5%
D) 40% and 12.5%
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: **LF = 400 + 50 = 450**, so **LFPR = 450/1000 = 45%**. **UR = 50/450 = 11.1%**, and **WPR = 400/1000 = 40%**. The 5% in the distractors is 50/1000, which uses the wrong denominator.
SC: UR divides by the labour force, not the population.

Q: Under CWS, a person is considered "unemployed" if during the reference week he/she:
A) did not work even for one hour on any day but made efforts to get work or was available for work
B) worked for less than 4 hours on any day
C) was not available for work but wanted a job
D) worked only as an unpaid helper
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **Unemployed (CWS)** = **no economic activity** in the week, but **sought or was available** for work, including those not actively seeking because they believed no work was available. **Unpaid helpers in household enterprises are employed** (self-employed).
SC: No work plus availability means unemployed; helpers count as employed.
