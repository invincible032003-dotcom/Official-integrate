@topic History & Base Year
Q: The first set of national income estimates for the entire Indian Union was compiled by the National Income Committee set up in 1949 under the chairmanship of:
A) Prof. P.C. Mahalanobis
B) Dr. V.K.R.V. Rao
C) Prof. D.R. Gadgil
D) Dr. C. Rangarajan
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: The **National Income Committee (1949)**, a high-powered expert committee chaired by **Prof. P.C. Mahalanobis** with members **D.R. Gadgil and V.K.R.V. Rao**, compiled the **first official national income estimates** for India. **V.K.R.V. Rao** had made the pre-Independence estimates (1931-32).
SC: The 1949 National Income Committee was chaired by Mahalanobis.

Q: Why were the base years of national accounts earlier revised decennially to a year ending with "1"?
A) Workforce estimates, especially for the unorganised sector, were obtained from the decennial Population Census held in years ending with 1
B) Five-Year Plans began in years ending with 1
C) The SNA was revised every ten years
D) The RBI required it for monetary policy
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: Unorganised-sector **workforce** estimates came from the **Population Census (years ending with 1)**. That practice continued up to the **1980-81** series.
SC: Census years ending with 1 set the old base years.

Q: Since which series did the CSO start revising the national accounts base year every five years, in line with the NSSO quinquennial Employment-Unemployment Surveys?
A) 1993-94 series
B) 1980-81 series
C) 2004-05 series
D) 1999-2000 series
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: From the **1993-94 series** the CSO used **NSSO quinquennial EUS** workforce data, so base years became **quinquennial**. The **NSC** also recommended **rebasing economic indices at least every 5 years**.
SC: From 1993-94 the base was revised every 5 years with the EUS.

Q: The 2009-10 quinquennial EUS was not used to revise the national accounts base year because:
A) 2009-10 was not considered a "normal" year, as it followed the global slowdown of 2008, so a fresh EUS was conducted in 2011-12

C) the sample size was too small
D) the NSC rejected the EUS methodology
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: **2009-10 was not a "normal" year** because it followed the **2008 global slowdown**. A fresh **EUS was held in 2011-12** and used for the **base 2011-12** series.
SC: 2009-10 was abnormal, so the base became 2011-12.

Q: The new series of national accounts with base year 2011-12 was released on:
A) 30 January 2015
B) 31 May 2014
C) 1 April 2015
D) 28 February 2015
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: **Base 2011-12 was released on 30 January 2015**, replacing the **2004-05** base. It follows the UN **SNA 2008**.
SC: The 2011-12 series was released on 30 January 2015 and follows SNA 2008.
TIP: A newer GDP series with base year 2022-23 has been announced by MoSPI. Know both the 2011-12 series facts and the latest base.

Q: The System of National Accounts 2008, on which India's 2011-12 series is based, was jointly issued by:
A) UN, World Bank, IMF, OECD and European Commission (EU)

ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: **SNA 2008** was issued by five organisations: **UN, World Bank, IMF, OECD and the EU (Eurostat / European Commission)**. It is the internationally agreed standard for compiling measures of economic activity.
SC: SNA 2008 has five issuing bodies: UN, WB, IMF, OECD, EU.

@topic Aggregates & Formulae
Q: GVA at basic prices by the income approach equals:
A) CE + OS/MI + CFC + production taxes less production subsidies
B) CE + OS/MI + product taxes less product subsidies
C) Output − intermediate consumption + product taxes
D) PFCE + GFCE + GCF + net exports
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **Income approach: GVA(bp) = Compensation of Employees + Operating Surplus / Mixed Income + CFC + (production taxes − production subsidies)**. Adding **product taxes less product subsidies** then gives **GDP**.
SC: The income approach is CE + OS/MI + CFC + net production taxes.

Q: Net National Disposable Income (NNDI) equals:
A) NNI + other current transfers from the rest of the world (net)
B) GNI − CFC
C) GDP + net primary income from abroad
D) GNDI + CFC
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: The chain is **GNI = GDP + net primary income from RoW**; **NNI = GNI − CFC**; **NNDI = NNI + net other current transfers from RoW**; **GNDI = NNDI + CFC**.
SC: Disposable income = national income + net current transfers.

Q: "Primary incomes" in national accounts comprise:
A) compensation of employees plus property and entrepreneurial income
B) current transfers such as remittances and grants
C) taxes on products
D) capital transfers
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **Primary incomes = CE + property and entrepreneurial income**. **Other current transfers** (remittances, grants) are **not** primary incomes; they take us from **NNI to NNDI**.
SC: Primary income is earned; transfers are received.

Q: Gross Capital Formation from the expenditure side is:
A) GFCF + change in stocks (CIS) + valuables
B) Gross savings + net capital inflow from RoW
C) GFCF − CFC
D) PFCE + GFCE
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: **Expenditure side: GCF = GFCF + CIS + Valuables**. **Financing side: GCF = Gross Savings + Net capital inflow from RoW**.
SC: GCF uses: fixed assets + stocks + valuables.

Q: Gross Disposable Income of the Government equals:
A) GFCE + gross saving of the general government
B) GNDI − gross savings of corporations
C) tax revenue − subsidies
D) GFCE − transfers
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: **GDI(Govt) = GFCE + gross saving of general government**. **GDI(Households) = GNDI − GDI(Govt) − gross savings of all corporations**.
SC: Government disposable income is consumption plus saving.

Q: Gross Disposable Income of Households is derived as:
A) GNDI − GDI of Government − gross savings of all corporations
B) GNI − CFC
C) PFCE + household savings − taxes
D) GDP − GFCE
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: The household GDI is the **residual**: **GNDI less the government's GDI less corporate gross savings**.
SC: Households get the residual after government and corporations.

Q: Which of the following are "product taxes" as per the national accounts classification?
1. Excise duties
2. Land revenue
3. Import duties
4. Stamps and registration fees
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 3 only
B) 2 and 4 only
C) 1, 2 and 3 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: A
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **Product taxes** are levied **per unit of product**: excise, sales tax, service tax (now GST) and **import/export duties**. **Production taxes** are **independent of the volume** produced: **land revenue, stamps & registration fees**, profession tax.
SC: A tax per unit is a product tax; a tax regardless of volume is a production tax.

Q: Which of the following is an example of a production subsidy (as distinct from a product subsidy)?
A) Subsidies to Railways and to village and small industries
B) Food subsidy
C) Fertiliser subsidy
D) Petroleum subsidy
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **Production subsidies** are **not per unit**, e.g. subsidies to **Railways** and to **village and small industries**. **Product subsidies** are **per unit**: **food, fertiliser, petroleum**. Production taxes and subsidies are **included in basic prices**.
SC: Railway and VSI subsidies are production subsidies; food, fertiliser and fuel subsidies are product subsidies.

Q: Of the two approaches used to estimate Gross Capital Formation, which is treated as giving the "firmer" estimates?
A) Flow of funds approach (gross saving plus net capital flow from RoW)
B) Commodity flow approach by type of assets
C) Income approach
D) Production approach
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 3
EXP: The **flow of funds** estimate (saving + net RoW inflow) is treated as **firmer**. The **commodity flow** approach gives GCF by type of asset. GCF by **industry of use** and **institutional sector** **excludes valuables**, so it is **lower** than the commodity flow estimate.
SC: Flow of funds is the firmer estimate; by-industry GCF excludes valuables.

@topic Concepts: Domestic vs National, Basic Prices
Q: The term "Domestic" in GDP implies that the boundary is:
A) geographical: all goods and services produced within the country's borders, regardless of by whom
B) based on citizenship, regardless of where production takes place
C) based on residence of the head of household
D) limited to the organised sector
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **Domestic = territorial boundary**. **National = production by nationals / residents** wherever it takes place, which is why **GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad**. **Net = Gross − depreciation (CFC)**.
SC: Domestic means where it is produced; national means who produces it.

Q: The difference between GDP and GVA at basic prices is:
A) product taxes less product subsidies
B) production taxes less production subsidies
C) consumption of fixed capital
D) net factor income from abroad
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 6
EXP: **GDP = GVA(bp) + product taxes − product subsidies**. Production taxes and subsidies are already **inside basic prices**. **CFC** separates gross from net, and **NFIA** separates domestic from national.
SC: GDP minus GVA equals net product taxes.

Q: Basic prices, as used in GVA, are:
A) inclusive of production taxes less production subsidies, but exclusive of product taxes and subsidies
B) inclusive of product taxes less product subsidies
C) factor cost prices excluding all taxes and subsidies
D) purchasers' prices including trade and transport margins
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: **Basic price = factor cost + production taxes − production subsidies**. Adding product taxes less subsidies gives **market prices**; adding trade and transport margins gives **purchasers' prices**. GVA at basic prices = **output at basic prices − IC at purchasers' prices**.
SC: Factor cost + net production taxes = basic price; + net product taxes = market price.

Q: In compiling GVA at basic prices, output and intermediate consumption are valued respectively at:
A) basic prices and purchasers' prices
B) purchasers' prices and basic prices
C) factor cost and market prices
D) market prices and factor cost
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: **Output at basic prices, inputs at purchasers' prices**. These are the prices the producer actually **receives** and **pays**, which makes GVA particularly relevant for the producer.
SC: Outputs are valued at what is received (basic), inputs at what is paid (purchasers').

Q: Real GDP differs from nominal GDP in that real GDP is:
A) measured at constant (base-year) prices, while nominal GDP is at current-year prices
B) measured at current prices, while nominal GDP is at constant prices
C) net of depreciation
D) adjusted for population
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 5
EXP: **Real GDP = constant (base-year) prices**; comparing it over years gives **real growth**. **Nominal GDP = current prices**. Nominal ÷ real × 100 = the **GDP deflator**.
SC: Real means constant prices; nominal means current prices.

Q: The main reason for periodically changing the base year of national accounts is to:
A) account for structural changes taking place in the economy and depict a true picture
B) inflate the growth rate
C) comply with WTO requirements
D) align with the Finance Commission cycle
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
P: 8
EXP: Base revision captures **structural changes**: new products, sectors, data sources and prices. The aim is to **depict the true picture** of the economy.
SC: A new base captures a changed economy.

@topic Release Calendar & Methods
Q: As per the release calendar in the notes, the First Advance Estimates of annual GDP (for 2019-20) were released on:
A) 7 January 2020
B) 31 January 2020
C) 28 February 2020
D) 29 May 2020
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: For 2019-20: **FAE on 7 January 2020**, **SAE on 28 February 2020**, **Provisional Estimates on 29 May 2020**. The **First Revised Estimates for 2018-19** came on **31 January 2020**.
SC: FAE early January, FRE end-January, SAE end-February, PE end-May.

Q: As per the notes, the quarterly GDP estimates for Q3 (October-December) are released around:
A) end of February
B) end of May
C) end of August
D) end of November
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: Quarterly GDP comes about **2 months after** the quarter ends: **Q3 (Oct-Dec) at end-February**, **Q4 (Jan-Mar) at end-May**, **Q1 (Apr-Jun) at end-August**, **Q2 (Jul-Sep) at end-November**.
SC: Quarter end + 2 months.

Q: The approach used for compiling Quarterly and Advance Estimates of GDP is:
A) the benchmark-indicator method
B) the commodity flow method only
C) complete enumeration of enterprises
D) the double deflation method only
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: The **benchmark-indicator method** extrapolates the **previous year's** constant-price output or value added using **volume indicators** such as IIP, listed company results, crop production, Government expenditure, and service indicators (railways, aviation, telecom, deposits and credit).
SC: Advance and quarterly estimates move the benchmark forward with indicators.

Q: Which of the following indicators are used in the benchmark-indicator approach for Advance Estimates of GVA?
1. Index of Industrial Production
2. Financial performance of listed private companies from BSE/NSE
3. Crop production estimates from the Ministry of Agriculture
4. Railway passenger and freight earnings
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
A) 1 and 2 only
B) 1, 2 and 3 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: All four indicators are listed: **IIP**, **listed company financials**, **crop estimates**, **Central and State Government expenditure**, and **service indicators** (sales tax, deposits and credit, railway earnings, civil aviation, telephone connections).
SC: The indicators are IIP, company results, crops, Government expenditure and services data.

Q: When provisional estimates are revised and released as First Revised Estimates in January, GDP estimates are compiled on the basis of:
A) analysis of budgets of Central and State Governments, financial statements of corporations and accounts of local bodies
B) the same indicators as the advance estimates
C) the Population Census
D) only the IIP and CPI
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 7
EXP: Revised estimates replace **indicators** with **detailed data**: **budget analysis** of Governments, **financial statements** of public and private corporations, **accounts of local bodies**, and so on.
SC: Revisions swap proxies for actual accounts.

Q: In compiling expenditure-side GDP, the estimates of PFCE and GFCF are generally derived by:
A) the commodity flow approach
B) analysis of budget documents
C) household surveys only
D) the income approach
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 7
EXP: **PFCE and GFCF use the commodity flow approach**. **GFCE** comes from **budget documents** of Central, State and local governments. **Exports and imports** come from **Customs records and BoP** data.
SC: PFCE and GFCF use commodity flow; GFCE uses budgets.

Q: Government Final Consumption Expenditure (GFCE) consists of:
A) compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital and intermediate consumption less sales
B) transfer payments to households
C) capital expenditure on roads
D) interest payments on public debt
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **GFCE = CE + CFC + net intermediate consumption** (purchases, including repair and maintenance, less sales). It is classified by **COFOG**: public administration, defence, education, health and others. **Transfers and interest** are not consumption.
SC: GFCE is salaries plus depreciation plus net purchases; transfers are excluded.

Q: The estimates of change in stocks in national accounts are prepared:
A) separately for institutional sectors, using annual data on corporations and benchmark surveys of households
B) only for the Government sector
C) from IIP data alone
D) by the RBI from bank credit data only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 7
EXP: **CIS** is estimated **by institutional sector**: public and private, financial and non-financial corporations, general government and households. It uses **annual corporate data** and **benchmark household surveys**.
SC: Stocks are estimated sector by sector.

@topic Supply-Use Tables & IOTT
Q: In the Supply-Use Tables (SUT) published by NAD, the supply table and the use table are valued respectively at:
A) basic prices and purchasers' prices
B) purchasers' prices and basic prices
C) factor cost and market prices
D) market prices and factor cost
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: **Supply table at basic prices; use table at purchasers' prices**. The SUT is the **ideal framework for balancing supply and demand** and the **best framework for compiling GDP at current prices**.
SC: Supply is at basic prices, use at purchasers' prices.

Q: The Supply-Use Tables are considered the best framework for:
A) compiling GDP at current prices by balancing supply and demand of products
B) compiling the Consumer Price Index
C) estimating population
D) measuring poverty
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 4
EXP: The SUT links **industry output** to **intermediate and final uses** (consumption, GCF, exports) and shows the **cost structure and incomes generated**. After balancing, it yields coherent **current-price GDP**.
SC: SUT balances supply and use to fix GDP.

Q: The Input-Output Transactions Tables (IOTT) in India were first released for the year:
A) 1968-69
B) 1951-52
C) 1978-79
D) 1993-94
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The **first IOTT was for 1968-69**, and the latest in the notes is **2007-08**. IOTT is published by **NAD** roughly **every 5 years**, as per the core statistics list.
SC: IOTT first 1968-69; latest in the notes 2007-08.

Q: In the IOTT, the entries are presented:
A) at factor cost, as a commodity × industry matrix, after removing trade and transport margins and net indirect taxes
B) at purchasers' prices only
C) at basic prices as an industry × industry matrix
D) in physical units
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 4
EXP: IOTT entries are at **factor cost**, as a **commodity × industry** matrix: **columns = industries**, **rows = commodity groups**. They are first prepared at purchasers' prices, then **trade and transport margins and NIT are removed**.
SC: IOTT is commodity × industry at factor cost.

Q: GDP measures the gross value added from which of the following institutional sectors?
A) General Government, public and private financial and non-financial corporations, and households

C) Only the household sector
D) Government and households only
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 5
EXP: GDP covers **all institutional sectors**: **General Government**, **Public and Private Financial and Non-Financial Corporations**, and **Households** (including NPISHs).
SC: GDP covers all sectors: government, corporations and households.

Q: Data on exports and imports for national accounts are available from:
A) Customs records and the Balance of Payments statistics
B) the Annual Survey of Industries
C) the NSS Consumer Expenditure Survey
D) the Economic Census
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 7
EXP: **Exports and imports** come from **Customs records (DGCI&S)** and **RBI BoP statistics**.

Q: Which of the following is the correct sequence in the annual GDP release cycle for a financial year t?
A) First Advance Estimate → Second Advance Estimate → Provisional Estimate → First Revised Estimate
B) Provisional Estimate → First Advance Estimate → First Revised Estimate → Second Advance Estimate
C) First Revised Estimate → First Advance Estimate → Provisional Estimate
D) Second Advance Estimate → First Advance Estimate → Provisional Estimate
ANS: A

D: Medium
P: 3

@topic Aggregates & Formulae
Q: GVA at basic prices = ₹1,000 crore; product taxes = ₹120 crore; product subsidies = ₹20 crore; production taxes = ₹30 crore. GDP at market prices is:
A) ₹1,100 crore
B) ₹1,130 crore
C) ₹1,150 crore
D) ₹1,080 crore
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **GDP = GVA(bp) + product taxes − product subsidies = 1000 + 120 − 20 = 1100**. **Production taxes are already included** in basic prices, so adding them again (₹1,130 crore) is the trap.
SC: Add only net product taxes to GVA; production taxes are already inside.

Q: GDP = ₹2,000 crore; net primary income from abroad = −₹50 crore; CFC = ₹200 crore. NNI is:
A) ₹1,750 crore
B) ₹1,850 crore
C) ₹1,950 crore
D) ₹1,800 crore
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **GNI = 2000 + (−50) = 1950**; **NNI = GNI − CFC = 1950 − 200 = 1750**.
SC: NNI = GDP + NFIA − CFC.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a component of GDP by the expenditure approach?
A) Compensation of employees
B) Private final consumption expenditure
C) Gross fixed capital formation
D) Net exports
ANS: A
T: Odd-one-out
D: Easy
P: 5
EXP: **Expenditure side = PFCE + GFCE + GFCF + CIS + valuables + (X − M)**. **Compensation of employees** is an **income-side** component.
SC: CE belongs to the income side, not the expenditure side.

Q: In the income approach, "operating surplus" includes:
A) undistributed profit, rent, dividend and interest
B) wages and salaries
C) product taxes
D) consumption of fixed capital
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: **Operating surplus** covers **profits (distributed and undistributed), rent, dividend and interest**. **Wages and salaries** are **compensation of employees**. **Mixed income** is the self-employed's surplus, which blends labour and capital returns.
SC: Operating surplus is the return to capital (profit, rent, interest).

Q: Gross Capital Formation by industry of use is lower than GCF from the commodity flow approach because the former:
A) does not include valuables
B) includes depreciation
C) excludes Government investment
D) is measured at constant prices only
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 3
EXP: GCF by **industry of use** and by **institutional sector** **excludes valuables**, so it is **lower** than the **commodity-flow** (asset-type) estimate, which includes valuables.
SC: The by-industry GCF leaves out valuables.
