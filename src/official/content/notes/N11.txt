@topic SRS: Objectives & Components
Q: The main objective of the Sample Registration System (SRS) is to provide reliable estimates of birth rate, death rate and IMR at:
A) the natural division level for major States and the State level for smaller States
B) the district level for all States
C) the village level
D) the national level only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: The **SRS**, run annually by **ORGI** in all States and UTs, gives **BR, DR and IMR** at the **natural division level for major States** and the **State level for smaller States**. **District-level** vital rates can only come from the **CRS**.
SC: SRS gives natural-division or State rates; districts need CRS.

Q: In the SRS, continuous enumeration of births and deaths in the sample units is carried out by:
A) resident part-time enumerators, generally anganwadi workers and teachers
B) SRS supervisors every six months
C) FOD staff of NSO
D) ASHA workers under NHM only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: **Continuous (longitudinal) enumeration** is done by **resident part-time enumerators**, generally **anganwadi workers or teachers**. **SRS supervisors** conduct the independent **half-yearly retrospective survey**. The two records are **matched**.
SC: Enumerators record continuously; supervisors survey every 6 months.

Q: Arrange the following SRS components in the correct order:
1. Matching of events from continuous enumeration and half-yearly survey
2. Base-line survey of sample units
3. Field verification of unmatched and partially matched events
4. Filling of verbal autopsy forms for finalised deaths
A) 2-1-3-4
B) 1-2-3-4
C) 2-3-1-4
D) 2-1-4-3
ANS: A
T: Chronology
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: The sequence is **baseline survey → continuous enumeration → half-yearly survey → matching → field verification of unmatched events → verbal autopsy** for finalised deaths.
SC: Base, count, survey, match, verify, autopsy.

Q: In SRS, the 95% confidence interval for the Birth Rate is estimated as:
A) BR ± 2 SE(BR)
B) BR ± 1 SE(BR)
C) BR ± 3 SE(BR)
D) BR ± 1.645 SE(BR)
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: SRS reports **95% CIs as estimate ± 2 SE** for BR, DR and IMR. Two SE approximates 1.96 SE.
SC: A 95% CI is about ± 2 SE.

Q: The sample design of SRS is:
A) uni-stage stratified simple random sampling without replacement, except in stratum II (larger villages) of rural areas where two-stage stratification is applied
B) two-stage PPS sampling in all areas
C) systematic sampling of households
D) cluster sampling with replacement
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: SRS uses **uni-stage stratified SRSWOR**. The **exception** is **rural stratum II (large villages)**, where **two-stage stratification** is applied. The **urban sampling unit** is a **Census Enumeration Block**.
SC: SRS is single-stage SRSWOR, except that large villages get two stages.

Q: In the SRS urban sample design, towns/cities are divided into how many strata based on size classes?
A) Four: towns below 1 lakh, 1-5 lakh, 5 lakh and above, and the four metro cities
B) Six, as in the frame before 2001
C) Three, by female literacy
D) Two: million-plus and others
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: The urban strata are **I: < 1 lakh**; **II: 1-5 lakh**; **III: ≥ 5 lakh**; **IV: the four metros** (Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata) as separate strata. There were **six strata before 2001**. Within each size stratum, CEBs are ordered by **female literacy (Census 2011)** into **3 equal sub-strata**.
SC: 4 urban size strata, then 3 sub-strata by female literacy.

Q: Within each urban size stratum in SRS, Census Enumeration Blocks were ordered by which variable to form three equal-size sub-strata?
A) Female literacy rate (Census 2011)
B) Sex ratio
C) Population density
D) Proportion of SC/ST population
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: CEBs are **ordered by female literacy rate** and split into **3 equal sub-strata**. Blocks are then drawn by **SRSWOR with equal probability** in each sub-stratum.
SC: The urban sub-strata use female literacy.

Q: As per the SRS Bulletin (reference year 2019) in the notes, the State with the highest birth rate and the State/UT with the lowest birth rate were respectively:
A) Bihar and Andaman & Nicobar Islands
B) Uttar Pradesh and Kerala
C) Madhya Pradesh and Goa
D) Bihar and Kerala
ANS: A
D: Medium

SC: SRS 2019: BR high Bihar, low A&N; IMR high MP.
TIP: Newer SRS bulletins have been released since. Rank patterns (Bihar and MP high; Kerala, Delhi and the small NE States low) tend to persist, but check the latest year.

@topic Demographic Rate Formulae
Q: The General Fertility Rate (GFR) is defined as the number of live births in a year per 1000:
A) mid-year female population in the age group 15-49 years
B) mid-year total population
C) mid-year married female population aged 15-49
D) women who gave birth during the year
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: **GFR = live births / mid-year females 15-49 × 1000**. **GMFR** uses **married** females 15-49. **CBR** uses the **total mid-year population**.
SC: GFR uses all women 15-49; GMFR uses married women only.

Q: The Age-Specific Marital Fertility Rate (ASMFR) uses as its denominator:
A) the mid-year married female population of the same age group
B) the mid-year female population of the same age group
C) the total population of the same age group
D) the number of live births in the age group
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **ASMFR = births to women of age group x / married women of age group x × 1000**. **ASFR** uses **all** women of that age group.
SC: "Marital" in the name means married women in the denominator.

Q: The Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) is defined as:
A) infant deaths during the year per 1000 live births during the year
B) infant deaths per 1000 mid-year population
C) deaths under 5 years per 1000 live births
D) infant deaths per 1000 women aged 15-49
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **IMR = deaths under 1 year / live births × 1000**. Its denominator is **live births**, not population. IMR = **NMR + PNMR**.
SC: IMR = infant deaths per 1000 live births.

Q: The neo-natal mortality rate relates to infant deaths of age:
A) less than 29 days
B) less than 7 days
C) 7 days to less than 29 days
D) 29 days to less than one year
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **NMR: < 29 days** = **Early NMR (< 7 days)** + **Late NMR (7 to < 29 days)**. **PNMR: 29 days to < 1 year**. All use **live births** as the denominator.
SC: Early < 7 days, late 7-28 days, post-neonatal 29 days to 1 year.

Q: Match List-I (Rate) with List-II (Age at death of infants in numerator):
| List-I | List-II |
|---|---|
| A. Early neo-natal mortality rate | 1. 29 days to less than one year |
| B. Late neo-natal mortality rate | 2. Less than 7 days |
| C. Post neo-natal mortality rate | 3. 7 days to less than 29 days |
A) A-1, B-3, C-2
B) A-2, B-3, C-1
C) A-2, B-1, C-3
D) A-3, B-2, C-1
ANS: B
T: Match
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **Early NN: < 7 days**; **Late NN: 7 to < 29 days**; **Post NN: 29 days to < 1 year**.
SC: Under 7 days, then under 29 days, then under 1 year.

Q: The Perinatal Mortality Rate (PMR) is defined as:
A) (still births + infant deaths of less than 7 days) per 1000 (live births + still births)
B) infant deaths of less than 29 days per 1000 live births
C) still births per 1000 live births
D) (still births + neo-natal deaths) per 1000 live births
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 4
EXP: **PMR = (still births + deaths < 7 days) / (live births + still births) × 1000**. The **Still Birth Rate** = still births / (live births + still births) × 1000. Both include still births in the **denominator**.
SC: PMR adds still births to both the numerator and the denominator.

Q: In a year there were 9,800 live births, 200 still births and 150 infant deaths of age under 7 days. The Perinatal Mortality Rate is:
A) 35 per 1000
B) 15.3 per 1000
C) 20 per 1000
D) 35.7 per 1000
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Hard
P: 4
EXP: **PMR = (200 + 150) / (9800 + 200) × 1000 = 350 / 10000 × 1000 = 35**. Dividing by live births only gives 35.7, which is the trap.
SC: Still births also go in the denominator.

Q: Crude Death Rate (CDR) is calculated as:
A) number of deaths during the year per 1000 mid-year population
B) number of deaths per 1000 live births
C) number of deaths per 1000 population aged 15-49
D) number of deaths per 1000 households
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **CDR = deaths / mid-year population × 1000**. **ASMR** uses deaths in an age group over the population of that age group.
SC: Crude rates use the total mid-year population.

@topic Population Census
Q: The 2011 Census of India was:
A) the 15th Census since 1872 and the 7th after Independence
B) the 14th Census since 1872 and the 6th after Independence
C) the 15th Census since 1881 and the 7th after Independence
D) the 16th Census since 1872 and the 8th after Independence
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: The **first census was 1872** (non-synchronous, limited coverage), the first **synchronous** one **1881**, and the first after Independence **1951**. **2011 = 15th overall, 7th after Independence**, so the next is the **16th**.
SC: 2011 was the 15th census and the 7th after Independence.
TIP: The next Census, postponed from 2021, has been notified as Census 2027, with houselisting in 2026.

Q: As per the notes, up to 1931 the Indian Census used the "de facto" canvasser method, and since 1941 it has used:
A) the de jure canvasser method
B) the householder method
C) the de facto householder method
D) the register-based method
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: Per the notes: **de facto up to 1931, de jure since 1941**. **De facto** counts a person where found on the reference date; **de jure** counts at the place of **normal residence**. India uses the **canvasser method** (the enumerator fills the schedule) because **low literacy** limits the householder method.
SC: De facto until 1931, de jure from 1941, always by canvasser.

Q: In the "householder method" of census information gathering:
A) the enumerator gives a schedule to every household and the household fills it in
B) the enumerator records the answers himself
C) data are drawn from administrative registers
D) data are collected by telephone
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **Householder method** = **self-enumeration** by the household. **Canvasser method** = the **enumerator records** the answers. India uses the canvasser method.
SC: In the householder method the family writes; in the canvasser method the enumerator writes.

A) the Office of the Registrar General of India (ex-officio Census Commissioner) under MHA
B) the Central Statistical Organisation

EXP: In **1949** a **permanent ORGI** (RGI = ex-officio **Census Commissioner**) was set up under the **MHA**. **R.A. Gopalaswami** was the first RGI of independent India and conducted the **1951 Census**.
SC: The RGI has been permanent since 1949; Gopalaswami ran the 1951 census.

Q: The first Registrar General of India after Independence, who conducted the 1951 Census, was:

D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: **R.A. Gopalaswami** was RGI for the **1951 Census**. **C. Chandramouli** was RGI for **Census 2011**. **Asok Mitra** was Census Commissioner for **1961**.

Q: Census operations in India are carried out in two phases. The Population Enumeration phase follows the Houselisting and Housing Census with a gap of about:
A) six to eight months
B) two years
C) one month

ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **Phase 1: Houselisting & Housing Census**, which lists buildings as the **frame** and gives **housing stock and amenities**. **Phase 2: Population Enumeration**, about **6-8 months later**, counts **every person** with their characteristics.

Q: The National Population Register (NPR) is prepared under provisions of:

Q: Which of the following is NOT among the primary functions of the Office of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, India?

@topic Civil Registration & RBD Act
Q: Under the RBD Act, 1969, births, still births and deaths are registered:
A) at the place of occurrence of the event, with a normal reporting period of 21 days
B) at the place of normal residence within 30 days
C) at the district headquarters within 7 days
D) online only, within one year
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: Registration is **mandatory**, at the **place of occurrence**, within **21 days**. Later registration falls under **delayed registration (Section 13)**. The **Registrar** of the local area is appointed by the **State/UT**, and a **Chief Registrar** in each State is the executive authority.
SC: Register where it happened, within 21 days; later under Section 13.

Q: Delayed registration of births and deaths beyond the normal reporting period is provided for under which section of the RBD Act, 1969?
A) Section 13
B) Section 7
C) Section 23
D) Section 3
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 3
EXP: **Section 13** of the RBD Act covers **delayed registration**. The normal reporting period is **21 days**.
SC: Delayed registration is under Section 13.

Q: The amendment to the RBD Act, 1969 (enacted as the RBD (Amendment) Act, 2023) provides that:
A) the RGI shall maintain a national database of registered births and deaths, and birth certificates shall serve to prove date and place of birth for purposes like admissions, driving licence, voter list and government jobs
B) registration of births will become voluntary
C) States will no longer register births and deaths
D) death registration is transferred to the Election Commission
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The amendment creates **national and State databases** (the RGI and the Chief Registrars). It makes the **birth certificate** the document proving **date and place of birth** for **admissions, DL, voter list, marriage registration, Government/PSU jobs and passports**. The database can update the **NPR, electoral rolls, Aadhaar, ration cards, passports and DL**, and hospitals must give **cause-of-death** certificates. It came into force on **1 October 2023**.
SC: The 2023 amendment gives a national database, and the birth certificate becomes the single proof of birth.

Q: The Civil Registration System (CRS) is defined as a unified process of recording vital events which is:
A) continuous, permanent, compulsory and universal
B) periodic, sample-based and voluntary
C) decennial and complete
D) half-yearly and retrospective
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 6
EXP: **CRS = continuous, permanent, compulsory, universal** recording of vital events under the law. In India it covers **births and deaths**. It is **complete** only if it covers **100%** of events.
SC: CRS is continuous, permanent, compulsory and universal.

Q: Which source is the only one that can provide vital rates at the district level on an annual basis?
A) Civil Registration System
B) Sample Registration System
C) Population Census
D) NFHS
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 6
EXP: The **Census** is decennial, so its rates centre on the **mid-decade**. The **SRS** gives **national and State** rates, but district samples would be **prohibitively large**. Only the **CRS** can give **district-level** (and all administrative level) rates when complete.
SC: CRS for districts, SRS for States, Census once a decade.

Q: Registration records under CRS are primarily useful as:
A) legal documents, and secondarily as a source of vital statistics
B) a source of vital statistics, and secondarily as legal documents
C) sample survey data
D) inputs to national accounts only
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 7
EXP: CRS records are **primarily legal documents** and **secondarily vital statistics**. The statistics are a **by-product** of an administrative process, so they are **relatively inexpensive**, but they depend on **completeness and promptness** of registration.
SC: Legal use first, statistics second, which keeps them cheap.

Q: Which of the following is cited in the notes as a main reason for low registration levels of births and deaths?
A) Lack of awareness among the public and lack of procedural knowledge among registration functionaries
B) High registration fees
C) Absence of any law on registration
D) Registration permitted only in urban areas
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 7
EXP: The main reasons are **public unawareness** of the value of certificates and **functionaries' lack of procedural knowledge**. States have responded with **publicity** such as posters, pamphlets, radio talks and newspaper advertisements.
SC: Low registration comes from low awareness and weak procedures.

Q: The publications of the Office of the Registrar General of India include:
A) Vital Statistics based on CRS (annual), SRS Bulletin, and Report on Medical Certification of Cause of Death (annual)

ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: ORGI publishes **Vital Statistics of India based on CRS (annual)**, the **SRS Bulletin** and the **MCCD Report (annual)**, along with census publications.
SC: ORGI publishes CRS, SRS and MCCD reports.
