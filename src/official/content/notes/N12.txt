@topic NFHS-5: Sample Design
Q: The NFHS-5 sample design is:
A) a stratified two-stage sample with Census 2011 as the sampling frame for PSUs
B) a single-stage simple random sample of households
C) a three-stage cluster sample based on Census 2001
D) a quota sample of women aged 15-49
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: NFHS-5 is a **stratified two-stage** sample. **Stage 1: PSUs**, meaning **villages (rural)** and **CEBs (urban)** from **Census 2011**. **Stage 2: 22 households** per cluster, selected by **systematic sampling**.
SC: NFHS-5 is two-stage: PSUs from Census 2011, then 22 households.

Q: In NFHS-5, PSUs with fewer than how many households were linked to the nearest PSU?
A) 40
B) 100
C) 22
D) 300
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: PSUs with **< 40 households** were **linked** to the nearest PSU. PSUs with **≥ 300 households** were **segmented** into parts of **100-150 households**, and **2 segments** were chosen by PPS.
SC: Below 40 households, link the PSU; 300 or more, segment it.

Q: In the rural stratum of NFHS-5, six approximately equal substrata were created by crossing:
A) three substrata based on the number of households with two substrata based on the percentage of SC/ST population
B) two substrata by literacy with three by sex ratio
C) three substrata by district size with two by religion
D) six substrata by female literacy
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 1
EXP: Rural: **3 (village household size) × 2 (SC/ST share) = 6 substrata**. Within each, PSUs were **sorted by the literacy of women aged 6+** and selected by **PPS systematic sampling**.
SC: Size × SC/ST = 3 × 2 = 6 substrata, then sorted by female literacy.

Q: In the urban areas of NFHS-5, CEBs were sorted according to which variable before PPS systematic selection?
A) Percentage of SC/ST population
B) Female literacy rate
C) Number of households
D) Sex ratio
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: **Urban CEBs** (information from ORGI) were **sorted by SC/ST percentage**, then selected by **PPS systematic** sampling. In **rural** areas, PSUs were sorted by **female literacy (6+)** within substrata.
SC: Urban sorts by SC/ST share; rural sorts by female literacy.

Q: Selected PSUs in NFHS-5 with at least 300 households were segmented into segments of approximately:
A) 100-150 households, of which two were selected
B) 22 households, of which one was selected
C) 300 households, of which three were selected
D) 50 households, of which four were selected
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: A PSU with **≥ 300 households** is split into **100-150 household segments**, and **2 segments** are chosen with PPS. So an **NFHS-5 cluster** is **either a PSU or a segment**.
SC: 300 or more households: segments of 100-150, pick 2.

Q: In NFHS-5, how many households were randomly selected in every selected rural and urban cluster in the second stage?
A) 22
B) 16
C) 12
D) 30
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: **22 households** per cluster were selected by **systematic sampling**, after a complete **household mapping and listing** operation.
SC: NFHS takes 22 households per cluster.

@topic NFHS: Concepts & Interpretation
Q: In NFHS, the "de facto population" refers to:
A) all persons who stayed in the selected households the night before the interview, whether usual residents or visitors
B) all usual residents, whether or not they stayed the previous night
C) only the head of household and spouse
D) all persons registered in the NPR
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **De facto** = everyone who **stayed the night before** the interview. **De jure** = all **usual residents**, whether present or not. NFHS tables are based on the **de facto population** unless specified otherwise.
SC: De facto means slept there last night; NFHS tables use it.

Q: Unless specified otherwise, NFHS tables are based on:
A) the de facto population
B) the de jure population
C) the Census 2011 population
D) the voter list population
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: NFHS **tables use the de facto population** by default.
SC: The NFHS default is de facto.

Q: The NFHS sex ratio rose from 991 (NFHS-4) to 1020 (NFHS-5). The notes caution against a simple interpretation mainly because:
A) NFHS's household definition excludes many single male migrants living away, and includes visitors who stayed last night, who are largely women visiting parental homes
B) NFHS-5 covered only urban areas
C) the NFHS counts only children
D) the NFHS-5 sample size was smaller than NFHS-4
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: NFHS's **modified** concept leaves out many **single male migrants** who live away from their household. Counting **last-night visitors**, largely **women at parental homes**, adds to the female count. Together these push the sex ratio **up** without a demographic change of that size.
SC: Absent migrant men plus visiting women inflate the NFHS sex ratio.

Q: As per NFHS, a household is defined as a person or group of related or unrelated persons who:
A) live together in the same dwelling unit, acknowledge one adult as head, share the same housekeeping arrangements and are considered a single unit
B) are related by blood only
C) share a common bank account
D) are registered at the same address in Aadhaar
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: An NFHS household means **living together**, **acknowledging one adult head (male or female)**, **sharing housekeeping arrangements** and being **considered a single unit**. Members may be **related or unrelated**.
SC: A household shares one dwelling, one head and one kitchen arrangement.

@topic DGCI&S & Foreign Trade Data
Q: The Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics (DGCI&S) is located at:
A) Kolkata, under the Ministry of Commerce
B) New Delhi, under the Ministry of Finance
C) Mumbai, under the RBI
D) Nagpur, under MoSPI
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: **DGCI&S, Kolkata** (Ministry of Commerce) is the **pioneer** official body for **India's trade statistics** and commercial information.
SC: DGCI&S is in Kolkata, under the Ministry of Commerce.

Q: DGCI&S collects the basic data for foreign trade statistics from customs formations in the form of:
A) Daily Trade Returns (DTR)
B) Monthly Trade Summaries
C) Quarterly Customs Reports
D) Annual Export Declarations
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: Customs formations send **Daily Trade Returns (DTR)** to DGCI&S, which processes and compiles them following **UNSD's IMTS Rev.2 Compilers Manual**.
SC: Customs sends DTRs to DGCI&S.

Q: India's merchandise trade statistics compiled by DGCI&S follow the recommendations of:
A) International Merchandise Trade Statistics, Revision 2 (IMTS Rev.2) of UNSD
B) SNA 2008
C) BPM6 of the IMF
D) ISIC Rev.4
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: DGCI&S follows **IMTS Rev.2 (UNSD)** for merchandise trade. **BPM6** (IMF) is the standard for the **RBI's balance of payments**.
SC: Merchandise trade follows IMTS; the BoP follows BPM6.

Q: The main source documents for India's foreign trade statistics are:
A) Shipping Bills for exports and Bills of Entry for imports, submitted to customs
B) GST returns only
C) Income tax returns of exporters
D) RBI foreign exchange transaction reports only
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: **Shipping Bills (exports)** and **Bills of Entry (imports)** are the declarations filed with **Customs** at ports.
SC: Shipping bills are for exports; bills of entry for imports.

Q: Index Numbers of Foreign Trade were first started by DGCI&S in 1944 with base period:
A) 1938-39
B) 1950-51
C) 1947-48
D) 1960-61
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 3
EXP: The foreign trade indices **started in 1944 on base 1938-39**. The **current base is 2012-13**, covering all **8-digit ITC-HS** items, and the indices are published **monthly, quarterly and yearly**.
SC: The trade index started in 1944 on 1938-39; the current base is 2012-13.

Q: In DGCI&S Index Numbers of Foreign Trade, the Unit Value Indices are computed using:
A) Laspeyres' price index formula with a fixed base
B) Paasche's formula with a chain base
C) Fisher's ideal formula
D) a simple arithmetic average of prices
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **Unit Value Index = Laspeyres (fixed base)**. The **Quantity Index = Value Index ÷ Unit Value Index**, which makes it an implicit Paasche-type quantity index.
SC: The UVI is Laspeyres; the quantity index is value divided by UVI.

Q: If the Value Index of exports is 180 and the Unit Value Index is 120, the Quantity Index of exports is:
A) 150
B) 60
C) 216
D) 300
ANS: A
T: Numerical
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **QI = VI / UVI × 100 = 180 / 120 × 100 = 150**.
SC: Quantity = Value ÷ Price.

@topic NFHS: Concepts & Interpretation
Q: The notes describe the NFHS concept of household membership used for the sex-ratio caveat as:
A) "modified de jure", neither purely de facto nor purely de jure
B) purely de facto
C) purely de jure
D) register-based
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: The notes call NFHS's approach **"modified de jure"**. Single male migrants are **usual members but not usual residents** of their households, so they are left out. Visitors who stayed last night are covered in the **de facto** tables. This is why the NFHS sex ratio of **1020** needs care.
SC: NFHS uses a modified de jure concept, which pushes the sex ratio up.

Q: As per NFHS, the "de jure population" comprises:
A) all usual residents of the selected households, whether or not they stayed there the night before the interview
B) all persons who stayed in the household the previous night
C) only visitors
D) only persons aged 15-49
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 3
EXP: **De jure = usual residents**, whether present or absent last night. **De facto = everyone present last night**, residents and visitors alike.
SC: De jure is about who lives there; de facto is about who slept there.
