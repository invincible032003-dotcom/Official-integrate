Q: Which of the following are criteria for selecting the base year of a price index?
1. It should preferably synchronise with the income and expenditure survey period.

3. It should be a normal year, free of abnormal price variations.
4. The base year of constituent centres should be uniform.

A) 1 and 3 only
B) 1, 2 and 3 only
C) 2, 3 and 4 only
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: All four criteria hold: the base year should be **close to the expenditure survey**, have **reliable prices**, be a **normal year**, and be **uniform across centres** for easy compilation of the all-India index.
SC: A base year should be near the survey, reliable, normal and uniform.

Q: Assertion (A): 2009-10 was not chosen as the base year for the national accounts series that followed 2004-05.
Reason (R): A base year should be a normal year without abnormalities in price levels and economic conditions.

B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A

D) A is false, but R is true
ANS: A
T: Assertion-Reason
D: Medium
P: 1
EXP: **2009-10** followed the **2008 global slowdown**, so it was not **normal**. A fresh EUS in **2011-12** became the base. This shows the **"normal year" criterion** directly, so R explains A.
SC: An abnormal year cannot be a base year, hence 2011-12.

@topic Population Projections
Q: The universally accepted method for making population projections, which accounts for fertility, mortality and migration, is:
A) the Cohort Component Method
B) the Mathematical (exponential) Method
C) the Ratio Method
D) the Logistic Curve Method
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: The **Cohort Component Method** projects each **age-sex cohort** forward using **fertility, mortality and migration**. It was applied to **21 States + 1 UT** in India's projections.
SC: Projections use the cohort component method: births, deaths and moves by cohort.

Q: In India's official population projections discussed in the notes, the Cohort Component Method was applied to how many States/UTs individually?
A) 21 States and 1 UT
B) 28 States and 8 UTs
C) 15 States only
D) All States and UTs
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **21 States + 1 UT (J&K)** were projected individually by cohort component. The **7 NE States (excluding Assam)** were projected **as a whole**, and **Ladakh** was derived as a **residual**. **Goa and the remaining UTs** used the **mathematical method**.
SC: 21 + 1 by cohort, the NE States as one block, Ladakh by residual, the rest mathematical.

Q: For population projections, which method was applied for Goa and the remaining UTs?
A) Mathematical method
B) Cohort component method
C) Residual method
D) Delphi method
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: **Goa and the remaining UTs** use the **mathematical method**, because their numbers are too small for stable cohort-component inputs.
SC: Goa and the small UTs are projected mathematically.

Q: The population of Ladakh (UT) in the projections was obtained as:
A) the residual of the projected population of J&K (State) and J&K (UT)
B) a separate cohort component projection
C) a mathematical projection from Census 2001
D) an SRS-based estimate
ANS: A
D: Hard
P: 2
EXP: **Ladakh = J&K (State) projection − J&K (UT) projection**, a **residual**. Both of those were projected by cohort component.
SC: Ladakh = old J&K minus new J&K.

Q: The data used for official population projections in the notes are:
A) Census 2011 and the Sample Registration System
B) NFHS-5 and PLFS
C) Economic Census and ASI
D) Aadhaar enrolment data
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 2
EXP: The projections use **Census 2011** as the **base population** and the **SRS** for **time series of fertility and mortality** to predict their future levels.
SC: Projections use the Census for the base and the SRS for the rates.

@topic Unorganised & Informal Sector
Q: In the absence of regular annual enterprise survey data, GVA estimates for the unorganised segments of manufacturing and services are compiled indirectly through:
A) the labour input method using the benchmark-indicator procedure
B) the commodity flow method
C) the expenditure approach
D) direct annual census of all enterprises
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 2
EXP: The **labour input method** gives unorganised GVA as **workforce × GVA per worker**. **GVA per worker** comes from benchmark enterprise surveys and is moved forward with **indicators**.
SC: Unorganised GVA = workers × GVA per worker.

Q: The unorganised manufacturing sector accounts for roughly what share of the manufacturing sector's contribution to GDP, as per the notes?
A) About one-third
B) About two-thirds
C) About one-tenth
D) About half
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: As per the notes, **unorganised manufacturing is about 1/3** of manufacturing GDP, and the **unorganised service sector is about 30%** of GDP.
SC: About a third of manufacturing is unorganised.

Q: In the service sector, which units are considered "organised" for national accounts purposes?
A) Units registered under the Companies Act or Societies Act, and educational institutions whose salaries are funded by the Government
B) All units with more than 5 workers
C) Only public sector undertakings
D) Units registered under the Factories Act
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: **No single Act** separates organised from unorganised services. Units registered under the **Companies or Societies Acts** count as **organised**. In **education**, **Government-funded-salary institutions** are organised. In **manufacturing**, the dividing line is the **Factories Act, 1948**.
SC: Services are organised by Companies/Societies registration; manufacturing by the Factories Act.

Q: Apart from enterprise surveys, information on the number of workers in unregistered manufacturing is also available from which sources?

2. Decennial Population Census
3. Periodic Economic Censuses

A) 1 only
B) 1 and 2 only
C) 2 and 3 only
D) 1, 2 and 3
ANS: D
T: Statement
D: Medium
P: 3
EXP: All three give worker counts: the **EUS, the Population Census and the Economic Census**, each covering **all workers**, registered or not. The **EC** also gives the **number of enterprises**.
SC: Worker counts come from the EUS, the Census and the EC.

Q: The informal sector is characterised as consisting of units engaged in production with the primary objective of generating employment and incomes to the persons concerned. This definition was given by:
A) the 15th International Conference of Labour Statisticians (ILO, 1993)
B) the 19th ICLS (2013)
C) SNA 2008 alone
D) the NCEUS (2007)
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 4
EXP: The **15th ICLS (1993)** gave this definition. Such units operate at a **low level of organisation**, with **little division between labour and capital**, on a **small scale**. Labour relations rest on **casual employment, kinship or personal ties**.
SC: The informal sector definition comes from the 15th ICLS, 1993.

Q: The National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector (NCEUS) was set up by the Government of India in:
A) 2004
B) 1997
C) 1993
D) 2008
ANS: A
D: Medium

EXP: **NCEUS (2004)**, chaired by **Arjun Sengupta**, reviewed the unorganised sector: its enterprises, size, spread and employment. The **Delhi Group** dates from **1997** and the **15th ICLS** from **1993**.
SC: NCEUS 2004; Delhi Group 1997; 15th ICLS 1993.

Q: The Expert Group on Informal Sector Statistics, set up in 1997 as a city group of the UN Statistical Commission, is commonly known as:

@topic IIP Use-Based Classification
Q: In the IIP use-based classification (base 2011-12), the category "Primary goods" replaced which earlier category because of definitional ambiguity?
A) Basic goods
B) Intermediate goods
C) Capital goods
D) Consumer non-durables
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: **Primary goods** replaced **"Basic goods"**, which had **definitional ambiguity** and overlapped with intermediate goods. Primary goods are those **directly obtained from natural sources**: ores and minerals, fuels, and electricity.
SC: Primary replaced Basic.

Q: Which of the following is classified under "Infrastructure/construction goods" in the IIP use-based classification?
A) Cement, paints, cables, bricks and tiles
B) Tractors and boilers
C) Cotton yarn and plywood
D) Pressure cookers and TV sets
ANS: A
D: Medium
P: 5
EXP: **Infrastructure/construction goods**: paints, **cement**, cables, **bricks and tiles**, rail materials. **Capital goods**: boilers, **tractors**, engines, machinery. **Intermediate goods**: **cotton yarn, plywood**, steel pipes, fasteners. **Consumer durables**: cookers, ACs, TVs, cars, two-wheelers, gold jewellery.
SC: Cement is infrastructure, tractors are capital, yarn is intermediate, TVs are durables.

Q: In the IIP use-based classification, "Consumer durables" are products directly used by consumers with durability of more than:
A) 2-3 years
B) 6 months
C) 10 years
D) 1 month
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 5
EXP: **Consumer durables** last **more than 2-3 years**, e.g. pressure cookers, ACs, **tyres**, mobile phones, TVs, cars, two-wheelers and **gold jewellery**. **Non-durables** cannot be preserved long: milk, rice, biscuits, sugar, tea, cigarettes.
SC: Durables last more than 2-3 years.

@topic Payroll, MISO & Base Year Criteria
Q: The payroll data on EPF, ESI and NPS subscribers provide a perspective on employment mainly in which segment?
A) The formal sector
B) The informal agricultural sector
C) Self-employed household enterprises
D) Unpaid domestic work
ANS: A
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: EPF, ESI and NPS cover **formal** employees, so the payroll report gives a **formal-sector** employment perspective. It is **not** total employment, which is the role of **PLFS**.
SC: Payroll data measure formal jobs only.

Q: Why should the base year of constituent centres of a CPI be uniform?
A) To facilitate easy and quick compilation of the all-India index
B) To reduce the sample size
C) To eliminate seasonality
D) To comply with WTO rules
ANS: A
T: Conceptual
D: Easy
P: 1
EXP: A **uniform base across centres** lets the **all-India index** be compiled easily, as a weighted average of centre indices, without re-linking.
SC: A common base lets centre indices be aggregated.
